# Dominique Ferraro

> [!danger] DM-only
> This note is now spoiler material. To the players, Dominique is [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s cool older cousin and the party's trusted aboveground "heart." She is secretly [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s apprentice/heir and the ritual's bloodline anchor — the most personal betrayal in Act 1. Seed every step of it as *good news;* the reread is what hurts.

**[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s older cousin (~18) — bookish but well-liked, the bridge between the nerds and the popular crowd — established early as the PCs' "woman in the van" aboveground, and revealed late as [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s chosen heir and the [[The Equinox Ritual|ritual]]'s [[Elias Mercer|Mercer]]-bloodline anchor.**

**Tonal direction: Mercer Falls' own Anakin Skywalker.** Not a mole and not a victim — a bright, loved, genuinely good person who is *walked* into it one defensible step at a time, in full view, with her agency intact the whole way.

## Who she is (the players' view)
~18, a Ferraro (Bash's maternal cousin). **Bookish and scholarly** — she shares [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s curiosity, which is why he idolizes her — but also genuinely *liked:* pretty, easy in any room, welcome among nerds and popular kids alike. She got Bash into computers; she's [[Tank]]'s crush. Early in Act 1 she becomes the party's **aboveground operator** — chasing leads, checking on people, holding the town side together while they delve.

## Current state — Episode 2 / Session 3
**She is in a very good spot, and that is the point.** As played ([[Episode 2 — The Hush — Play Log]]):
- **She confronted [[Tank]] on his stoop** about the smear landing on her cousin, came down off the boil on a good Charisma read, and **formally enlisted him** — *"I'm just trying to protect my baby cousin. I could really use your help."*
- **[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] gave her the entire download**: the dungeon, the torches, the radio, the dog creature, the janitor, both deaths — and then **conjured a torch in his bare hand in front of her.** She read the conjuration as **a very good magic trick** and the rest as a wild story she wasn't going to call a lie.
- **She had already been told about [[Bash Whitlock|The Window]].** ⚠ Load-bearing — see below.
- **Provisional buy-in:** *"You say this happened. Until we find out otherwise — let's see this thing through."*
- She's in and out of Bash's kitchen regularly, on good terms with his mother [[Beth Whitlock]].

> [!note] Resolved TBD — "how to make the players love Free-Dominique first"
> **Answered in play, emphatically.** She is the only person in Mercer Falls who took a kid's impossible story and said *let's see it through.* **Keep her effective.** A Dominique who is *materially useful* buys far more grief later than one who is merely warm.

## She is Filter-porous, and it's Bash's fault

**Belief is what makes [[The Reality Filter|the Filter]] fail.** She half-believes because Bash told her the whole story months ago, so when she sat with him through a hollowing in [[Episode 3 — Second Shift|Ep 3]], **it didn't slide off her.** His overshare protected her perception — a genuinely good thing that is going to cost.

Same mechanism as [[Russ]] and [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]; different cause. Russ has grief, Ren has a notebook, **Dominique has a cousin who told her the truth.**

## The pressure-point — an absence, not a wound
Dominique isn't broken, so Wesley's cure-the-broken-leg pitch ([[The Cast and the Permanent Cast]]) doesn't work on her. Her crack is **rootlessness:** the universal bridge belongs *everywhere and nowhere* — welcome in every circle, a member of none. [[The Community Center]] is the first place that feels like a **family with her near its center**, and [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] offers the one thing no one else can: **not healing, but partnership.** *(Rhymes against [[Gern]]: Gern is offered a* seat*, Dominique a* throne beside the king*.)*

## Generating the fractures
**The requirement:** grievances **major from her side and nearly invisible from theirs.** The players should be able to review every interaction afterward and find nothing they'd have called a betrayal.

**The unifying axis — welcome vs. membership.** Every fracture is a moment where the party's genuine warmth turns out to be welcome without membership, and Wesley's offer is membership.

1. **She's left at the threshold, every time.** They delve; she waits. Structurally unavoidable, therefore endlessly repeatable.
2. **She is handed errands and calls it trust.** Each assignment is a real vote of confidence *and* a reminder that she's the courier for a group of children having the actual experience. **Now mechanised — see below.**
3. **She's told the truth and cannot verify a word of it.** No ember, no dungeon access, no proof. She believed them on nothing but love.
4. **Her wins become their wins.** She solves something town-side; it enters the party's account as a thing *they* figured out.
5. **She is talked *about* more than talked *to*.** Especially by Bash.

> [!danger] The Window — armed in Session 3
> Bash's precognition arc has him seeing **"something terrible happening to Dominique."** The reading is *accurate* (her at the ritual, aligned with Wesley); his interpretation is wrong.
>
> **She now knows the Window exists.** So there is a scene available where **Dominique learns her little cousin has been watching her fate on an instrument** instead of asking her a question. Devastating, entirely earned, and **invisible to Bash as anything but love.** **Do not spend this early.**

**Keep it out of the players' hands.** No fracture should ever be *stateable* by a PC.

# The mechanics — how the party uses her

> [!danger] The design lock
> The lesson the party learns is *"Dominique gets us answers we can't get."* **That's true, it's useful, and it is the exact mechanism that loses her.** No second mechanism is needed. Make the useful option **cheap** and the including option **cost something**, then track honestly which one they pick.

## The Adult Access ladder

**The best result has her in the room without them.**

| Who asks | Effect | TP |
|---|---|---|
| **PCs alone** | **DC +5.** Some doors don't open at all | — |
| **PCs with Dominique fronting** | **Base DC**, and she can Help for advantage | **1** |
| **Dominique alone** | **Advantage, +2** — and some things become possible that otherwise aren't | **0** |

**What triggers the +5:** anything an adult wouldn't discuss with somebody's kid — grief, health, money, family trouble, drinking, why somebody lost a job, why somebody was "off." Plus anything after dark, and anything where the honest answer is embarrassing.

**Four teenagers asking a widow about her sleep is not a neutral act.** It makes people close up, visibly. **Let them fail one of these early** so the ladder teaches itself, and the discovery lands on its own: *she should just go by herself.* Which is **correct, free, and −1 every time.**

## Limits — so the free option stays a choice

- **One lead per day.** She has her own life and will say so pleasantly. Forces the party to choose which thread she gets, which makes it a real decision and keeps it trackable.
- **She won't go anywhere dangerous.**
- **She won't lie to an adult she actually knows.** She'll shade and omit; she will not lie to [[Beth Whitlock]]'s face.

## The Affinity tracker

**Secret. Start at 0.** She's in a good spot.

| Event | Δ |
|---|---|
| Sent on an errand (the free option) | **−1** |
| Left at the threshold with no explanation of where they're going | **−1** |
| Her find absorbed into *"we figured out"* with nobody naming her | **−1** *(fires automatically)* |
| Talked about rather than to — including any Window use | **−2** |
| Brought along (the 1-TP option) | **+1** |
| Told the plan **before** they need anything from her | **+1** |
| **Brought to a social scene they'd have passed anyway** | **+2** |
| Consulted on a decision she has no role in | **+2** |
| **Someone declines her off-ramp** and engages the hard thing | **+2** |
| Her win credited to her, out loud, to a third party | **+2** |

> [!note] The math is deliberately steep, and honestly so
> Five or six episodes at two-to-three deployments each is a **−10 to −18 baseline** before anything else. Reaching positive requires the party to consistently pay TP for a worse-value option *and* keep noticing her in scenes where she isn't needed. That's a behavioural change, not a lucky roll.
>
> **The −1 for sending her alone fires even though it is the correct play.** She is not being mistreated. She is being used well, by people who like her, in the way that most efficiently gets everyone what they want. **If it ever feels like the party is being punished for playing smart, it's working.**

**Read at [[The Equinox Arc|the Equinox]]:**

| Score | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **≤ −6** | Lieutenant, fully committed. The reveal lands as designed |
| **−5 to 0** | Lieutenant, but reachable at Interest 4+ |
| **+1 to +5** | Drifting but conflicted. A PC can reach her in one scene |
| **≥ +6** | **She never becomes the heir.** Wesley needs someone else |

## Her hurt looks like extra grace

**The behavioural tell to run consistently.** She's a bridge person; managing rooms is what she does. Wounded, she doesn't escalate — she gets *more gracious*, smooths it over, and moves to being useful.

**So she always offers an off-ramp**, and taking it is free and feels kind. Declining it is the only unambiguous way a PC can show her she's a member rather than a resource. See the Ep3 confrontation in [[Episode 3 — Second Shift]] for the pattern.

## If the party actually wins her

Her two roles **come apart**, and this matters:

- **Bloodline anchor** — she is the only Mercer-line participant available. If she refuses, **the ritual cannot complete.**
- **Apprentice/heir** — a role somebody else could fill.

A high-affinity Dominique therefore costs Wesley *the ritual*, which is a bigger prize than good manners should buy outright. **The fix: Wesley needs another Mercer.** Losing his heir doesn't stop him — it sends him looking. Are there other Ferraros? Is Dominique's mother in play? A cousin? Now the party has a **different** person to protect, who they have never met.

**And the natural replacement heir is [[Ren Sołtys]].** She keeps records, she needs to know how things work, and she is about to arrive at his door asking for method. **If the party saves Dominique by including her, the seat is filled by the girl they also got hollowed.**

> [!note] The inclusive path has its own way of losing her
> A Dominique who feels like a **member** follows them in. A courier waits by the phone. **High affinity makes it more likely she eventually goes into the dungeon after them, not less** — and she has no ember. Planned inciting incident: she finds a crucial piece of intel *after* the party has already gone down, follows to deliver it, succeeds, and is **hollowed** for a long stretch — long enough that the party may forget her across multiple sessions, which leaves her wide open to Wesley. **The tracker never becomes a "solved it" button.**

## The fall — gradual, clear-eyed, on-screen
She **becomes**, by degrees, in plain sight: belonging → status → (when she glimpses what Wesley is really doing) rationalization, because by then the group is too much of a family to betray and she's too important in it to leave. She is **not hollowed and not a victim** in the Act-1 arc — she keeps full agency, which is the horror. **Design rule: every step of her drift must look, in the moment, like good news.**

> [!danger] The woman-in-the-van betrayal
> The party hands Dominique the town-side follow-ups — including checking on the Rivals (*"Dominique's been looking out for [[Zander Pruitt|Zander]] since [episode] — glad someone is"*). They think she's helping the Rivals warm toward the party. She's walking each one **further into Wesley's family.** Every act of trust they place in her is a thread she uses to pull a Rival away — they aren't failing to protect the Rivals, they're *delivering* them, through the person they trust most.

## Bash's precognition payoff (true but misread)
The window is *accurate* but Bash reads it as something being *done to* her and sets out to **save** her. The unbearable turn: **she chose it, in plain sight, while he was reading her as a system to track instead of a person to talk to.** His instrument was never wrong; his interpretation was.

## Mechanical role
- **Bloodline anchor:** [[The Equinox Ritual|the ritual]] cannot complete without a [[Elias Mercer|Mercer]]-line participant. **Exempt from the hollowing qualifier** — the ritual needs her *blood* (her lack of the "lost-a-week" look is a quiet tell of her status).
- **Continuation-regardless:** if [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] is killed pre-equinox, **Dominique completes the ritual** — unless she herself is pulled back. See [[The Other Party (Rival or Evil)]].
- **Reachable — but only just.** Not won by Time-Points disposition like the recruits. Reachable only if a PC refuses the misdirection and **sees her** — confronts the person, not the asset. If the ritual succeeds, she's **corrupted, not sacrificed.**
- **Re-homes [[Eddie Park]]'s former "into-the-Mercer-papers" thread** — bookish enough to follow it into the [[The Mercer Mansion|Mercer papers]] herself, which is partly how Wesley spots his heir.
