# Dominique Ferraro

> [!danger] DM-only
> This note is now spoiler material. To the players, Dominique is [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s cool older cousin and the party's trusted aboveground "heart." She is secretly [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s apprentice/heir and the ritual's bloodline anchor — the most personal betrayal in Act 1. Seed every step of it as *good news;* the reread is what hurts.

**[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s older cousin (~18) — bookish but well-liked, the bridge between the nerds and the popular crowd — established early as the PCs' "woman in the van" aboveground, and revealed late as [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s chosen heir and the [[The Equinox Ritual|ritual]]'s [[Elias Mercer|Mercer]]-bloodline anchor.**

**Tonal direction: Mercer Falls' own Anakin Skywalker.** Not a mole and not a victim — a bright, loved, genuinely good person who is *walked* into it one defensible step at a time, in full view, with her agency intact the whole way.

## Who she is (the players' view)
~18, a Ferraro (Bash's maternal cousin). **Bookish and scholarly** — she shares [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s curiosity, which is why he idolizes her — but also genuinely *liked:* pretty, easy in any room, welcome among nerds and popular kids alike. She got Bash into computers; she's [[Tank]]'s crush. Early in Act 1 she becomes the party's **aboveground operator** — chasing leads, checking on people, holding the town side together while they delve. The bright, real, outside-the-dungeon thing the cage-carriers orbit.

## Current state — Episode 2 / Session 3
**She is in a very good spot, and that is the point.** As played ([[Episode 2 — The Hush — Play Log]]):
- **She confronted [[Tank]] on his stoop** about the smear landing on her cousin, came down off the boil on a good Charisma read, and **formally enlisted him** — *"I'm just trying to protect my baby cousin. I could really use your help."* First act of the episode's reassembly, exactly as designed.
- **[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] gave her the entire download**: the dungeon, the torches, the radio, the dog creature, the janitor, both deaths — and then **conjured a torch in his bare hand in front of her.** She read the conjuration as **a very good magic trick** (*"I didn't see anything up your sleeve… magicians and their secrets"*) and the rest as a wild story she wasn't going to call a lie.
- **She had already been told about [[Bash Whitlock|The Window]].** *"This isn't the first crazy thing you've told her."* ⚠ Load-bearing — see below.
- **She landed on provisional buy-in:** *"You say this happened. Until we find out otherwise — let's see this thing through."*
- She's in and out of Bash's kitchen regularly, on good terms with his mother.

> [!note] Resolved TBD — "how to make the players love Free-Dominique first"
> **Answered in play, emphatically.** She is the only person in Mercer Falls who took a kid's impossible story and said *let's see it through*. That is the exact debt the betrayal will collect on, and no further engineering is needed. **Keep her effective.** A Dominique who is *materially useful* — who runs down leads, calms adults, opens doors the kids can't — buys far more grief later than a Dominique who is merely warm.

**The full download is an asset, not a leak — for now.** She half-believes, which is the ideal state: enough to act, not enough to have a coherent picture she could hand to anyone. Whether and when any of it reaches [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] is **deliberately TBD** and should stay that way until her drift has actually begun; a premature leak spends the betrayal early and cheaply.

## The pressure-point — an absence, not a wound
Dominique isn't broken, so Wesley's cure-the-broken-leg pitch ([[The Cast and the Permanent Cast]]) doesn't work on her. Her crack is **rootlessness:** the universal bridge belongs *everywhere and nowhere* — welcome in every circle, a member of none; many loose ties, no deep one; never rejected, never truly held. [[The Community Center]] is the first place that feels like a **family with her near its center**, and [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] — who has spent his whole life alone in his clarity — offers her the one thing no one else can: **not healing, but partnership.** He finally meets a young mind that keeps up with his and grooms her as an **apprentice / intellectual heir.** *(Rhymes against [[Gern]]: both crave real belonging over loose belonging — Gern is offered a* seat*, Dominique a* throne beside the king*.)*

## Generating the fractures — the design problem, and its solution
**The requirement:** the party must be creating real grievances that are **major from her side and nearly invisible from theirs.** Not conflict — *conflict would be visible.* The players should be able to review every interaction afterward and find nothing they'd have called a betrayal.

**The unifying axis — welcome vs. membership.** Her wound is being *welcome everywhere and a member of nowhere.* So every fracture is a moment where **the party's genuine warmth turns out to be welcome without membership** — and Wesley's offer is membership. That's the whole engine. Any beat that puts her *adjacent to* the thing rather than *inside* it does the work, and every one of those beats will look, from the PC side, like being included.

**Concrete generators (use freely; none require anyone to be unkind):**
1. **She's left at the threshold, every single time.** They delve; she waits. Structurally unavoidable and therefore endlessly repeatable. From their side: she's holding down the town. From hers: she is the one person in this who never gets to go.
2. **She is handed errands and calls it trust.** The design already has the party routing town-side follow-ups through her. Each assignment is a real vote of confidence *and* a reminder that she's the courier for a group of children having the actual experience. She is eighteen, the most capable of them, and the only one with no powers.
3. **She's told the truth and cannot verify a word of it.** No ember, no dungeon access, no proof. She believed them on nothing but love — which is isolating in a way the party structurally cannot perceive, because they have each other and the memory. She has a magic trick and a wild story.
4. **Her wins become their wins.** She solves something town-side; it enters the party's account of events as a thing *they* figured out. Nobody is stealing credit; it just isn't her story.
5. **She is talked *about* more than talked *to*.** Especially by Bash. Which brings us to the loaded gun:

> [!danger] The Window — armed in Session 3
> Bash's precognition arc has him seeing **"something terrible happening to Dominique."** The payoff was always that the reading is *accurate* (her at the ritual, aligned with Wesley) and his interpretation is wrong — he sets out to **save** her from a thing she is *choosing*, because his flaw is treating a person as a system to monitor rather than someone to talk to.
>
> **She now knows the Window exists** — he told her about it before this episode. So when it eventually shows her, there is a scene available where **Dominique learns her little cousin has been watching her fate on an instrument** instead of asking her a question. From her side: *he has been reading me.* That is a devastating, entirely earned grievance, it is **invisible to Bash as anything but love and diligence**, and it is the single most on-theme fracture available. His disclosure armed it without either of them noticing. **Do not spend this early.**

**Keep it out of the players' hands.** No fracture should ever be *stateable* by a PC. If a player says "I think Dominique feels left out," soften and move on — the reveal must land as a reread, not a prediction.

## The fall — gradual, clear-eyed, on-screen
She **becomes**, by degrees, in plain sight: belonging → status → (when she glimpses what Wesley is really doing) rationalization, because by then the group is too much of a family to betray and she's too important in it to leave. She is **not hollowed and not a victim** — she keeps full agency the whole way, which is the horror: she chose it, step by step, while the party wasn't looking. **Design rule: every step of her drift must look, in the moment, like good news.**

> [!danger] The woman-in-the-van betrayal
> The party hands Dominique the town-side follow-ups — including checking on the Rivals (*"Dominique's been looking out for [[Zander Pruitt|Zander]] since [episode] — glad someone is"*). They think she's helping the Rivals heal and warm toward the party. She's walking each one **further into Wesley's family.** Every act of trust they place in her is a thread she uses to pull a Rival away — they aren't failing to protect the Rivals from Wesley, they're *delivering* them, through the person they trust most. It was visible all along, framed as good news.

## Bash's precognition payoff (true but misread)
The seed in [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s arc pays off here. The window is *accurate* but Bash reads it as something being *done to* her and sets out to **save** her. The unbearable turn: it isn't being done to her — **she chose it, in plain sight, while he was reading her as a system to track instead of a person to talk to.** His instrument was never wrong; his interpretation was — the exact failure his flaw sets up. His whole "save Dominique" mission rests on a false premise the campaign eventually detonates.

## Mechanical role
- **Bloodline anchor:** [[The Equinox Ritual|the ritual]] cannot complete without a [[Elias Mercer|Mercer]]-line participant; she's it. **Exempt from the hollowing qualifier** — the ritual needs her *blood* (her lack of the "lost-a-week" look is a quiet tell of her status).
- **Continuation-regardless:** if [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] is killed pre-equinox, **Dominique completes the ritual** — the gate opens whether he lives or dies, *unless she herself is pulled back.* See [[The Other Party (Rival or Evil)]].
- **Reachable — but only just.** She isn't won by Time-Points disposition like the recruits (she has full agency; she chose). She's reachable *only* if a PC refuses the misdirection and **sees her** — confronts the person, not the asset. If reached (Interest 4+), the bloodline anchor refuses and the **ritual cannot complete** (the highest-leverage equinox outcome). Wesley himself stays unreachable; she is the rival party's one reachable heart. If the ritual succeeds, she's **corrupted, not sacrificed.**
- **Re-homes [[Eddie Park]]'s former "into-the-Mercer-papers" thread** — bookish enough to follow it into the [[The Mercer Mansion|Mercer papers]] herself, which is partly how Wesley spots his heir.
