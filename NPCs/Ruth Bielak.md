# Ruth Bielak

Up: [[Hollowed Townspeople]]

**71, widowed, on Delaney two blocks off the mill road. Hollowed in autumn 1994, eleven days, recovered. She is the only previously-hollowed person inside the [[The Siren|Siren]]'s radius who has never once walked — and she isn't protected by anything clever. She's protected by a broken tape player.**

## The anomaly

Put [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s historical roster on [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]]'s map and every previously-hollowed person inside the circle is walking. **Except one dot, well inside the innermost ring, dark.**

**She should have been in the first three.** She's closer to the mill than nearly anyone on the list and she's been notched for two years. The anomaly reads as **a hole in a circle** — legible to any player who looks at the map, requiring no statistics and no misdirection.

## Walt

Her husband died in 1989. She sleeps in the front room with a portable cassette player on the windowsill, and it plays a tape of him — reading the sports page, or talking on some long car trip. Something ordinary. **Seven years, every night.**

**The machine is nearly dead. The belt is stretched. It runs slow.**

> *"He sounds slower than he was lately. I keep meaning to take it in."*

## Why it works — occupancy, not blocking

**[[Nullprints|Her notch is already full.**]] Seven years of a stretched belt running a voice at the wrong speed, every night, sitting in exactly the frequency the Siren calls on. **There is no room in her for anything else to get in.**

**Content is irrelevant. Only speed matters, because speed is the frequency.** Any recording would do. **If she had gotten the machine fixed, she would be walking to the mill.**

This is [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s deduction to make, and he should get to make it out loud. It converts directly into the Talkboy — see [[Episode 3 — Second Shift]].

## Why this cannot come from the party

> [!danger] Dominique-only
> Four teenagers do not get told about a dead husband's voice on a cassette. It's in her bedroom, at night, and **Ruth doesn't know it's relevant** — nobody would volunteer it. It comes out sideways, in the kind of conversation an eighteen-year-old has with a lonely seventy-one-year-old over an hour and a cup of coffee.
>
> **The party goes door to door and gets names. [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] goes to one house and stays.** That's what she's for — and it is precisely the skill that will be used on the party in September.
>
> Per the **Adult Access ladder**, PCs asking a widow about her sleep is a **DC +5** situation. Let them try it and fail before Dominique goes.

Her report, delivered without knowing it's the answer:

> *"She talked at me for an hour and a half. I've got her whole life now — her husband Walt, the store, the dog they had."*
>
> *"She plays a tape of him at night. To sleep. Seven years."*
>
> *A shrug — she thinks the point is that Ruth is lonely.*
>
> *"She said it's broken. Said he sounds slow now, and she keeps meaning to take it in."*

## Her own hollowing

**Autumn 1994, eleven days.** In [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s book with an end date. What she was doing near the mill is **undecided** — she is not a mill family, and a 71-year-old widow with no reason to be down there is a small open question worth answering eventually.

## Open dials

- **What Walt is saying on the tape.** Unwritten, and it matters more than it looks — the party will hear it. Recommended: something completely mundane, mid-sentence, with a laugh in it somewhere.
- **Why she was hollowed in '94.** Undecided.
- Whether she ever gets the machine fixed. *(If she does, and the party knows what that means, that's a whole scene.)*
- Whether the party ever tells her — and whether telling her would take the protection away.
