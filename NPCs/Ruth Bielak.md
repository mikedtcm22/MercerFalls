# Ruth Bielak

Up: [[Hollowed Townspeople]]

**71, widowed, on Ellis Street. Hollowed in autumn 1994 because she went into the mill looking for her husband's lunch pail. For five years she was the only previously-hollowed person inside the [[The Siren|Siren]]'s radius who never walked — protected by a broken tape player. Then in [[Session 7 — Second Shift|Session 7]] the party fixed it for her, and now she walks.**

> [!danger] Street correction
> Run in play as **Ellis Street** — the innermost ring, alongside [[Stan Prokop]] and the first three walkers. Earlier drafts said Delaney; **Ellis is canon.** It also sharpens the anomaly: she was as close as anyone and still wasn't out there.

## The anomaly — found by Ren

Put [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s historical roster against her walker roster and every previously-hollowed person inside the circle is walking. **Except one.**

**Ren found it herself**, at the diner counter, off her own two pages: *"Wait a minute — Ruth lives on Ellis Street. She should be walking. If the closest people get called first she should be up there. I don't understand why it's not affecting her."*

## Walt

**[[Walt Bielak]] died in [[The 1991 Mill Incident|the 1991 incident]]** — a real industrial death in the accident that covered the hollowings. Thirty-one years on the job, much of it on [[The Worked Floors|the worked floors]]. **The mill shut the same week, so his effects were never returned.**

She sleeps in the front room with a portable cassette player on the windowsill playing a tape of him — reading the sports page, half a conversation, something ordinary. **Five years, every night.**

**She visits a park bench by the waterfall, with the mill rising over it**, and sits there thinking about him. *(Established Session 7 — and worth noting she has been going to look at that building voluntarily for five years.)*

## Why it worked — occupancy, not blocking

**Her [[Nullprints|notch]] was already full.** A stretched belt and dying batteries ran his voice **slow**, every night, in exactly the frequency the Siren calls on. **There was no room in her for anything else to get in.**

**Content is irrelevant. Only speed matters, because speed is the frequency.**

## What the party did to her

> [!danger] They removed her protection, knowingly, out of kindness
> All four PCs plus [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] came to her door. Tea, chocolate milk for [[Gern]]. She played the tape and the warble was plain.
>
> **[[Old Man]] rolled a natural 27 to talk her into lending it overnight** to "fix" it. **[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] cast Mending.** They returned it working, and she was delighted: *"I can finally hear my Walt the way he used to sound again."* Bash earned inspiration for the kindness — and said the quiet part out loud: ***"I just signed her death warrant."***
>
> **That same night, testing [[The Modded Talkboy|the Talkboy]], Ruth was among the walkers.**
>
> **Cash this slowly.** Don't underline it. Just have her out there again the next night, and the night after. She is now one of the two people the party has personally put on that road — [[Wendell Pace|Wendell]] is the other, and he's there for the opposite reason.

She is a walker for the rest of the episode, including the Session 8 cold open.

## The lunch pail — a minor quest object

> [!note] Why it's deep
> **Mill workers went down there as part of the job.** Walt's station was on [[The Worked Floors|the worked floors]]. **The pail is exactly where he set it down**, on a hook, the week the mill closed. Place it on **Floor 2 or below.** Not guarded, not magical, not a puzzle — a dented lunch pail in a room full of lockers, and the party walks past it unless they know to look.

**Her 1994 trip.** Three years after Walt died she went through the boxes and the one thing missing was the pail. She got in through a fence gap she'd known about since she was a girl, **got nowhere near his station** — Walt described the work, never the depth — and something took her a short way past the door. Eleven days. **She does not know whether she found it**, which is why she stopped looking.

**What's inside, which she doesn't know about:**
- **A photograph cut down with scissors to fit** — a bad one. She's laughing with her eyes shut, slightly blurred, a stranger's elbow in the corner. **He kept the bad one.**
- **A ticket stub.** A dance at the Armory, autumn 1947. Two admissions.
- **A napkin folded to the size of a stamp**, her handwriting, a phone exchange that stopped existing in 1961.

**He carried the night they met down a shaft into that thing, every shift, for thirty-one years, and never told her.**

**Running the return:** either they open it in the dungeon and have to decide how to hand it over, or they give it to her sealed and the table watches her find out in real time. **Either way she is not effusive.** She goes quiet, puts the napkin down, and says something small and off-topic.

**What it's worth:** nothing mechanical. **Ruth starts talking** — and a woman who absorbed thirty-one years of mill-shift conversation at her own kitchen table becomes a standing research node on that building, earned.

> [!note] Do not say this out loud
> [[Nullprints|Release]] requires an object proving a choice of theirs outlived them. **The party will be holding a textbook example, for unrelated reasons, well before they learn what release is.**

## Access

**[[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] is the key**, and it played exactly as designed — she knows Ruth from the **[[Mercer Falls Historical Society|Historical Society]]**, where she works part-time, and offered: *"She might open up a little bit more if I'm there too."* Four teenagers on a widow's porch is a **DC +5** situation; with Dominique fronting it's tea in the kitchen.

## Open dials

- **What Walt is saying on the tape.** Still unwritten, and the party has now heard it twice. Recommended: completely mundane, mid-sentence, with a laugh in it somewhere.
- Whether anyone ever tells her why she started sleepwalking.
- Whether she'd let them break it again if they asked — and whether they'd dare.
- **Held in reserve:** making Walt one of the hollowed seven. Devastating, promotes Ruth to an Act-2 figure, doesn't expire. See [[Walt Bielak]].
