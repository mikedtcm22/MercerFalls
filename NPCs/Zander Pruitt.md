# Zander Pruitt

Up: [[Cast & NPCs]]

**The fourth of [[Wesley's Recruits]] — debuting in [[Episode 5 — The Long Glass]] as a friendly rival. Designed as the foil to [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]: competence without direction. Eventually runs mechanically as a Wild Magic Sorcerer.**

> [!warning] HIS ROLE IN EP5 IS EXPLICITLY UNSETTLED — REVISIT BEFORE BUILDING
> The DM is **not satisfied** with the current treatment and wants a dedicated session to explore alternatives.
>
> **The specific objection:** as written he is a **scavenger / 100% opportunist** — he's found a door, there's free junk behind it, and he keeps going back for comic money. That's too thin. **The character needs more depth than "pleasant little looter."**
>
> What stays regardless:
> - Foil to Bash — competence without direction, flashes of brilliance = choosing a worthy target
> - Friendly rival from day one, not a victim and not a gate
> - Wild Magic Sorcerer eventually
> - Gets hollowed-and-survived in Ep5, and Wesley is the one who restores him
>
> What's up for grabs: **why he goes into the mirror-bridge at all.** Everything below the line is placeholder until that's resolved.

## Design thesis (stable)

**The Bash foil.** [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s creed is *it's all readable — you just need the right instrument.* Zander cracked a genuinely hard supernatural puzzle box in three weeks, alone, with no instruments and no theory, and won't hold still to be measured. He is a walking refutation who doesn't care that he is.

> [!danger] DM-only
> Bash's gear is the Entity's antenna. Zander's luck may be the same signal with no instrument at all. **Two antennas — one tuned, one not.**

**The agency thesis.** The campaign's argument is that agency is the answer. Zander's best outcomes are the ones he *didn't choose*, and it's been working, so why stop. That makes him the recruit [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] barely has to work on: the pitch is *hand me the wheel*, and Zander handed it to luck years ago.

**The flashes of frightening competence are load-bearing, not a gag.** They are the moments he takes the wheel back — the same skills aimed at a worthy target for the first time.

**Disposition hinge:** the party wins him by **catching him being deliberate and naming it out loud.** Pitfall: praising his luck. He is maximally reachable and maximally losable on exactly that axis.

**Wild Magic as chassis:** a caster whose power is *outcomes without choice.* Keep the on-screen magic to one unexplained step in his method. No more than that in Act 1.

## Ep5 treatment (PLACEHOLDER — see warning above)

### Access to the bridge
Did the dare at [[Panels]] in late June — **patient zero of the 1996 window.** Night 2 came and The Kept couldn't find him: latent wild magic means his reflection won't hold still, the lookup fails, **his file can't close.**

The mark is a door held open on you. **His never shuts.**

He discovered it by nerve rather than luck: he stood in the room and *watched it fail to find him.* Didn't run — watched. Then spent a week testing the glass and went in.

Nobody can copy this. It isn't a technique, it's a condition, and every other mark in town closes within days.

### The schedule
The Panels mirror goes soft every other evening for about an hour. He clocked the rhythm in June and wrote it inside a Trapper Keeper. **He thinks it's just what the mirror does.**

It's actually The Kept going out to collect. They pass each other in the dark, in opposite directions, and he never sees a victim because it exits through the victim's own bathroom mirror across town. **His end of the operation is genuinely, completely empty.**

> The Act 3 gut-punch requires no guilt on his part: the party works out that the schedule isn't a property of the mirror. It's a feeding rhythm. **Every window is a person — and he's been counting.**

### Motive *(THE WEAK PART)*
Curios off the corridor walls, fenced to [[Jerry]] and [[Russ]], funding comics and arcade time. He's never gone past twenty feet in and has no idea there's a dungeon end.

**This is the bit that needs replacing.** It gives him an unattended warehouse and no interior life.

### The race
Goals aren't opposed — he doesn't want anyone dead. **Methods** are. The party's fix collapses the bridge permanently; that's his door. He stalls, misdirects and lies pleasantly until Act 4.

**His argument is good and the party has to actually beat it:** *stop the dare, problem solved — why does it have to die?* Counter: his stuck-open door means covering the mirror doesn't fully work. **He is the reason the safe option fails.**

### Exit
Inside the bridge when it collapses. Ejected into the mirror chamber, hollowed by the ejection — full deep black [[Nullprints|nullprint]], no ember. **Wesley restores him on the spot** and he wakes with Wesley's hand on his shoulder in the dark under [[The Mill]].

Genuine rescue, genuine debt. Qualifier satisfied: **hollowed, survived, notched, audible.** Candidate for the roster's one *manufactured* hollowing if Wesley knew he was in there.

## Threads to pull when revisiting

- **What does he want that isn't money?** The scavenging reads as a symptom with no cause behind it.
- **Home situation.** Nothing established. Likeliest source of the missing depth.
- **Why Panels specifically** — is Russ a father-shaped figure to him? That would reframe the whole Ep5 dynamic and make the ceded-territory decision bite harder.
- **Does he know he's unusual?** He has survived something nobody survives and has never asked why.
- **Alternative frames worth pitching:** he's protecting someone; he's looking for a specific object; he's buying his way out of town; he's the only one who believes the dead kid mattered; he's doing it because it's the one thing he's ever been good at.
- **Pre-Ep5 Wesley contact** — "a nice man who buys weird junk and asks unusually good questions" is how Wesley eventually learns the near mirror's location. How early does that start?
