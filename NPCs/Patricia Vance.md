# Patricia Vance

Up: [[Cast & NPCs]]

**[[Ricky Vance|Ricky]]'s mother — the frazzled, present parent holding a house together around an absent husband and a son who has been quietly doing an adult's job since he was old enough to notice. As of Session 3 she is the most dangerous unattended thread in [[Episode 2 — The Hush]]: her son is missing, her husband has disappeared with a duffel bag of shotgun shells, and the party is about to convince the whole town that Ricky is walking around fine.**

## Profile
Forties. The parent who is *there* — school forms, dinners, the phone calls, the worry — and who has been there alone for most of a marriage to a man who is always needed somewhere he can't discuss. Not fragile; **worn**. She has run out of the kind of resilience that comes from having anyone to lean on, and what's left is vigilance.

She is the mirror of [[Wade Vance|Wade]] in Ricky's psychology: **Wade is absent and idealized, Patricia is present and discounted.** [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]] built his whole self out of imitating the father who isn't there, which means the parent who actually raised him is the one whose steadiness he never learned to see. *(Worth playing: she knows this. It is an old, quiet grief and she doesn't blame him for it.)*

**Not Filter-resistant** in the way Wade is. She doesn't perceive the anomalous; she perceives that **everyone around her is behaving strangely about her son**, which is worse in some ways — it isolates her without giving her anything to act on. See [[The Reality Filter]].

## Current state (Episode 2)
- **Ricky has been gone since the dare night.** The town believes Wade shipped him off to a military-style summer program. She knows better, because Wade didn't tell her he was taking him, and Wade isn't answering.
- **Wade has vanished too** — off hunting the [[The Hush|Hush]]. She has no idea that's what he's doing; she knows only that he bought an unreasonable amount of ammunition, packed a bag, and left. She has asked around town about it, including at [[Jerry's Pawn Shop|Jerry's]].
- **She is asking people if they've seen either of them.** Tank's defensive coordinator has already run into her and described her as *"real worried"* — and told [[Tank]] to go check on the family. That is the first of two intended nudges toward her door. See [[Episode 2 — The Hush]].

## In play — the branch she controls

> [!danger] DM-only — Patricia is the decoy plan's price
> In Session 3 the party invented a plan to have [[Old Man]] appear at [[Marco's Diner|Marco's]] **disguised as Ricky** so the football team would stop blaming [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] for snitching. **It will work on the kids, and it will reach Patricia within the day.**
>
> She does not hear *"my son is alive."* She hears **"everyone in this town is lying to me about where my son is."** Her husband is gone, her son has apparently been seen strolling into a diner, and nobody will give her a straight answer. That is the shape of the thing that breaks her.
>
> **Mitigated by a Town-Time visit to Ricky's house** (unlocked in Session 3, unvisited). A successful visit — calming her, giving her one true thing to hold, or simply being present and kind — defuses it. **This is also where the blaze-orange bandana comes from,** the [[Wade Vance|Wade]] key and the spore mask, so the trip that prevents the catastrophe is the same trip that arms the climax.
>
> **If she festers:** she concludes Wade is searching in the wrong place and **goes into the woods to bring him home herself.** No training, no protection, no idea what is out there. **She is the one the Hush hurts** — and that becomes the reason [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]] resents the party **even if they rescue him.** Timing runs her into the woods around Day D–E, comfortably before the climax, which can also cost the party Wade as an ally without a single bad roll in the fight.
>
> **She is also the convergence backstop.** The party has skipped both designed routes to "something took Ricky" ([[Russ]] and the [[The Library|Library]] pattern). A mother mauled in the woods forces the connection — ugly, earned, and entirely their doing. If they never find the clock any other way, this is how they find it.

**The cruelty of the shape, worth not editorializing.** The decoy plan was invented to keep bullies off a friend — a small, real, sympathetic good. Its price is a frightened woman walking into the trees. Let the table make that connection themselves, afterward.

## How to play her
- **Not hysterical.** She is *precise*, and it's unnerving. She asks specific questions and remembers the answers. A kid lying to her should feel it.
- **She will know something is off about the boys.** They were at the mill that night. She hasn't accused them of anything, but she has noticed.
- **She is easy to be kind to and hard to fool.** Deception here should be a real check with real fallout; sincerity should be disproportionately rewarded. The party cannot lie their way out of this branch, only care their way out.
- **One line to have ready:** *"People keep telling me not to worry. Every single one of them. Do you know how that sounds, all together?"* — the Filter, described from inside by someone who can't name it.

## Open dials
- **What she actually knows about Wade.** Probably nothing about the unit; possibly more than she lets on after twenty years of not asking. Decide before she and the party talk properly.
- **Whether she survives the woods**, if it comes to that. Wounded is the designed outcome and the more useful one — a mother laid up is a permanent reproach and keeps Ricky's grievance alive; killed is available but should be reserved.
- **Whether she becomes an ally afterward.** A Patricia who is *told the truth* and believes it is a rare thing: a Filter-broken adult who is neither trained nor armed, but who is inside the Vance household and has every reason to help. Not designed; available.
