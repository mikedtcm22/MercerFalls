# Walt Bielak

Up: [[Hollowed Townspeople]]

**[[Ruth Bielak|Ruth]]'s husband. Thirty-one years at the mill, much of it on the worked floors below. Died in [[The 1991 Mill Incident|the 1991 incident]] — a real industrial death, in the accident that covered the hollowings. His lunch pail is still on a hook where he left it.**

## The death

**He is not one of the hollowed seven.** He died the way the town believes everyone died that day: an actual industrial accident, in the chaos, alongside seven deaths that were something else entirely.

**That is what makes him useful.** Every other 1991 casualty is a [[Ghosts|ghost]] problem. Walt is just a man who died at work — which keeps [[Ruth Bielak|Ruth]] the size the design needs her to be, and leaves nothing to recover but the pail.

> [!note] Held in reserve — the alternative
> Making him **one of the seven** instead is available and devastating: Ruth would be playing his voice on a windowsill half a mile from his ghost, every night for five years, and [[Episode 4 — The Crossing]] hands you the mechanics for release.
>
> **Costs:** it promotes Ruth from a one-episode anomaly into an Act-2 figure, and it puts a release rite with her name on it in the party's hands. **Don't spend it unless you want her that big. It doesn't expire.**

## The effects that never came home

**The mill shut the same week.** Nothing at his station was ever returned to the family — no box, no call, no paperwork. Everything he had at work is still down there.

Ruth went through his things three years later and found the one object missing that she was certain of: **the old dented lunch pail he carried for thirty-one years.** That's what sent her into the mill in autumn 1994, and what got her hollowed. See [[Ruth Bielak]].

## The pail

On a hook, on the [[The Worked Floors|worked floors]], exactly where he set it down. Not guarded, not magical, not a puzzle — **a dented lunch pail in a room full of lockers**, and the party walks past it unless they know to look.

**What's inside, which Ruth does not know about:** a badly-cut photograph of her laughing with her eyes shut, a 1947 Armory dance ticket for two, and a napkin folded to the size of a stamp with her handwriting and a phone exchange that stopped existing in 1961.

**He carried the night they met down a shaft into that thing, every shift, for thirty-one years, and never told her.**

## What he's for

1. **He puts a named, ordinary man on the worked floors.** The lower mill isn't alien architecture — men from this town clocked in down there. See [[The Worked Floors]].
2. **He is the reason Ruth was in the dungeon**, which is the only load-bearing question her design had open.
3. **He is the tape.** Five years of a stretched belt playing his voice is what makes his widow the one person in the Flats the [[The Siren|Siren]] cannot reach.

## Open dials

- **What he's saying on the tape.** The party will hear it. Recommended: completely mundane, mid-sentence, with a laugh in it somewhere.
- Whether anyone else in town remembers him well enough to be a second source on the worked floors.
- Whether his station is findable as a *place* — a locker with his name on it is a stronger find than a pail on a hook.
