# Lowell Brandt

Up: [[Cast & NPCs]]

**Grace Tabernacle elder, dead a year, and the [[Ghosts|ghost]] at the mill-road grade crossing. A father who concluded his son's life was worth defying his church, his doctor, and his God for — and who was quietly eaten by the machine one Tuesday at a time while everyone who loved him thought he was at prayer meeting.**

> [!danger] DM-only
> Everything here is behind the screen. What the town has is an obituary, a cold funeral, and a bad crossing.

> [!important] Status after [[Session 9 — The Crossing Remembers|Session 9]]
> **The party knows who he is.** Via [[The Reading Glass]] (the crossing; his finger bone), the library microfilm and Ren's notebook, they have: Lowell Brandt, killed at the same crossing in July 1995, Grace Tabernacle, a bedridden son, three entries in [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s notebook, and that Ray Kubiak re-enacted his death. **They know nothing yet about the how** — white prints, release, the shoebox or the vial.
>
> **Canon conflicts to settle** (see *Conflicts* below): his death date, his three hollowings vs. the Severance trap, and where his live print is.

## Vitals

- **Lowell A. Brandt**, 1939–1995. Mid-fifties. Longtime elder at [[Reverend Hollis|Grace Tabernacle]].
- **Alma Brandt** — widow. Still in the house. Was shamed by the congregation and found kindness at [[The Community Center]].
- **Denny Brandt** — son, **11** (9 at diagnosis). Degenerative illness, doctors had nothing. Bed-bound as of summer 1996.
- **[[Eddie Park]]** — the boy he mentored. See that note; the two characters are built as one mechanism.

## Conflicts — proposed reconciliations (not yet decided)

- **Death date.** Proposed: **Saturday 8 July 1995**, matching Ren's note. Freights run Tuesdays and Saturdays, about 11:40 p.m., so the vigil night is the anniversary train. His Tuesday route stays; by the end, Saturdays too.
- **Three entries vs. "never restored topside."** Ren's notebook (as read at the table): 1993, eight days; 1995, "much longer"; June 1995, ending in death. Proposed: entry 2 = spring 1995 with Wesley, recovered; entry 3 = June 1995, the fall where he finds the elixir; the Severance cycle runs about five weeks, not four months (fewer "deliberate decisions," same shape).
- **The 1993 entry needs a reason** — Denny wasn't diagnosed yet. Options: (1) a 1993 Historical Society volunteer survey of the closed mill led by Wesley (preferred — ties to Wesley being "a fellow" there, Session 9); (2) pastoral, going in after someone's kid; (3) a misread, if the handout prints no year.
- **The bone vision's "colder weather."** Proposed: what read as cold was his work coat, worn in July because the routine said to.

## The Severance trap

The mechanism that defines him — see [[Nullprints]] for the rules (**revised 2026-10-17: the threshold rule**).

1. Each Tuesday, the autopilot keeps the appointment: his emptied body walks itself to [[The Mill]].
2. **Crossing the archway into [[The Cataloguing Office]] puts him back on his live [[Nullprints|nullprint]], himself** — no rite. **[[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] waits beside the print for him to appear.** Lowell is fully himself again — underground.
3. They delve. Wesley moves toward what *Wesley* wants; Lowell searches for the elixir. **They are not looking for the same thing and never were.**
4. Lowell falls. New print, further in — deeper, and the next hollowing longer. Hollowed body reconstitutes at the entrance and walks home.
5. Repeat, weekly.

**He was never restored topside** during the cycle. Alma never saw him well again after it began. Neither did Denny. Neither did Eddie.

> [!danger] For the last weeks of his life, [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] was the only person on earth who saw Lowell Brandt awake.
> Hold this. It's an Act-2 knife.

**Why he never simply left.** He never told Wesley *why* he was going down — he kept Denny out of it, to protect the boy. So when he woke at his print each week no longer holding the locket, he concluded it had been lost or taken by whatever killed him, and Wesley had no idea there was anything to correct. He spent the last of his life re-searching ground he had already cleared. **He had already won and nobody could tell him.**

**The skim** did the rest: each restoration returned slightly less. He wasn't descending at the end. He was getting slower and shallower.

## The one-directional filing rule → the shoebox

The hollowed **execute routines but cannot initiate them.** Storing is a routine (end of every trip, the thing in your hands goes in the box). Retrieving is a *decision*, and there was nobody home to make one.

So **everything Lowell ever brought up went into the box and nothing ever came out.** His autopilot's Tuesday-night route ended with a stop at Grace Tabernacle, where he had a floor cavity he used for private things — four minutes, alone, weekly, then home.

**The shoebox is at the church, not the house.** Alma never knew it existed. [[Eddie Park]] knows the spot, and found the box while clearing Lowell's effects. He has never opened it. *(Branch B prep proposes he put it back, so it's retrieved from the vestry floor on Sunday with Hollis present.)*

### What's in it — the stratification is the story

- **Bottom:** a real bound notebook. Neat, dated, Mercer citations, a plan. Written topside in early 1995 by a man who was still himself. **It stops abruptly.**
- **Middle:** loose scraps. After the first hollowing he could never retrieve the notebook again, so every week he woke at his print with no supplies and wrote on whatever was in his pockets — a mill requisition form, a church bulletin, the back of a receipt. Coherent at first. Increasingly repetitive. **The same rooms, searched again and again.** *(The scrap "woke on my own shape again" is the threshold rule in his handwriting.)*
- **Top:** the **locket** — a small vial on a chain. And above it three or four scraps that say almost nothing.

Lifted out in order, it is a man coming apart. That's the campaign's hollowing lecture delivered as a **prop, topside, in Act 2**, in front of [[Gern]] — not an infodump in a dungeon.

**The companion is never named.** Lowell protected his identity throughout. The kindest line in the notebook is the most damning: *"He is the only one who never told me to stop."*

## The emotional truth

**His faith outlived his church.**

Months before he died he had quietly concluded that [[Reverend Hollis|Hollis]] was wrong about his son. Not defiantly — he kept attending, kept his mouth shut, kept the peace. He simply never agreed, and went on doing what he thought was actually righteous, on his own schedule, with nobody watching and nobody to approve it.

And **restored Lowell chose to go deeper anyway.** Every week, fully lucid, knowing exactly what it cost, he walked further in instead of walking out.

> [!important] The release counter-claim (superseded here; see [[Episode 4 — The Crossing]] and [[Nullprints]])
> This note once said the counter-claim "isn't really an object — it's the trail." **Superseded.** Under the decided rule, a white print needs **proof that the unfinished business is finished**: the vial, after Denny is cured with it, refills with black ichor — the delivered errand. That is what gets burned on his print. The trail remains the emotional truth; the vial is the receipt.

> [!note] Why this is Gern's ending wearing a corpse
> [[Gern]]'s locked resolution is *a life of conviction, consciously chosen.* That is exactly and only Lowell Brandt. Gern will stand over the nullprint of the man he's supposed to become and not know it.

## As a ghost

Full rules in [[Ghosts]]. Lowell-specific:

- **Anchor:** the mill-road grade crossing where he died. **Lights only, no gates** (as played).
- **He is commuting, not hunting.** He is *late*, and Denny is waiting.
- **Blind spot:** the crossing itself. The file says he never crossed, so he cannot perceive bells, lights, horn or train — or any gate arm. He walks borrowed bodies into it while sincerely trying to save a child. (As played: the driver of Ray Kubiak's train thought his horn didn't work.)
- **Broadcast 1 — shame**, constant, low volume. Hollis's verdict on him, still ringing. Stand near the crossing and you start re-litigating your own failures, and some that aren't yours. *(This is the episode's misdirection radiating from the monster itself.)*
- **Broadcast 2 — compulsion**, in the victim's own voice: *I'm so late. He's waiting.*
- **The visible loop:** a man in a work coat walking the ballast, stopping, **patting his coat pockets for something that isn't there.** He was supposed to be bringing something home. He can't remember what and he can't stop checking. *(Branch B prep: his Tuesday habit was bringing Denny something in his right coat pocket.)*
- **The lethal window is a train on approach** — roughly ninety seconds, twice a week. Otherwise he's dread. The freight timetable is public and a two-TP investigation converts directly into survival.
- **His remains in play:** Credo found **a human finger bone of his** in the ballast (Session 9). It's a remnant, not a choice — never a counter-claim. Proposed: burning it topside quiets him until the next train, and the smoke leans uphill toward the mill.
