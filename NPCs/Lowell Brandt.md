# Lowell Brandt

Up: [[Cast & NPCs]]

**Grace Tabernacle elder, dead a year, and the [[Ghosts|ghost]] at the mill-road grade crossing. A father who concluded his son's life was worth defying his church, his doctor, and his God for — and who was quietly eaten by the machine one Tuesday at a time while everyone who loved him thought he was at prayer meeting.**

> [!danger] DM-only
> Everything here is behind the screen. What the town has is an obituary, a cold funeral, and a bad crossing.

## Vitals

- **Lowell A. Brandt**, 1939–1995. Mid-fifties. Longtime elder at [[Reverend Hollis|Grace Tabernacle]].
- **Alma Brandt** — widow. Still in the house. Was shamed by the congregation and found kindness at [[The Community Center]].
- **Denny Brandt** — son, **11** (9 at diagnosis). Degenerative illness, doctors had nothing. Bed-bound as of summer 1996.
- **[[Eddie Park]]** — the boy he mentored. See that note; the two characters are built as one mechanism.

## The Severance trap

The mechanism that defines him — see [[Nullprints]] for the rules.

1. Each Tuesday, the autopilot keeps the appointment: his emptied body walks itself to [[The Mill]].
2. [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] meets it and walks it to his live [[Nullprints|nullprint]]. **Restoration.** Lowell is fully himself again — underground.
3. They delve. Wesley moves toward what *Wesley* wants; Lowell searches for the elixir. **They are not looking for the same thing and never were.**
4. Lowell falls. New print, further in. Hollowed body reconstitutes at the entrance and walks home.
5. Repeat, weekly, for months.

**He was never restored topside.** Not once. Alma never saw him well again after spring 1995. Neither did Denny. Neither did Eddie.

> [!danger] For the last four months of his life, [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] was the only person on earth who saw Lowell Brandt awake.
> Hold this. It's an Act-2 knife.

**Why he never simply left.** He never told Wesley *why* he was going down — he kept Denny out of it, to protect the boy. So when he woke at his print each week no longer holding the locket, he concluded it had been lost or taken by whatever killed him, and Wesley had no idea there was anything to correct. He spent the last months of his life re-searching ground he had already cleared. **He had already won and nobody could tell him.**

**The skim** did the rest: each restoration returned slightly less. He wasn't descending at the end. He was getting slower and shallower.

## The one-directional filing rule → the shoebox

The hollowed **execute routines but cannot initiate them.** Storing is a routine (end of every trip, the thing in your hands goes in the box). Retrieving is a *decision*, and there was nobody home to make one.

So **everything Lowell ever brought up went into the box and nothing ever came out.** His autopilot's Tuesday-night route ended with a stop at Grace Tabernacle, where he had a floor cavity he used for private things — four minutes, alone, weekly, then home.

**The shoebox is at the church, not the house.** Alma never knew it existed. [[Eddie Park]] knows the spot, and found the box while clearing Lowell's effects. He has never opened it.

### What's in it — the stratification is the story

- **Bottom:** a real bound notebook. Neat, dated, Mercer citations, a plan. Written topside in early 1995 by a man who was still himself. **It stops abruptly.**
- **Middle:** loose scraps. After the first hollowing he could never retrieve the notebook again, so every week he woke at his print with no supplies and wrote on whatever was in his pockets — a mill requisition form, a church bulletin, the back of a receipt. Coherent at first. Increasingly repetitive. **The same rooms, searched again and again.**
- **Top:** the **locket** — a small vial on a chain. And above it three or four scraps that say almost nothing.

Lifted out in order, it is a man coming apart. That's the campaign's hollowing lecture delivered as a **prop, topside, in Act 2**, in front of [[Gern]] — not an infodump in a dungeon.

**The companion is never named.** Lowell protected his identity throughout. The kindest line in the notebook is the most damning: *"He is the only one who never told me to stop."*

## The emotional truth (the release counter-claim)

**His faith outlived his church.**

Months before he died he had quietly concluded that [[Reverend Hollis|Hollis]] was wrong about his son. Not defiantly — he kept attending, kept his mouth shut, kept the peace. He simply never agreed, and went on doing what he thought was actually righteous, on his own schedule, with nobody watching and nobody to approve it.

And **restored Lowell chose to go deeper anyway.** Every week, fully lucid, knowing exactly what it cost, he walked further in instead of walking out. Fifteen-odd deliberate decisions.

So the counter-claim to the ledger isn't really an object — **it's the trail.** The prints *are* the proof of agency. The elixir is the receipt.

> [!note] Why this is Gern's ending wearing a corpse
> [[Gern]]'s locked resolution is *a life of conviction, consciously chosen.* That is exactly and only Lowell Brandt. Gern will stand over the nullprint of the man he's supposed to become and not know it.

## The nullprint trail

- **Closed prints** (restored from) run downward through Floors 2+, mostly consumed. Unreachable at Act-1 levels — a promise of depth, not a destination.
- **The live print is in [[The Pit Room]] (room 9)** — shallow, because by the end the floor's exit-guardian was as far as he could get. White since June 1995.
- **He isn't arms-open like [[Wendell Pace|Wendell]].** He's on his side, one arm out toward the crossing. He was still trying to get across.
- *(Optional texture: one nearly-eaten early smudge somewhere the party has already walked — the [[The Expedition Post|Expedition Post]] or the [[The Octagonal Vestibule|Vestibule]]. Not needed for anything. Just the moment they realise they've stepped over him before.)*

## As a ghost

Full rules in [[Ghosts]]. Lowell-specific:

- **Anchor:** the mill-road grade crossing where he died.
- **He is commuting, not hunting.** He is *late*, and Denny is waiting.
- **Blind spot:** the crossing itself. The file says he never crossed, so he cannot perceive bells, lights, or train. He walks borrowed bodies into it while sincerely trying to save a child.
- **Broadcast 1 — shame**, constant, low volume. Hollis's verdict on him, still ringing. Stand near the crossing and you start re-litigating your own failures, and some that aren't yours. *(This is the episode's misdirection radiating from the monster itself.)*
- **Broadcast 2 — compulsion**, in the victim's own voice: *I'm so late. He's waiting.*
- **The visible loop:** a man in a work coat walking the ballast, stopping, **patting his coat pockets for something that isn't there.** He was supposed to be bringing something home. He can't remember what and he can't stop checking.
- **The lethal window is a train on approach** — roughly ninety seconds, twice a week. Otherwise he's dread. The freight timetable is public and a two-TP investigation converts directly into survival.
