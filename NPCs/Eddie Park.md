# Eddie Park

Up: [[Cast & NPCs]]

**15. The kid who chose his faith himself, defended it, spent it all trying to save one man — and lost. Now he's at [[The Community Center]] making himself useful, and he has stopped believing anything on purpose. [[Gern]]'s foil, and the purest *cast that worked and won't come off* case in the roster.**

> [!danger] DM-only
> This note **supersedes** the old "lonely brilliance library kid" draft entirely. The Mercer-annex tie, the Sam-cousin dependency, and the [[Episode — The Mirror]] slot are all dissolved. His recruit nature and his lever are secret; to the table he's a quiet older kid who volunteers at the Center.

## The foil axis

Not faith vs. no faith. **Gern has conviction and is starving for belonging. Eddie has belonging and has lost his conviction.** Each holds exactly what the other needs, which means each one's presence is a quiet accusation.

| | [[Gern]] | Eddie |
|---|---|---|
| Origin of faith | Imposed before he could answer | **Chose it himself** — the devout one in a lukewarm family |
| The world's verdict | Keeps confirming it | Refuted it, specifically and cruelly |
| Social result | Right, and dismissed | Doubting, and **smothered** — the church won't let him go |
| Volume | Got louder | Went quiet |

The second row is the sharp one. **Eddie already did the thing Gern's whole arc is aimed at** — chose a conviction, held it, defended it — **and it broke on him.** He is the living counterargument to Gern's destination, not a mirror-image gimmick.

> [!note] Reconciling with the reserved "zealot foil"
> [[Gern]]'s note reserves *"a Wesley-orbit zealot — the kid who took the Order-belonging seat and became a true believer."* That is Eddie's **destination**, not his starting state. They cross twice: Gern's conviction surges now while Eddie's collapses; later, when Gern hits his destructive-rebellion phase and dumps conviction wholesale, Eddie will have locked in at the Center. Gern ends with a conviction he **chose**; Eddie ends with one he was **handed**, and never notices.

## Profile

15. Mother died of cancer in 1993, when he was 12. Grace Tabernacle carried the family through it, and one elder in particular — **[[Lowell Brandt]]** — took Eddie under his wing.

**Eddie's faith is genuinely born from a rescue.** A real, good, temporary cast, working exactly as designed ([[The Cast and the Permanent Cast]]). He didn't inherit it; he ran toward it, harder than his own family did.

His father is emotionally absent — went through the motions at church for the family's sake and quietly stopped. He isn't disappointed in Eddie's doubt; he's **relieved**, which is somehow worse. Nobody at home will fight him about it. His crisis is invisible at home and unacceptable at church.

## What broke him

In spring 1995 [[Lowell Brandt|Lowell]] started emptying out. Nobody could say why; doctors found nothing. Eddie read it as a spiritual condition and **fought for him for months** — sat with him, prayed over him, did everything a fourteen-year-old could do. He also **echoed the congregation's line at him** — gently, but he echoed it, because he was still the devout one and he believed it.

Then Lowell died in a stupid accident at a rail crossing.

He was hollowed. **[[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] took him down.** Eddie has no idea, and won't for a long time.

Over the following year Eddie's faith didn't snap — it drained. By summer 1996 he's at the Center.

### The guilt (three layers, peel in order)

1. **Surface, and it's the misdirection:** *the church made him worse — the sessions, the shame, "you must have opened a door." I should have gotten him out of there sooner.* Eddie will say this if pushed.
2. **The self-indictment:** he was one of the people pushing from the Hollis side. He should have pushed Lowell *toward* the Center instead of pulling him back. **(Which, ironically, would not have helped him at all.)**
3. **Private, and only for a party that really earns it:** **he stopped visiting near the end.** Missed the last two weeks. Nothing structural — just a fifteen-year-old who couldn't take it anymore.

### He is also the exoneration

Pushed for feelings, Eddie's account damns [[Reverend Hollis|Hollis]]. Asked for a **careful chronology** — dates, not feelings — his own testimony collapses the Hollis theory: **Lowell was already emptying before the deliverance sessions started.** Eddie's timeline is compressed by grief; the sessions came *after* the change.

> [!note] This is the episode's best structural trick
> The exoneration only surfaces if someone treats Eddie as a **competent source** rather than a grieving kid — which is identical to his saving-grace move. Decency is rewarded with the truth. Mine him, minister at him, or let [[Gern]] proselytise at him, and the party keeps the wrong theory *and* pushes him further toward [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]. The misdirection persists in exact proportion to how badly they treat him.

## The lever (build key: [[The Cast and the Permanent Cast]])

**Pressure-point.** Eddie's conclusion isn't *"God isn't real."* It's ***"I was completely certain and completely wrong, so my judgment about what's true is worthless."***

[[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s pitch is devastating for that specific wound: **"Don't believe anything. Just be useful. Show up, help people, and let me handle the why."**

That's the permanent cast in its purest form — **relief from the burden of deciding what's true, sold as humility.** Eddie doesn't join a religion. He takes a job that means he never has to have one again. Three months of not-deciding later, Wesley has quietly filled the vacuum, and Eddie is the truest believer in town **precisely because he thinks he's immune.** His Paladin oath, when it lands, is sworn to a *person* rather than a principle.

*(Mechanically he is pre-oath through Act 1. The class comes online when the oath does.)*

**Saving-grace.** Everyone in Eddie's life either **scolds** the doubt ([[Reverend Hollis|Hollis]]: *doubt is a test, pray harder*) or **bypasses** it (Wesley: *you don't need to answer that*). Nobody offers the third thing: ***"Okay — so what do you actually think is true? Let's find out."***

Treating his doubt as legitimate intellectual work rather than damage. [[Old Man]]'s natural register, incidentally — and a rare non-Gern route into him.

**Wesley's counter-cultivation** (after Ep3): *"They used you."* And if the party pressed him hard for information — *"They made you dig him up."*

## The Gern collision (build the episode around this)

[[Gern]] is the only person in Mercer Falls who can offer Eddie **evidence** — a scripture that keeps coming true, corroborated in front of witnesses. Eddie is a kid who left a faith because it *never* came true.

**Gern will go for it.** He's starving for anyone to take him seriously, and Eddie is the first person who might.

And if he does, **Gern is doing to Eddie exactly what Wesley does** — handing certainty to someone too burned to carry the weight of deciding. Gern would even be *right* about the phenomena. He'd just be wrong about the frame, and he'd be recruiting for [[Order and Chaos|Order]] without knowing it.

**Gern as a competing Wesley**, discovered by the player from the inside, is the sharpest available way to teach that character that being right is not the same as discernment. Whether the player takes the bait is genuine drama, not a railroad.

*Small gift:* Grace Tabernacle would consider [[Certaintism]] a cult, so Eddie's first read on Gern is *"oh, you're one of* those *people"* — and then the book checks out.

## Negotiation profile (DM-only)

- **Motivations (Interest up):** **Being useful to someone actually suffering** (his real gift, and it's genuine — he did the hospital visits and the shut-in visits at fifteen); **honest reckoning** — someone willing to engage the question instead of soothing it; **the truth about what happened to [[Lowell Brandt|Lowell]]**.
- **Pitfalls (Interest down):** **Being ministered to** — prayed over, comforted, handled; it's the shape of every failure in his life. **Cheap certainty** — anyone who tells him what's true too fast *(a live trap for Gern's player)*. Anyone saying Lowell is in a better place.
- **Reachable through:** treating his doubt as work, not damage; letting him be **the expert on the dead man** rather than the grieving kid.
