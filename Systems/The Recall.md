# The Recall

Up: [[Systems]]

**Everything the dungeon makes is on loan. When it dies, it goes back — as light, going up, the way hollowing looks. Outside, this happens overnight and nobody ever sees it. Inside, it happens with the [[Floor Reset and the Intelligence Gradient|reset]], and it *is* the reset.**

> [!note] Why this rule exists
> The party will keep killing dungeon-natives **aboveground**, because that's where the life-or-death stakes are. Without this rule, every such fight leaves an impossible corpse in the woods that somebody has to deal with. The Recall removes corpse-logistics from the campaign permanently, in one clause — and turns the cleanup into a clue instead of a chore.

## The two clauses

| Where | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Inside the dungeon** | The corpse **stays.** Search it, loot it, hide behind it. It is reclaimed with the floor **when the party leaves.** |
| **Outside** | **Gone by the next sunrise.** No mark, no smell, no depression in the grass. |

**Nothing is recalled that the dungeon didn't make:**

- Items a native was carrying that came from a person (the not-bone tooth from [[Episode 2 — The Hush]] stays).
- Human remains. **A person killed by a monster in the woods is still there in the morning.** The dungeon reclaims its property; it does not do you favours.
- Blood, damage, wreckage, and anything the *party* did. See the persistence rule below.

> [!danger] The reset fires on EXIT, not on a clock
> Established in play, [[Session 6 — Second Shift]]. The party looped back through Floor 1 via the [[The Bell Junction|Bell Junction]] portal without ever surfacing, and **nothing had reset** — the [[The Octagonal Vestibule|Vestibule]]'s collapsed spokes were still collapsed, and they had to go the long way.
>
> **A party that stays down faces a persistent, degrading dungeon.** Consumed resources stay consumed, collapsed routes stay collapsed, cleared rooms stay cleared, corpses stay where they fell.
>
> **Repeated short delves and one long delve are now strategically different things**, and the difference is entirely in the players' hands. A short delve buys a fresh, fully-stocked floor; a long one buys ground you've already paid for. Neither is correct.

## The overnight clock

Deliberately **not** instant, and deliberately long.

**A two-minute recall would be plumbing.** An overnight one is an **evidence clock:**

- **Proof that expires.** Kill something at nine at night and you have until dawn. Long enough to drag it somewhere, photograph it, wake a parent, get a deputy out — which stages the best possible [[The Reality Filter|Filter]] scene: an adult standing over a dead impossible thing, finding a way not to see it.
- **Photographs outlive the subject.** The body goes; the Polaroid stays. The party accumulates pictures of things that no longer exist anywhere — the exact epistemic position this campaign is about.
- **A daytime kill lingers all day.** Eight hours of exposure to manage.

**If someone stakes it out, let them see it.** It costs a whole night and nobody will do it twice. What they get: the outline going soft, the light going up thin and cold like breath in January, then dark — and no way whatsoever to prove it.

## Two systems, one job each

> **The Recall handles the object. [[The Reality Filter]] handles the memory.**

Neither has to do the other's work, which is why both can stay simple. The Filter's job shrinks to covering *witnesses*. **If the party works that out, it's a real insight** — reward it.

## What persists through a reset

Established at the table in [[Session 5 — Second Shift]], where the party ran a deliberate methodology on this question.

**The rule: the dungeon restores its own inventory. It does not touch yours, and it does not repair what you break.**

| | Through a reset |
|---|---|
| Monsters it spawned | **Back.** Same roster, same rooms |
| Their corpses | **Reclaimed** |
| Traps it built | **Re-armed.** Even ones you solved |
| Set dressing | **Restored.** The coffee is still steaming; the ink is still wet |
| **Items you took** | **Still gone.** Confirmed — the journal never came back |
| **Damage you did** | **Stays.** Tank's hammer-hole in the [[The Cataloguing Office\|Cataloguing Office]] wall |
| **Marks you left** | **Stay.** Bash's inked *TANK WUZ HEAR* under the desk |
| **[[Wesley's Sigils\|Wesley's chalk]]** | **Stays** — including the **dead white chalk** in [[The Buzzsaw Room\|the Millrace]], confirmed [[Session 6 — Second Shift\|Session 6]] |
| **Room geometry** | **Not guaranteed.** The [[The Octagonal Vestibule\|Vestibule]] went from one door to eight between visits |

> [!danger] The payoff the party can derive unaided
> **This is why Wesley's chalk survived the reset in Session 1.** The tally marks and glyphs are not dungeon features — they're a *person's* marks, persisting exactly the way the party's own graffiti now will.
>
> And as of Session 6 they have the second half: **the Millrace sigil is white chalk that no longer glows**, while the one through the archway is lit green. Same hand, one dead, one live, **a week apart.** That's "someone else is active down there and it started recently," sitting on the floor in front of them. **Do not connect it for them.**

**The tension that keeps it honest: their marks last, the map doesn't.** They can tag a room permanently and find it rearranged.

**Downstream:** the party leaves an accumulating scar trail. Sooner or later **Wesley walks past a hole in a wall that says TANK WUZ HEAR.**

## Consequences already in play

- **[[The Hush]]'s corpse is gone.** Killed in a cave a mile northwest of the mill; gone by morning. Nothing there now.
- **Which is why [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]] doubts.** The only evidence it existed is a Polaroid, and photographs are easy to talk yourself out of.
- **The Millrace has now killed two natives by geography** (a wolf, then a hyena, both into the water). Their bodies are in the river and will go with the floor. Players may start treating the room as a weapon.

## Open dials

- Does a *recalled* native leave residue? Proposed: no — hollowing spends a person's agency; a recall is the dungeon collecting its own, and collects cleanly.
- Whether anything can **prevent** a recall. Great Act-2 objective if the party ever needs a body to persist.
- What happens to a native killed outside that is **carried into** the dungeon before dawn. Undefined; the party hasn't thought of it.
- **Whether a very long delve degrades the floor past viability** — no food, no water, every route spent. Currently just a pressure; could become a genuine failure state.
