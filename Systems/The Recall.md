# The Recall

Up: [[Systems]]

**Everything the dungeon makes is on loan. When it dies, it goes back — as light, going up, the way hollowing looks. Outside, this happens overnight and nobody ever sees it. Inside, it happens with the [[Floor Reset and the Intelligence Gradient|reset]], and it *is* the reset.**

> [!note] Why this rule exists
> The party will keep killing dungeon-natives **aboveground**, because that's where the life-or-death stakes are ([[Hollowing and Agency-Loss|inside, death is hollowing]]). Without this rule, every such fight leaves an impossible corpse in the woods that somebody has to deal with. The Recall removes corpse-logistics from the campaign permanently, in one clause — and turns the cleanup into a clue instead of a chore.

## The two clauses

| Where | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Inside the dungeon** | The corpse **stays.** Search it, loot it, hide behind it, use it as cover. It is reclaimed with the floor when the party leaves. **The recall is the reset.** |
| **Outside** | **Gone by the next sunrise.** No mark, no smell, no depression in the grass. |

**Nothing is recalled that the dungeon didn't make.** This is the whole exception list:

- Items a native was carrying that came from a person (the not-bone tooth from [[Episode 2 — The Hush]] is an artifact in its own right and stays).
- Human remains. **A person killed by a monster in the woods is still there in the morning.** The dungeon reclaims its property; it does not do you favours.
- Blood, damage, wreckage, and anything the *party* did. See the persistence rule below.

## The overnight clock

Deliberately **not** instant, and deliberately long.

**A two-minute recall would be plumbing** — nobody would ever observe it, and it would only ever save the DM bookkeeping. An overnight one is an **evidence clock**, and that's worth much more:

- **Proof that expires.** Kill something at nine at night and you have until dawn. Long enough to drag it somewhere, photograph it, wake a parent, get a deputy out — which stages the best possible [[The Reality Filter|Filter]] scene: an adult standing over a dead impossible thing, finding a way not to see it.
- **Photographs outlive the subject.** The body goes; the Polaroid stays. The party accumulates a folder of pictures of things that no longer exist anywhere, which is the exact epistemic position this campaign is about.
- **A daytime kill lingers all day.** Eight hours of exposure the party has to manage. Makes daylight fights outside feel structurally different from night ones without any new rules.

**If someone stakes it out, let them see it.** It costs a whole night and nobody will do it twice. What they get: the outline going soft, the light going up thin and cold like breath in January, then dark — and no way whatsoever to prove it, because now there isn't even a body.

## Two systems, one job each

> **The Recall handles the object. [[The Reality Filter]] handles the memory.**

Neither has to do the other's work, which is why both can stay simple. The Filter's job shrinks to covering *witnesses*, which is a far smaller lift than explaining away physical remains. **If the party works that out, it's a real insight** — reward it.

## What persists through a reset — the party's own experiment

Established at the table in [[Session 5 — Second Shift]], where the party ran a deliberate methodology on this exact question.

**The rule: the dungeon restores its own inventory. It does not touch yours, and it does not repair what you break.**

| | Through a reset |
|---|---|
| Monsters it spawned | **Back.** Same roster, same rooms |
| Their corpses | **Reclaimed** (the Recall, inside) |
| Traps it built | **Re-armed.** Even ones you solved |
| Set dressing | **Restored.** The coffee is still steaming; the ink is still wet |
| **Items you took** | **Still gone.** Confirmed in play — the Buzzsaw journal never came back |
| **Damage you did** | **Stays.** Tank's hammer-hole in the [[The Cataloguing Office\|Cataloguing Office]] wall |
| **Marks you left** | **Stay.** Bash's inked *TANK WUZ HEAR* under the desk |
| **Room geometry** | **Not guaranteed.** The [[The Octagonal Vestibule\|Vestibule]] went from one door to eight between visits |

> [!danger] The payoff the party can derive unaided
> **This is why [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s chalk survived the reset in Session 1.** The tally marks and glyphs they've been following are not dungeon features — they're a *person's* marks, persisting exactly the way the party's own graffiti now will, laid down across multiple resets by someone who has been coming back.
>
> That's the "someone else is down here on a schedule" beat delivered by the players' own experiment instead of by exposition. **Do not connect it for them.**

**The tension that keeps it honest: their marks last, the map doesn't.** They can tag a room permanently and find it rearranged. Mapping is possible and unreliable at the same time, which is the correct texture for a roguelike megadungeon.

**Downstream:** the party now leaves an accumulating scar trail. Sooner or later **Wesley walks past a hole in a wall that says TANK WUZ HEAR** and learns he isn't alone down there. That thread is live from Session 5 onward and costs nothing to hold.

## Consequences already in play

- **[[The Hush]]'s corpse is gone.** Killed in a cave a mile northwest of the mill; gone by the following morning. If the party goes back there is nothing — not a mark, not a smell.
- **Which is why [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]] doubts.** He watched them kill it and the next day there was nothing in that cave. **The only evidence it ever existed is a Polaroid**, and photographs are easy to talk yourself out of. His drift now has a mechanical cause, not just a character note — and it sharpens the hook for whoever eventually tells him *"no, it was real."*

## Open dials

- Does a *recalled* native leave residue, the way a hollowed person leaves a shimmer? Proposed: no — hollowing spends a person's agency; a recall is the dungeon collecting its own, and collects cleanly.
- Whether anything can **prevent** a recall (a containment, a ward, a sufficiently cold room). Would make a great Act-2 objective if the party ever needs a body to persist.
- What happens to a native killed outside that is **carried into** the dungeon before dawn. Currently undefined; the party has not thought of it yet.
