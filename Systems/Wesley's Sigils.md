# Wesley's Sigils

Up: [[Game Systems]]

**[[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s anti-ember relic grants a class of inscribed sigil abilities that scale as he integrates further with [[The Dungeon|the dungeon]] — i.e., as he is consumed by it. To the PCs these read as Wesley *mastering* the dungeon; the truth is the boundary between Wesley and dungeon thinning.** Everything here is DM-only.

Each sigil requires **inscription** (drawn with dungeon-extracted chalk/ink-equivalents; takes time; visible once placed). **PCs cannot use sigils** — the relic is the prerequisite, and even capturing Wesley's notebook leaves the inscriptions inert in PC hands.

> [!danger] Locked rule — one active exit sigil at a time
> **Wesley can only have one live exit sigil in the dungeon.** Drawing a new one **renders the previous one inert.** He does not maintain a network; he maintains a *position*, and moving it is a real decision with real cost.
>
> **This is load-bearing for the Act-1 timeline:**
> - Session 1, the party watched him trigger his exit sigil in [[The Buzzsaw Room]] and vanish.
> - **Within a day** he re-entered through it, pushed deeper, and drew a new one in [[The Spiral Stair Room]] (Room 8). **The Buzzsaw sigil went inert at that moment.**
> - The toll for the new one is [[The Siren]] and her retinue — which is what starts the sleepwalking in [[Episode 3 — Second Shift]], about a week before that episode opens.
>
> **Open question worth deciding:** did he move it *because* the party saw it? If yes, the causal chain is real — they went down, he relocated, the toll landed in the shaft, the town started walking. Their guilt in Ep3 would be **warranted but misattributed**, and they'd be right by accident.
>
> **What's left behind.** An inert sigil is not erased. Undecided, and it matters — the party stood in the Buzzsaw Room in [[Session 5 — Second Shift]] and nobody looked. Options: scuffed/faded chalk that no longer holds green; a clean dead outline; or nothing at all because he wiped it. **Pick before they go back.**

## Level 1 — Real Sigils (active from Session 1)

Wesley pre-inscribes a sigil in a chamber; activation is fast (seconds).

- **Exit:** Wesley walks through any door in the room and wakes at home, fully restored. The exit-side stays active **1–2 minutes** — long enough that an astute PC can ride the same door out (lands them in their own bed normally).
- **Cost:** monsters spawn in the room and **persist** — they don't dissipate on their own; they stay bound to the sigil until defeated, at which point the sigil dissipates.
- **Re-entry:** Wesley can return to the sigil's location **once**, from a paired private site (a back room at [[The Community Center|the community center]] locked to the public). Once used for re-entry, the sigil is spent and can be redrawn elsewhere.

**Cat-and-mouse:** PCs who find a real sigil can clear it — defeating the bound monsters dissipates it and forces Wesley to redraw, costing him significant delve progress. Each delve is a strategic choice: push deeper, or hunt sigils to deny Wesley his position. High-risk (every active sigil is a tough encounter) but a successful clear is one of the highest-leverage things the PCs can do.

**Given the one-at-a-time rule, a clear is worse for him than it looks** — it doesn't cost him a node in a network, it costs him *his only foothold*, and he has to delve from the entrance to re-establish one.

> [!note] The chalk survives resets
> Per [[The Recall]], the dungeon restores its own inventory and does not touch marks left by visitors. **Wesley's chalk, tallies and cartography persist across resets** — which is why the party could follow them in Session 1, and why their own graffiti experiment in [[Session 5 — Second Shift]] will eventually prove to them that those marks belong to a *person* crossing multiple resets. **Do not connect it for them.**
>
> Corollary: **the party is leaving marks too**, and Wesley will eventually walk past a hole in a wall that says TANK WUZ HEAR.

## Level 2 — Decoy Sigils (unlocked mid-Act 1, Week 2–3)

A *different spell*; visually similar but subtly distinct on close reading.

- **Activation:** proximity-triggered (not pre-emptive); summons monsters as a real sigil does.
- **No exit, no re-entry.** The decoy only baits PCs into burning resources on a "sigil" that accomplishes nothing — Wesley loses no progress; the PCs lose HP, slots, consumables.
- **Decoys do not count against the one-active-exit limit.** He can maintain several.

The first decoy-clear is a significant beat: it tells the PCs Wesley is *gaming the meta*, not just defending positions.

## Detection Paths (distinguishing decoy from real)

Should be hard, especially at Levels 2–3:

- **Visual pattern recognition** over many encounters (unreliable until ~4–5 mixed-type sigils cleared)
- **Glyph-language decoding** (see [[Forensic Delving]])
- **Forensic deduction** — Wesley's chalk-cartography around real sigils differs from around decoys
- **A captured notebook** resolves the question instantly — highly-prized loot

## Anti-Embers as an Item Type

Sigils flow from the **anti-ember relic** — the Order-side mirror of the PCs' embers. Wesley's relic is ~100% Order (see [[The Embers]] / [[Order and Chaos]]). The equinox produces anti-embers for the [[Wesley's Recruits|Rivals]]: failure-path Evil Party anti-embers are pure-Order; success-path Rival Party embers are forced 80/20 Order/Chaos by PC interference. Future Acts may let other anti-ember bearers use Wesley's sigils (*Shared Sigil* below).

## Banked Abilities (provisional — Acts 2–3, not committed)

Pattern: early = *drawing* → middle = *mapping/shaping* → late = *summoning* → endgame = *being*.

- **Trapdoor Sigil** — exit lands in a different room than inscription.
- **Shared Sigil** — other anti-ember bearers can use his sigils.
- **Silent Sigil** — exit doesn't summon monsters (the dungeon stops charging the toll).
- **Second Anchor** — *(new)* a second simultaneous exit sigil. **The obvious first upgrade given the one-at-a-time constraint**, and it lands hardest right after the party has finally learned to hunt them.
- **Bound Sentinel** — binds one specific monster as a guard instead of a wave.
- **Floor Sculpting** — rearrange room contents / move walls on cleared floors (see [[Floor Reset and the Intelligence Gradient]]).
- **Outflow** — extend dungeon effects (apathy waves, Filter intensification) into specific places in town.

## The Surfeit Counter

> [!danger] DM-only — the hidden cost of saving the PCs
> Every ember-bearer hollowing ticks a behind-the-scenes counter (**Surfeit**). [[The Entity|The entity]] pays the dungeon from its own substance to spare the PCs, and that payment accrues. **At thresholds the dungeon becomes incrementally stronger — Wesley unlocks new sigil-class abilities, the [[The Reality Filter|Filter]] strengthens, the entity's interventions strain.** The PCs don't see the counter; they may notice that bad streaks of delves make the dungeon push back harder.
>
> **Running total worth tracking:** Ep1 hollowed [[Tank]] and [[Old Man]]; Ep3 will hollow some or all of the party again. That's a real tick, and *Second Anchor* is a natural thing to unlock off it.
