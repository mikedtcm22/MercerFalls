# Floor Reset and the Intelligence Gradient

Up: [[Game Systems]]

**The dungeon replenishes daily between delves — and *appears* to get smarter the deeper you go. The replenishment is intrinsic to the machine; the "intelligence" is not. It's [[Wesley Crane]]'s depth-scaled manipulation, the single biggest hidden fact about [[The Dungeon]].**

## Daily Replenishment (intrinsic)

> [!warning] Revised 2026-10-10 — the dungeon replenishes, it doesn't reset
> The machine does not wipe a floor back to a saved state. It **refills what was killed and re-arms what was sprung**, and it leaves the living alone. Anything still alive at the end of a delve is still there next time — wherever it ended up, with whatever it learned, and **remembering the PCs.** Only a creature that **dies** comes back in its original state, with no memory.

The dungeon replenishes daily, between delves. This is intrinsic — [[Order and Chaos|Order]] built the machine with an automatic nightly refresh that runs **whether Wesley is active or not.** PCs entering on Day N find every floor replenished: the dead back in their places, traps and mechanisms re-armed, and the living exactly where they left off.

### What the machine does overnight

- **Refills the dead.** A monster killed on a prior delve is back at its original placement and state, with no memory of how it died.
- **Re-arms its fixtures.** Traps, locks, and room mechanisms return to their armed state. Floor 2's gremlin-built rigs fall under this — the ferry re-tied to the sluice winch in [[The Wash]], the outflow re-choked, [[The Door Machine]] re-stripped.
- **Clears traces** — see *What does NOT persist*, below.

### What it leaves alone: the living

- **Survivors persist.** A creature alive at the end of a delve keeps its position, its knowledge, its grudges, and its memory of the PCs.
- **Time heals them, not the machine.** Wounds, spent spell slots, and ability cooldowns recover through ordinary rest across the gap between delves. A survivor comes back healthy because a day has passed, not because the dungeon restored it.
- **Death is the only reset.** Kill a survivor and the next replenishment brings back a fresh one — a stranger.
- **Example:** the [[The Gremlins|mogwai]] on Floor 2. Freed and alive, he stays free between delves and remembers the party. If he dies, he's replenished back in his cage in [[The Warren Wall]].

**What persists** (rule of thumb: *chalk and glow persist; footprints and blood are gone*):

- [[Wesley's Sigils|Wesley's sigils]] (real and decoy)
- Wesley's chalk-cartography
- Glyphs on the architecture itself
- **[[Nullprints|Nullprints]]** — see the disambiguation below
- Residue (semi-physical agency-substance; fades on its own hours-to-days timeline regardless of replenishment — see [[Forensic Delving]])
- Found keystones (carried by PCs — see [[The Keystone Tablet]])
- **Writing left in [[The Cataloguing Office]]** — its ledgers, and anything a visitor writes and leaves there *(DM ruling 2026-10-01; see below)*
- **Living creatures, and what they remember** *(DM ruling 2026-10-10; see above)*

**What does NOT persist:** drag marks, blood trails, residue-ash and scorch; spent supplies (torches, flares, wrappers); dropped personal effects and tools; monster corpses (and loot on them — **loot during the delve**).

> [!note] Nullprints are not ash — disambiguation
> Three different marks get loosely called "the stain," and only one of them survives the night.
> - **Residue** — semi-physical agency-substance hanging in the chamber. Fades hours-to-days on its own clock. Forensic gold; short-lived.
> - **Residue-ash / scorch** (e.g. the ash-rings around the [[The Buzzsaw Room|Buzzsaw]] platform) — the *smoke*, physical soot. **Cleared by replenishment**, like blood and drag-marks.
> - **[[Nullprints|The nullprint]]** — the machine's ledger entry, seared into the stone underneath. **Not cleared by replenishment.** It persists for months or years, and it fades only because **the dungeon is slowly eating it.** This is why [[Wendell Pace]]'s mark is still in [[The Pillared Reach]] and [[Lowell Brandt]]'s trail is still readable a year on.
>
> A dropped **notebook or personal effect does not persist** — which is why nothing a delver caches below survives the night, and why everything [[Lowell Brandt|Lowell]] ever brought out came home in his pockets instead. *(This is why the party had to pick up [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s notebook the night they found it — [[Session 8 — Second Shift]].)*
>
> **Exception — the Cataloguing Office.** Paperwork *in the office* is the office's own record and stays: the century-old ledgers, and [[Old Man]]'s note to "the Archivist." If the office wiped its paper, nothing in those ledgers would have survived. The exception is about the office as an archive, not a general cache — a notebook dropped in a corridor still goes.

The daily replenishment operates independently of TPK — a TPK never compounds into the next day's dungeon (see [[Death, TPK, and the Lost Day]]).

## The Floor Intelligence Gradient

Layered on top of the replenishment. The dungeon seems to get smarter deeper down.

> [!danger] DM-only — it isn't getting smarter; it's Wesley
> The dungeon is a machine, not a mind. **Wesley has more sigil-class manipulation tools available at depth, where he is more deeply integrated with the dungeon.** The gradient describes the *style* of daily replenishment at each depth — what shape it takes — and each tier maps to an ability Wesley has unlocked.

| Floors | Apparent behavior | Underlying mechanic (Wesley) |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Static. Identical layout + monster placements on replenishment. | Can re-summon monsters but not rearrange them. |
| 3–5 | Monster shuffling. Layout same; which monsters spawn varies. | Can re-summon monsters of his choosing into chambers. |
| 6–9 | Room contents shuffle. A trap room may be empty, monstered, or treasured next time; layout stable. | Can rearrange chamber contents — move monsters, add traps, hide loot. |
| 10+ | Full reshuffles. The layout itself changes; mapping starts fresh each delve. | Deeply integrated — can move walls, restructure pathways. By now he's closer to the dungeon than to himself. |

> [!note] The gradient under replenishment
> "Static" on Floors 1–2 means the *replenished* return to identical placements. Survivors are wherever they ended up. *(Open: whether Wesley's deeper-tier rearranging can move survivors, or only what he replenishes.)*

Floor 1 is a knowable place; Floor 12 is a different dungeon every time — and **both facts are about Wesley, not the dungeon.** When the PCs work this out (late Act 2 at earliest), the entire dungeon retroactively becomes an extension of their antagonist: they've been crawling around inside Wesley all along.

> [!note] Floor 1 applied — the Pit Room Chief (Session 8; revised 2026-10-10)
> The [[The Pit Room|Pit Room]] Chief survived the party's poisoned stew. He's back at full HP next delve because a day's rest heals him, not because the dungeon restored him — **and because he survived, he remembers** the "mutinous dogman" who poisoned him. *(Supersedes the 2026-10-01 reading that he returns with no memory and that the disguise-and-stew approach meets a fresh Chief every time.)* Kill him, and the next replenishment brings back a Chief who has never met them.

## The Wesley-Status Forensic Signal

> [!danger] DM-only — tracking Wesley without seeing him
> When Wesley is **active**, the gradient operates as designed (shuffling on 3+, contents on 6+, layouts on 10+). When Wesley is **incapacitated** (injured, away, captured), the gradient-shuffle **stops** — the intrinsic daily replenishment continues, but deep floors come back *static*. Attentive PCs may notice: "Floor 5 is the same configuration today as yesterday — that shouldn't happen." This is the **late-Act-1 forensic handle** for tracking Wesley's status indirectly (pairs with the residue and sigil signals in [[Forensic Delving]]).
