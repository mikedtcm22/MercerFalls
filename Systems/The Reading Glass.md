# The Reading Glass

Up: [[Gear and Items]]

**A hand lens found in [[The Debrief Station|Mercer's cabin]]. Ask it a question about the past of a thing and it shows you a scene that answers — but first it names a price, in your own handwriting, and the price is always a memory. It is the DM's lifeline for a stalled table, and it is the object that will eventually tell [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] the truth about his Window.**

> [!danger] In play — passed around the whole party (as of [[Session 9 — The Crossing Remembers|Session 9]])
> **Found [[Session 8 — Second Shift|Session 8]] in the cabin's curio cabinet** (Gern, Investigation 18 with Tank helping). The DM sent **Kenny the item text privately as player knowledge.**
>
> **Session 9:** Gern asked the Glass about itself, then **explained it openly to the party** — the Session 8 conceal-or-not dial is resolved: *not concealing.* His gloss: *"You lose certainty, replaced with doubt."* All four PCs have now held it; it moves from hand to hand.

> # THE READING GLASS
> *Wondrous item, rare*
>
> A broad round lens in a ring of dark brass. Two grips lie folded flat across the glass; swing them out on their hinges and they lock at either side of the rim, so it is held in both hands, at arm's length, the way you'd hold a letter up to the light. The grips are worn smooth.
>
> ---
>
> **ACTION.** Once per long rest, hold it to an object, a place, or a mark, and ask **one question about its past.**
>
> **THE PRICE.** The far side of the lens fogs, and a fact you currently know appears in it, **in your own handwriting.** Only you can read it.
>
> **You choose whether to pay.** Decline and the fog clears — nothing lost, nothing gained, **and the use is not spent.** **The price for that question, on that object, never changes.** Come back in a month and it is the same price.
>
> **A narrower question costs less.** *"What is this sigil for"* is expensive. *"Was the hand that drew it left or right"* is not.
>
> **IF YOU PAY.** The fact leaves you. **It cannot be relearned.** Told it directly, you hear it, agree pleasantly, and do not retain it. Shown proof, you set the proof down and change the subject.
>
> **THE ANSWER.** You watch a scene from the past, chosen to answer what you asked. Colour, sound, the smell of the room. **You are present in it.** It runs its length and releases you, and **no time passes for anyone else.**
>
> ---
>
> **WHEN THE GAP SHOWS.** If you try to act or speak in a way that depends on what you paid, you falter mid-word. **Do something else instead, freely.** Press on and the glass takes it again: **2d6 psychic damage**, and you still cannot produce the thing.

## Table rulings (Session 9) — all endorsed

> [!important] The Glass is meant to be used, and to cause chaos
> Kids passing round a mildly cursed object is exactly the intended play. These rulings stand.

- **Once per *person* per long rest.** Four charges a day for the party, not one.
- **Shopping is the point.** Pass it round until someone has a price they'll pay. **Prices are fixed per holder, per question, per object** — a different holder gets their own price for the same question.
- **Say it, then pay.** A holder may announce the fact aloud before accepting; only the payer loses it, and the party keeps it.
- **What follows for pricing:** anything the group can hold for the payer is cheap. The knife lives in what each kid is willing to give up — promises, kindnesses, private feelings, a route only they walked. **Use the falter.**
- **Tank treats every price as free** ("I don't even remember that anyway"). Price him with promises.

## What it's for

> [!danger] This is a DM tool wearing an item's clothes
> **It exists so that a stalled table has a way out that doesn't feel like a handout.** When the clues aren't landing, the glass is sitting in a backpack, and using it is the party's decision rather than your intervention.
>
> **The dosage dial is the price, and you set it.** A small nudge costs something small. A campaign-turning answer costs something that hurts. You are never obliged to hand over more than the table needs, because **the answer is exactly as wide as the question.**
>
> *(With Kenny holding the rules, the lifeline is in the hands of the player most likely to actually reach for it.)*

## Naming a price

**The price is whatever twists the knife best.** There's no category restriction — pick from anywhere in the character's head.

**Rough tiers, escalating:**

| Weight | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Small** | A room's layout. Which door the arrow trap was behind. That the coffee in the Cataloguing Office is always steaming |
| **Real** | The reset rules they worked out themselves. The route to the Bell Junction. What the tally marks look like |
| **Sharp** | A fact about someone — that [[Ren Sołtys\|Ren]] keeps a notebook. That [[Dominique Ferraro\|Dominique]] works at the Historical Society. That [[Wendell Pace\|Wendell]] was the man on the catwalk |
| **Cruel** | A promise made to them, or by them. A kindness someone did. **What [[Wesley Crane\|Wesley]]'s face looked like in the Buzzsaw Room** |

> [!note] The one to save
> **Forgetting the antagonist's face** is the best single price in the campaign and should be spent on the biggest answer the party ever buys.

### What never to take

- **Class features, proficiencies, or anything on the sheet.** The glass takes facts, not capabilities.
- **Core identity and relationships.** Bash never forgets that Dominique is his cousin. Take *what she told him*, not *who she is.*
- **Anything that leaves a player unable to play their character.** The test: could they still sit down and roleplay this person? If not, price it lower.

**The sweet spot is load-bearing for *plot*, not for *self*.**

## Running it at the table

- **Only the holder reads the price.** It appears on the far side of the lens, in their handwriting, because it's theirs. The rest of the table sees someone go still and then either speak or not. *"What did it say?" — "...doesn't matter."* **Keep it isolating.**
- **Write each burned memory on a slip**, in the player's own words, kept at the table. The player still knows. The character does not.
- **Keep a short running list of each PC's knowledge**, so you can name a price without stalling. Useful anyway.
- **Shopping across holders is fine** (Session 9 ruling). A single holder still can't re-roll their own price. Declining is free, always available, and **doesn't spend that holder's use.**
- **The falter is the punishment, not the damage.** 2d6 at level 3 is a slap, not a threat, and it's meant to be payable repeatedly. The wall is the point.

## Where it was found

[[The Debrief Station]] — the cabin at the head of the shaft, reachable only by climbing out through the one-way hatch after the [[The Siren|Siren]] dies. **Undisturbed for a century.** [[Elias Mercer]] presumably used it for exactly what the party will: asking a place what happened in it. **As played, it sat in the cabin's curio cabinet**, among a strange skull, a lantern and other oddities.

**Session 9 — the Glass on itself:** Gern's vision showed **Elias Mercer showing it to a man in a chair, then shelving it in the cabinet.** *Open:* who the man in the chair was.

## The Bash payoff

> [!danger] He will recognise the feeling, not the object
> Not *"this is like my mirror"* — **"this is the same thing happening to me."** The exact quality of the vision, the way it arrives, the way it releases him. He'd know it anywhere.

**Three consequences, in order:**

**1. It confirms the Window is real**, which he has never had from anyone. And it hands him something nobody else has ever had: **a working, physical, examinable instance of the phenomenon.** He has been chasing a thing that only happens *to* him. Now there's one he can hold.

**2. It tells him the mirror is a made object.** This one plainly is — brass, ground glass, worn thumb-grips, somebody's hand. **The mirror is not a gift and not a curse. It's equipment.** Whoever made one made the other.

**3. It reframes what he has been doing to himself.** The glass charges a memory for a look backward. If the mirror works the same way — **and he has been using it for years without knowing there was a price** — then he has been paying all along and has no idea what with.

> [!danger] Leave this hanging. Never answer it directly.
> **What has Bash been spending?**

### Why he can't take it apart

**Three layers, and the third is the one that matters.**

**He can tell, and that's the point.** Artificer intuition, on a successful Arcana check: **the binding is in the whole, not the parts.** Separate frame from lens and he has a brass ring and a piece of ground glass. **He knows this before he tries**, so the constraint is his own competence rather than a DM wall — and he'll keep choosing it.

**Studying it means using it, and using it costs.** He can experiment, but every trial burns something the party needs, and he gets one per long rest. **They won't let him spend campaign knowledge on R&D**, he'll know they're right, and it will drive him quietly insane.

**And it isn't a mechanism.** No optics explain it. No linkage, no resonance, nothing that *does* anything. Eventually he works out why:

> **The glass does not choose the scene. Something on the other side chooses, and shows him.**

**You cannot build a correspondent.** Which is also why he will never engineer the Window — **because the Window was never his faculty either.** He has spent years believing he has an ability. He has been receiving.

**Hold that as long as you can stand to.**

### What it becomes later

When he finally "hacks" it into showing him [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] — **he hasn't hacked anything. He asked, and something answered.**

He will think he engineered it. He will be proud of it. And **the price the glass names for that question should be the most expensive thing on the slip all campaign** — and he will pay it, because it's about her.

> [!note] Not decided, and worth leaving open
> The cruellest available answer is that he pays with **something that would have let him understand what he was looking at.**
>
> Related, and equally undecided: the Window's content is *"something terrible happening to Dominique."* If the mirror has always charged the same way, the bleakest reading is that he has been buying visions of his cousin with **his capacity to read her** — which would explain why he cannot see what she is actually doing. **Don't commit. It costs nothing to leave the door open.**

### The Bash payoff — status after Session 9

- **Bash has held it twice and declined twice.** He has seen two prices and **no vision**, so *"that's the same thing happening to me"* is still fully loaded.
- **The mirror question is locked:** *"How do I make it do that again?"* costs **forgetting that Dominique knows about the place under the mill.** If he ever pays, he stops knowing she knows — so he stops telling her, leaves her at the threshold every time, and her ledger drains. **The Window would be feeding her fall.** Hold this gun.
- **Gern's own read is still the third layer.** An Order Domain cleric from a Certaintist house holding a Mercer object that sells true visions of the past for a piece of yourself is its own pressure on his faith, independent of Bash. See [[Gern]], [[Certaintism]].
