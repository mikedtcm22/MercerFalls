# The Hush-Hunting Graphic Novel

Up: [[Gear and Items]]

**_The Quiet Dark: Below_ — an obscure comic tie-in to the same B-movie [[Russ]] IDs the Hush from (*The Quiet Dark*, 1982). Where the film's teens run, the comic's survivors hunt the thing to its nest — so it's how the PCs learn to *track* a [[The Hush|Hush]] to its lair, the one thing Russ himself can't give them.**

## What it is
A slim, barely-printed comic adaptation that goes past the film: the characters stop running and go on the offensive, and the book lays out — in that lurid, over-explained tie-in way — **how you track a Hush to its den.** Russ knows the creature's *habits* (it takes people, ripens live prey, hunts by sound); he does **not** know how to find a *nest.* For that he points the kids here.

## The method it teaches (F4) — track it by the noise it *wants*
The comic's key trick, and the thing that makes it feel clever at the table: **you don't track a Hush by silence — you track it by excess noise.** Because it *sees* with its ears, a Hush cultivates a **loud** approach to its den — it wants prey and intruders to announce themselves. So it rakes drifts of **dry, crunchy leaf-litter and dead sticks** onto the trails toward the nest, scatters gravel and shale, clears away anything soft and muffling. You hunt it by moving **toward where the woods get *louder,* not quieter** — into the gauntlet of crunch — following prey drag-marks that converge through the noise-litter toward a resonant enclosed nest (a culvert, an old boiler) that carries every sound. (Nice dread-builder: the closer the kids get, the louder their own footsteps become, and they realize the forest floor has been *arranged* to give them away.)

## In play (Episode 2)
- **Payload:** the **den-tracking method** (F4) — turns a rough directional bearing from the [[The Mill|mill grounds]] / the nightly stalk into an actual hunt, so the party can locate the [[The Hush|Hush]]'s den (supports C5) and take the fight to it in the climax.
- **Also:** a little expanded, unreliable lore color — useful, but a *comic,* so the DM decides what's accurate.
