# The Modded Talkboy

Up: [[Gear and Items]]

**A 1993 Talkboy from the back of [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s closet with a powered speaker wired into the headphone jack. It plays recorded human speech at low speed, and that specific sound — nothing clever, just the wrong tape speed — is the only thing in Mercer Falls that reliably beats [[The Siren|the Siren]]'s song. Built [[Session 7 — Second Shift|Session 7]].**

## The item

> **THE MODDED TALKBOY**
> *Wondrous item · single-purpose · no attunement*
>
> **Action.** Until the start of your next turn, you and every creature within **30 feet** are **immune to the Siren's song** — her Charm, the ambient Chorus, and harpy Luring Song.
>
> **6 charges.** One per activation. **After the sixth use the tape snaps and the item is finished.** No recharge.
>
> **It works on this song and nothing else, ever.** It is not a general charm ward.

## Why it's balanced without further tuning

**The action cost sidelines whoever holds it.** One of four PCs does nothing else, every round they sustain it. That is the entire price, and it's steep enough on its own — the six charges are a hard stop on the fight's length, not the primary brake.

> [!note] The bubble is the tactical problem, not the limitation
> **Anyone the Siren carries above 30 feet is outside it.** Stay inside and the party is bunched up under a flying enemy; step out to go on offence and you're exposed.
>
> That tension is the whole reason the radius exists. Don't widen it.

## How they got here

**[[Ruth Bielak|Ruth]]'s cassette player** has a stretched belt and worn batteries, and for years it has played her dead husband's voice **slow**, every night, while she sleeps. She is the only previously-hollowed person inside the Siren's radius who has never walked.

**Content is irrelevant. Only speed matters, because speed is the frequency.** Her notch is occupied; nothing else can get in.

The chain, exactly as played:
1. Ren spots Ruth as the anomaly on her own notebook.
2. [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] gets the party through Ruth's front door.
3. Ruth plays the tape; the warble is audible.
4. **Old Man talks her into lending it** (natural 27 Persuasion) and **Bash mends it** — which **removes her protection.**
5. Old Man's Performance check connects slowed human speech to the song.
6. Bash remembers the Talkboy and wires a speaker to it.
7. **Field-tested on the walkers that night.** They come to their senses inside the radius and relapse outside it — which is how the party established the range themselves.

> [!danger] The cost, which they know about
> **Ruth is now walking**, because they fixed her machine. Bash said it out loud: *"I just signed her death warrant."* See [[Ruth Bielak]].

## What's on the tape

**Undecided as of Session 7.** The party planned to record **Hootie and the Blowfish**, probably at [[Gern]]'s house.

Worth letting them, and worth letting it be funny — **the joke and the horror are the same fact.** The thing that saves them is any human voice at the wrong speed, which means the object that protected a widow for five years was interchangeable with a 1995 pop record. That's the machine being indifferent, delivered as a bit.

## Afterwards

**The tape breaks and the item is done.** Don't hand-wave a repair.

But keep it on the sheet as a **dead prop**, because the knowledge survives the object: the party now knows *what kind of sound* beats *what kind of song*, and can rebuild against a future Siren-type with materials and a little time.

> [!note] DM note
> If a Siren-class singer ever turns up again — as a minion confounding a harder fight, say — **and the party remembers they solved this once**, let it work. That recall is worth more than the encounter tax.
