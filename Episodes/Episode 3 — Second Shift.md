# Episode 3 — Second Shift

Up: [[Episodes]]

**People in the Flats are walking to the mill in their sleep. They are not sleepwalking, they are being *called* — and the only people who can hear the call are the ones the dungeon already has an open account with. The party clears their first [[Wesley's Sigils|sigil]], learns that a lost day is not what a hollowing normally costs, and meets a girl with a paper route who has been writing all of it down for months. Featured Rival: [[Ren Sołtys]] (debut). Wesley fingerprint level: present, unattributed.**

> [!note] Replaces the old Ep3 slot
> [[Episode 4 — The Crossing]] moved back one after Session 4 (Ricky rescued clean and early; [[Panels]], the church, and the [[Mercer Falls Historical Society|Historical Society]] all still unvisited; the party has not seen the dungeon in its reset state). Ghost cosmology is deferred to Ep4 — **do not leak white-print/ghost mechanics here.** This episode establishes the *colour tell* as an unexplained observation only.

## Design commitments

**1. Start with a symptom, not a silhouette.** No monster on screen until Act 4. The horror of this episode is a street of people standing at a fence in their bathrobes at 2 a.m., not looking at each other. If the table doesn't get quiet during Act 1, it isn't working.

**2. Wesley is causally responsible and nobody finds out.** His sigil, his chalk, his toll. What the party *can* conclude is exactly the design target: **someone is down there, on a schedule, drawing things.** Not who. Not why.

**3. [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]] is the key witness, never the victim.** Deliberate variety against [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]]'s capture two episodes running. She is never in danger, never rescued, and never goes below. What happens to her is *social*, and the party does it.

**4. Nobody in this episode is unkind, and Ren still gets burned.** Every adult behaves well. [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] in particular is competent, warm, and effective. The damage comes out of the party doing the sensible thing.

**5. Every fact is true.** The error is only ever in the inference. Later flips must land as *"we reasoned correctly from half the picture."*

## The mechanism (DM-only)

> [!danger] DM-only — how the call actually reaches town
> A [[Nullprints|nullprint]] is a **live file the dungeon is still eating.** Every hollowed-and-living person in Mercer Falls is on the other end of a wire that is already installed and already carrying traffic.
>
> **[[The Caller]] is not projecting a song through seventy feet of bedrock. She is singing down the accounts.** The [[Wesley's Sigils|sigil]] is the antenna; the print is the receiver. Two-stage:
> - **The print is the phone number.** She can only *find* and first-touch someone with an open account.
> - **The Telepathic Bond is the open line.** Once she's charmed someone, she has them at unlimited range, permanently — which is why they come back night after night and why it is escalating.
>
> **Upshot:** the walker roster only recruits from the already-hollowed, and no name ever comes off it on its own.

### Susceptibility table

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Never hollowed | Nothing. No account, no wire. |
| Hollowed, non-bearer, alive | **Walks. No save.** Doesn't wake, doesn't remember. |
| Hollowed, **ember-bearer** | **Wis save.** Faint smudge = thin wire. Hears it, knows it's happening, can fight it. |
| Print closed (restored) | Nothing. *(Effectively an empty set in June 1996 — arms a later reveal.)* |
| Dead | Nothing. Account closed. |

**Depth of print = strength of pull.** Some walkers reach the porch, some the fence, one gets inside. [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]] logs *distance*, and distance is a debt reading.

> [!note] Locked ruling — ember-bearers and compulsion
> **Ember-bearers have advantage on saves against charm, domination, and compulsion.** The ember exists to protect agency; this is what that means in rules. It fixes the Ep3 math, rewards the lore they're learning, and plants a flag for Act 2 when [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] reaches for the same lever. Narrate it as the chest-warmth going *hot and painful* — the ember spending itself. Saving you from this hurts.

| Surface (Ep3) | Held back |
|---|---|
| The walkers are all people who "lost time" at some point. [[Dominique Ferraro\|Dominique]] surfaces this from her canvass. | It's not time. It's an **open account**, and the count of open accounts in this town is enormous. |
| Every walker stops at the **same dip in the mill fence**. | Because a sealed hatch sits thirty feet inside it, at the top of the shaft the singer is in. Sound comes up the pipe. |
| The thing is held by a **chalk line** and cannot leave the room. | It's a toll. Someone bought an exit and the dungeon charged him. He does this routinely and this is the first bill that came due topside. |

| **Ren's durations:** Wendell, still counting. Voss, never stopped. The party, one day each. | The party are the only four people in the recorded history of this town who came back in a day, and nobody — not the Tome, not Ren, not Mercer — has an explanation that fits. |

## Cold open — Stan Prokop

Third person, like Ricky's. Two or three minutes. **Asymmetry is the goal:** the players see a charm working; the characters get a town full of exhausted people who slept badly.

*[**Stan Prokop**, 58. Ex-mill. One of the men who walked out of [[The 1991 Mill Incident|1991]] — "lost some time," never quite came back. A [[Marco's Diner|Marco's]] counter regular, so the party has plausibly seen him. Alternates: Dale Ferris, Marty Voskuil. Optional and better if you're willing to spend it: **Marv**, of "Marv's Spot / the Quiet Rock" from Ep1.]*

1. 1:50 a.m. He doesn't wake up. He's already standing in his kitchen in the dark, holding his boots.
2. He's put them on the wrong feet. It doesn't bother him.
3. **The voice.** Not heard — *remembered.* His mother, calling him in off the porch on Ellis Street. She has been dead thirty years.
4. He is not frightened. He is **relieved.** It is the first time in five years he has been certain where he is supposed to be.
5. Down Ellis, past the [[The Sinclair Station|Sinclair]], up the mill road. **There are other people on the road.** Nobody talks. Nobody looks at anybody. Everyone knows the way.
6. The fence. They stop at the same spot — a section of chain-link where the ground dips. They stand there. Some ten minutes. Some until it gets light.
7. He gets his fingers through the links. Thirty feet in, in the weeds, there is a shape in the ground. He knows it is a door. He cannot reach it.
8. He notices, from a long way off, that he is crying, and cannot think why. He'd been so close.
9. 4:15 a.m. His own porch. Wet feet. One boot.

**Cut. Don't show the mill.**

**And there is no newspaper.** This never makes the Ledger — which is the Filter, doing its job, in front of them. What the party gets instead is Marco griping that three of his regulars fell asleep at the counter this week.

## The four acts

### Act 1 — The lawn

**Open on [[Tank]] and [[Old Man]] standing on their own front lawns at 2:40 a.m.**

They started for the mill. The ember woke them — chest-warmth gone hot enough to hurt. They are eleven and nine houses from home respectively, barefoot, and **there are still people walking past them.** Three or four adults, unhurried, not stopping. That image is the episode.

- [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] and [[Gern]] were not touched and don't know why. **Bash will want to know why**, and the answer is the episode's spine: the two who dissolved in the [[The Buzzsaw Room|Buzzsaw]] are marked; the two who walked out the archway aren't.
- **Morning:** nobody remembers. Marco's is full of tired people rationalizing — heat, a bad mattress, getting older. The [[The Reality Filter|Filter]] is visible for anyone paying attention.
- **A stakeout is available and cheap** (1 TP, night). Watching is free intel and genuinely frightening. But one night gets you one night. **The pattern is not derivable from inside the party's own observation** — they need a record.
- **First contact with Ren, and she doesn't take it.** 4:50 a.m., canvas bag, the only person on the street who is *awake*. She sees them see her. She keeps walking.

### Act 2 — Ren

**[[Ren Sołtys]]** — ~14, wood-elf, morning paper route in the Flats, 4:30 to 6:00. She has the route because her family needs the eleven dollars a week and because it is the only two hours of the day nobody talks to her. She reads people in order to **vanish**; [[Old Man]] reads people in order to **manage** them. That is the whole foil, and this act is his arena.

She is **Filter-porous** — she perceives and never rationalizes, because rationalizing is *social* and she never tells anyone. She just writes it down.

**The notebook (the payload).** A composition book. Months. No interpretation anywhere in it.

- ~30 walker entries with dates and **how far each one got.**
- The **fence coordinates** — the same dip in the chain-link, thirty-odd nights running.
- A durations column going back to last fall: how many days each person was "off."
  - **[[Wendell Pace]]** — entry opened three days ago. **No end date.**
  - **[[Henry Voss]]** — "off since I started the route." No end date, ever.
  - **[[Tank]] — 6/18, 4:40 a.m., Delaney St., *didn't answer when I said hi.*** And [[Old Man]], same night, two streets over.
- People who **used to walk and stopped.** Cross-reference against the obituaries and Timothy's clippings and they all died. *(Don't explain. Let them count.)*

**The negotiation.** What she wants is to not be seen. Escalating asks, in order:
1. She'll answer a question if you don't write it down.
2. She'll show you a page if you don't take it.
3. She'll give you the book if you promise **she stays out of it.**

**Reachable through:** being treated as a competent source rather than a strange kid; anyone who lets her not explain herself; anyone who notices she's been doing real work for free. **Pitfalls:** being thanked publicly; being asked how she knows; being described to a third party.

### Act 3 — The fence, the hatch, and the fork

**The fork — do they use her notebook, or do they use her?** To act on the data, someone has to make it credible to an adult.

| Route | Cost | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Cite the source** — hand the book up, or name her so it lands | 0–1 TP | Immediate adult traction. Word travels. Inside two days it's *"that Sołtys girl's been writing down what everybody does."* |
| **Re-derive it** — independently corroborate enough that the source is never named | **3+ TP across the party**, real nights | Same traction, no burn. The expensive true thing. |
| **Use it and tell nobody** | Free | No burn, but they lose Dominique's leverage and the daylight fence recon, and they're four kids with no adult cover. |

> [!danger] The cruelest routing is through [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]]
> She is the obvious person to hand it to — eighteen, trusted, gets answered. She will handle it **well.** And she'll mention it at [[The Community Center|the Center]], because that is where the adults who can help are, and that is where it spreads. Nobody is unkind at any point. The party did the sensible thing; the ally did her job competently; Ren gets burned. **First firing of the woman-in-the-van engine, at zero cost to Dominique's likability.** See her note.

**Dominique's canvass (her real contribution).** Door-to-door at 7 a.m. — *how've you been sleeping?* — and she comes back with the correlation the party could not get: **every walker is someone who lost time at some point.** She hands it over without knowing what it means, and it enters the party's account of the episode as a thing *they* figured out. (Fracture generator #4. Do not let a player state it.)

**The fence line, in daylight.**
- The dip in the chain-link. Thirty feet inside: **a hatch in the weeds.** Old iron, rusted, a keyhole.
- **[[The Expedition Post|Room-11]]'s skeleton key fits and will not turn.** (Existing canon — the wrong-but-similar lock.) It's bolted from the far side.
- **Put an ear to it.** They can hear her. Faint. Tank and Old Man save or start walking, in daylight, in front of each other.
- Conclusion available: it's underground, it comes out *here*, and this door is not the way in.

**The [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]] beat.** Gym or the Vance porch, Tank's scene, two minutes. He's stuck at eight reps where he was never stuck, and his hands open when he tells them to close. Three days of spore in a pit did something no doctor can find — the Filter won't let them find it. He's already decided it's his own fault for not accounting for it. **The party can't fix this, and Wesley doesn't say "you're fine" — he says "I know exactly what that is."** For a kid whose entire self is control of his own body, a diagnosis is a more fatal offer than a father figure. Plant it here; don't cash it.

### Act 4 — The delve

**Cost: 3 TP flat.** Route, all new ground except the first two rooms:

**Entrance (1) → [[The Cataloguing Office]] (2) → [[The Octagonal Vestibule]] (3) → [[The Bell Junction]] (7) → [[The Spiral Stair Room]] (8).**

- **The reset is the first thing they notice.** Wesley's footprints, the drag marks, the blood — gone. The chalk is not. Their own dead dogmen are gone. First time they've seen the floor come back.
- **The Vestibule collapses behind the spoke they pick.** No bailing.
- **The Bell Junction is a toy handed to them right before the boss** — ring the ceiling bell and the false door becomes a portal to a random already-visited room. Escape hatch, or a very bad gamble.

#### The Shaft — rebuilt geometry ([[The Spiral Stair Room]])

Reshaped for a flying fight. Sits on the ring immediately before the Floor-1 guardian ([[The Pit Room]]), which is exactly why Wesley put a save point here.

- **~25 ft across, ~70 ft to the sealed top hatch** (the mill-grounds exit, from underneath).
- **The sigil** — chalked on the floor, ten feet wide, faintly green. She has been *tending* it.
- **Iron spiral stair bolted to the wall — incomplete.** Two sections rusted out, at ~20 ft and ~48 ft. Ten-foot gaps: Athletics DC 13, or a plank, a rope, or something [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] builds.
- **Four partial catwalk landings at 15 / 30 / 45 / 60 ft**, jutting ~8 ft from the wall, none reaching the centre. L1 and L3 connect to the stair; L2 and L4 are jump-only (DC 13 Acrobatics from an adjacent landing). **The centre of the shaft is empty air. The flyers own it.**
- **Falls:** a creature falling within 5 ft of the wall catches the next landing down (half distance). A centre-shaft fall goes all the way. So most falls are 2d6–3d6 and only the top tier is 6d6 — terrifying, not arbitrary.
- **Ambient — the song.** Top of each round, any creature with a live print makes a **DC 13 Wisdom save** or moves its full speed toward the nearest singer by the most direct route. In this room the most direct route is off the edge. **Ember-bearers roll with advantage.**

#### Encounter block

**[[The Caller]]** *(succubus, modified)* — AC 15, **HP 50**, fly 60.
- **Charm** DC 13 (ember advantage). **Telepathic Bond** as printed.
- **Claw** 1d6+3, once per turn. *She is not the damage.*
- **Shapechanger:** at initiative she appears **differently to each PC** — narrate individually, privately if you can. She has no face of her own; she borrows the one that listener would come home for.
- **Draining Kiss is out of open combat.** Usable only on a charmed, incapacitated target, telegraphed one round. As printed it deletes a level-2 PC on a bad round.
- **No Etherealness.** She is bound to the sigil and cannot leave the shaft. That is the only reason this is solvable.
- **Retreat ladder:** starts on the floor by the sigil. At 75 / 50 / 25% HP she rises one tier. Every tier the party climbs after her makes the harpies more lethal. *The room's correct answer is: don't climb — make her come down.*
- **Reaction — Borrowed Face** (1/round): when hit, she may wear someone that PC won't swing at, imposing disadvantage on the attack.

**2 Harpies** — AC 11, **HP 25** each, fly 40. Luring Song DC 11 (action to sustain). One in the rafters at initiative; **the second arrives at the end of round 2** from a side gallery, so the pressure curve rises instead of front-loading.

**Difficulty:** ~100 HP pool. Reads Deadly on the XP tables; plays **Hard** against this party's debuff stack and a healer. The real dial is not HP — it's **how many rounds of song they eat before someone silences her.**

**Plays the room should reward:**
- **[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s clockwork noisemaker** — a competing sound source. Disadvantage on song saves, or pull a harpy off a target. He built it for the Hush; let it keep earning.
- **[[Old Man]] as a bard, against a thing that steals people with a song.** Countersong isn't a level-2 feature and **he will try it anyway.** Have the ruling ready: action + a Bardic die, Performance vs. the song DC, party gets advantage for a round. Give him this. It is the single most on-theme thing available to him all summer.
- Forcing her off a tier — shove, push mastery, [[Tank]]'s Warhammer.

**Clearing it.** When the last bound monster drops, the green goes out and the chalk goes to ordinary dust. **This costs [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] real delve progress; he has to redraw elsewhere.** The party will never know.

#### What the room gives them

- **The sigil was drawn.** Chalk, a human hand, a compass-steady ten-foot circle. **Tally marks** on the wall nearby — the same marks they followed in Session 2, with a fresh stroke added.
- A spent torch. Adult boot prints, work sole, ~size 11.
- **The top hatch.** The room-11 key fits and won't turn. A known, unopened door onto the mill grounds. Bank it.
- **The print cluster at the foot of the stair.** *(The centrepiece.)* Several full, deep human outlines, old and thinning — men who ran for a locked exit in 1991 and didn't reach it. **Some are dark and sunken. Some are pale ash.** The [[Certaintism|Tome of Conviction]] depicts one Figure 9, one caption, and has no room whatsoever for two states.

> [!note] Running the print cluster
> **Do not explain the colours.** Give them the observation and let them count. Seven men died in 1991 — Timothy's clippings and the Historical Society will both confirm the number. If they count the pale ones and the number doesn't match, they have deduced, unaided, that **some of the men who were taken down here are still alive** — and are, right now, walking to a fence at two in the morning. That is takeaway #1 delivered as a deduction instead of a handout, and it is worth an entire session of prep to leave alone.

## Aftermath & seeds

- **The walking stops that night.** Nobody in Mercer Falls notices except the party and Ren.
- **The one who got inside stays hollowed.** Put him at [[Marco's Diner|Marco's]] — **two glassy bussers now**, and Marco doesn't connect it. The party eats there every week.
- **[[Ren Sołtys|Ren]] ends up at [[The Community Center|the Center]] either way**, and that's correct:
  - **Burned:** it's the only place in town that takes a strange kid without asking what's wrong with her. Wesley's line: *"You saw something true and they spent you for it. I won't."*
  - **Protected:** she now has confirmation that what she's been logging for months is real, the party can't explain it and won't take her down there, and the only adult in Mercer Falls who talks to kids like they're competent is Wesley. **Her disposition toward the party stays high.** Those two facts are not yet in conflict, and that is the horror.
- **The 1991 grounds are no longer empty.** Direct feed into [[Episode 4 — The Crossing]] and [[Ghosts]].
- **Ricky's hands.** Unfixed. Unexplained. Waiting.
- **[[Reverend Hollis]]'s sermon** — he told [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] to come back in a couple of days. Still unclaimed.
- **[[Wade Vance]]** is still unaccounted for. His son has been home for a week.
- The adult [[The Hush]]'s corpse is still in a cave a mile northwest of the mill, outside the dungeon, and will not reset.

## Open dials

- Ren's age (currently ~14), household, and whether the paper route is need or refuge. Her hollowed-and-survived qualifier is **not** this episode.

- Whether her corpse persists to reset (ruling: it does, and it looks like nobody — every player describes her differently).
- Whether the second harpy arrives or the fight starts full-strength, depending on party HP going in.
- Stan Prokop vs. Marv for the cold open.
