# Episode 3 — Second Shift

Up: [[Episodes]]

**The Flats is sleepwalking to the mill. The party goes down to stop it, gets taken apart by a [[The Siren|Siren]] bound to [[Wesley's Sigils|Wesley's sigil]], and spends the rest of the episode working out how to fight something that kills you by making you love it. Featured Rival: [[Ren Sołtys]] (debut, and hollowed in the last thirty seconds). Wesley fingerprint: present, unattributed.**

> [!note] Status — in play
> **[[Session 5 — Second Shift]] played 2026-08-28.** Cold open through the first delve. **The party never reached the Siren** — they rang the [[The Bell Junction|Bell Junction]] bell, found the portal, and took it *away* from the singing. Session 6 opens mid-delve in [[The Buzzsaw Room]], no long rest.
>
> Read the play log before the run-sheet; several things below were superseded at the table.

## The four things that must land

| # | Takeaway | Vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A hollowing normally costs far more than a day, it compounds — and it does neither to the PCs.** | [[Ren Sołtys\|Ren]]'s four years of durations against their own repeat lost day |
| 2 | **[[Certaintism]] isn't the only working theory.** | The notebook is right about the *what* and silent on the *why*; a monster held by a chalk line |
| 3 | **Someone else is active down there, and it started about a week ago.** | The sigil is *drawn*. Chalk, tallies, boot prints — and the party's own graffiti proving marks survive resets ([[The Recall]]) |
| 4 | **Ren introduced; ends the episode hollowed, with a reason to seek out Wesley.** | Structural — fires on every branch |

## The mechanism

> [!danger] Susceptibility is a scar, not an open account
> **[[Nullprints|Nullprints]] fade to nothing on recovery**, so a recovered person has no print — and recovered people are exactly who's walking. The connection isn't a live file. It's **[[Nullprints|the notch]]**: once the machine has taken a bite of you, there's a place in you shaped like its teeth, and it never closes.
>
> **The Siren isn't reaching down a wire. She's just calling, and the only ears that can hear her are ears that have been bitten.** Everybody else sleeps through it.

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Never hollowed | Nothing. No notch |
| **Hollowed, recovered** | **Walks. No save.** [[Tomek Sołtys\|Tomek]] and most of the roster |
| Hollowed, **ember-bearer** | **Walks, then wakes.** Wis save with advantage — the ember spends itself, and it hurts |
| **Currently hollowed** | **Doesn't walk.** Nothing in there to call. **[[Wendell Pace\|Wendell]] is the control group** |
| Terminal ([[Henry Voss]]) | Doesn't walk. Already gone |

**The roster is exactly "people who were taken once and came back"** — the hardest category in town to see, because every one of them is fine.

**Why the PCs are last, after a full week notched:** an ember-bearer's notch is a **faint smudge**, the hardest thing in town to get hold of. She started with the craters and has been working outward. **Ren's walker column is a severity ranking she doesn't know she compiled.** She isn't getting stronger — she's getting *further*.

> [!note] Locked ruling — ember-bearers and compulsion
> **Advantage on saves against charm, domination, and compulsion.** The ember exists to protect agency; this is what that means in rules. Narrate it as the chest-warmth going *hot and painful* — saving you from this hurts.

> Ren dates the walking to **the week before last, Tuesday or Wednesday — the night the party was in the dungeon.** They went into the mill and the town started walking to it within a day.

## Structure

## Act 1 — the night

**Cold open, 1:50 a.m.** Tomek doesn't wake up; he's already up. The voice is **Ren**, calling him to come and look at something, delighted — *"and it has been a long while since anybody was delighted about him."* He walks past his sister twice. She writes down the time.

> [!note] The register — hold it for every walker
> **An invitation you don't have to earn.** No urgency, no debt, no deadline, nobody disappointed in you. Being called in off the street at dusk when you were a kid: the day is over, you're allowed to stop, and somebody is glad it's you.
>
> **Avoid entirely:** lateness, being expected, having failed to arrive. *That register is reserved for [[Episode 4 — The Crossing]].*
>
> **Vocabulary:** come in · come here · it's alright · you can stop · nothing you have to fix first · **"I'm coming."**

**2:40 a.m., the lawns.** Tank and Old Man come to standing up, barefoot, nine houses out, with walkers going past them. **Each gets one bonus Night TP for this scene only.** Don't roll — they wake because they have embers.

**Everything topside fails.** Can't wake a walker. Can't block one — they go around like water; restrained, they stand patient as a dog at a door and resume when you let go. Asked a question, one answers without stopping or looking: ***"I'm coming."*** Wendell is asleep in his bed.

**The gate.** *(Played as the front gate with the chain and master lock — the spot the campaign started — not a fence dip.)* Notched PCs hear it coming up out of the ground: a voice they'd want to go to. **DC 13 Wis, advantage.**

**Ren, writing.** Ten feet from the walkers, composition book on her forearm. See [[Ren Sołtys]] for the full scene. **She does not hand over the notebook in Act 1** — they should see it, watch her use it, and not get it. She warns them off and fails, which is her whole character: her brother went in on a dare and she couldn't stop him; now four kids are about to do the same.

## Act 2 — the delve and the wipe

**3 TP.** Route: Entrance → [[The Cataloguing Office]] → [[The Octagonal Vestibule]] → [[The Bell Junction]] → [[The Spiral Stair Room]].

**The reset.** Everything the dungeon made is restored; everything the party did or took is not. See **[[The Recall]]** for the full persistence table — the party ran their own experiments on this in Session 5 and the answers are now canon.

**The shaft.** 25 ft across, 70 ft tall. Incomplete iron spiral stair (gaps at ~20 and ~48 ft, Athletics DC 13). Four partial landings: **L1 (15 ft) and L2 (30 ft) reach the stair; L3 (45) and L4 (60) are jump-only.** Centre is empty air — the flyers own it. Ten-foot chalk sigil on the floor, faintly green; she's been *tending* it.

**Ambient song**, top of each round: every notched creature makes **DC 13 Wis, advantage**, or moves full speed toward the nearest singer by the most direct route. **House ruling: the song overrides the RAW damaging-terrain safeguard.** A charmed creature doesn't perceive the drop — it perceives *the way home.*

**Run her at full strength.** Full block in [[The Siren]]; short version: AC 15, **HP 60**, fly 60, **all damage resistances stripped except poison** (RAW she resists nearly everything this party can do), **no Etherealness**, tethered 40 ft to the sigil. Charm **DC 15**. Draining Kiss **live**. **Take Me Home** — she grapples a charmed creature automatically, because it goes willingly, flies up, and lets go; better still, hands off to a harpy, which has no tether. Two harpies, HP 25, one at initiative and one at the end of round 2.

**Show both kill routes at least once.** The drop is the one they'll remember, because the horror isn't being grabbed — it's that they went willingly.

**Fleeing is real and it works.** She's tethered; the harpies won't leave her. **Anyone who gets out under their own power takes no notch** — [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] and [[Gern]] currently have zero.

**Three branches:** full wipe (intended) · partial · **they win — let them.** Short episode; bank Ruth and the Talkboy. **Do not railroad the loss.**

## Act 3 — town

**The lost day.** Everyone who dropped loses **exactly one day. All of them. Same as last time.** Don't comment. It detonates when the notebook comes out.

**Ren finds them.** She warned them, they went anyway, and she's spent a day and a half watching four more kids not be people. The notebook, finally — see [[Ren Sołtys]] for contents and the four available deductions.

**[[The Community Center]] — [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]].** Structurally the redundancy for Ren: if they skipped or burned her, he keeps the episode running.

> [!danger] The eccentricity: he keeps a notebook
> Small, cheap, back pocket, soft at the corners. Names and dates and a word or two. Completely unembarrassed: *"I can't hold it all. Never could. So I write it down."*
>
> **Reads as:** a man who runs a community center and actually remembers people. **Is:** four years of candidates.
>
> **The rhyme is the point.** Two people in this episode keep notebooks of hollowed townspeople — one out of fear, one out of appetite, and they are physically indistinguishable. **If the party has Ren's book in a backpack while he opens his, draw no attention to it. Ever.**

He reads names off it and offers to write more out longhand. **His list overlaps Ren's almost exactly.** If you want the sharp version, he thumbs past a page with **their four names on it**, dated mid-June, and explains it plainly: *"Somebody said you'd been out at the mill overnight and looked half dead for a couple days after. I keep an eye on that kind of thing. Nobody keeps an eye on kids in this town. Somebody should."* Every word true.

**The thing he doesn't do: he never asks a second question.** A normal adult digs. He doesn't — because he already knows why anyone in this town asks that. Invisible now, unmissable on a reread.

**One warm beat that costs him and gains him nothing**, tuned to whoever's in the room. And on Ricky, if he comes up — he is the only adult in town who won't rationalize the Hush: *"That boy didn't get lost. People don't come back from getting lost looking like that."*

**Props:** chalk in the seams of his knuckles, one line, never mentioned again. And the last shot of the episode — through the window, Ren at a table with Wesley, *talking*, and he is listening, and it looks like a good thing. Because it is one.

**[[Panels]].** The Hush movie is on. **1 TP to ingratiate** and Russ recommends **MIDNIGHT SHIFT #11, "The Lorelei Waltz"** — the villain sings people off a bridge and the hero beats her with a dictaphone played back warped. He hands them the solution as a plot summary and nobody notices.

**[[Ruth Bielak]] — the anomaly.** Cross-reference the walker list against the durations and one name is on the second and not the first. Hollowed autumn '94, eleven days, never once a walker. Her husband died in '89; she sleeps with a cassette of his voice on the windowsill, seven years, every night. **The belt is stretched. It runs slow.** *"He sounds slower than he was. I keep meaning to get it fixed."*

**She isn't protected by anything clever. She's protected by a broken tape player.**

> [!danger] This cannot come from the party
> Four kids do not get told about a dead husband's voice on a cassette. It's in her bedroom, at night, and Ruth doesn't know it's relevant. It comes out sideways, in the kind of conversation an eighteen-year-old has with a lonely seventy-one-year-old over an hour and a cup of coffee.
>
> **The party goes door to door and gets names. [[Dominique Ferraro\|Dominique]] goes to one house and stays.** That's what she's for — and it's precisely the skill that will be used on the party in September.

**[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] gets the mechanism.** Dominique gets the fact; Bash gets the why. The belt, the drag, playback dropped about an octave — and then: **it isn't that the voice is comforting, it's that it's on her frequency.** Her notch is occupied; nothing else can get in. **Which means content is irrelevant. Any recording. Only speed matters.** He's right, and he should get to be right out loud.

> **THE TALKBOY** *([[Jerry]]'s, six dollars, bin of dead Christmas toys)*
> Any recording, played on SLOW. **3 charges** — the tape stretches further every pass.
> **Bonus action.** Until the start of your next turn, allies within 30 ft are **immune to the Siren's song and to Luring Song.**
> **First use only:** she hears a human voice at the wrong speed and it isn't hers. **DC 13 Wis or stunned until the end of her next turn.**
> After the third charge the tape is unreadable.

**Let the party decide who carries it.** Tank is the natural answer — free bonus action after round one, no ranged attack, and it keeps him in a fight happening sixty feet above his head. **Don't steer.** Let whoever records it choose what goes on the tape, on screen, before they know what it's for.

> [!note] The wax problem — needs a ruling
> The party landed on **wax in the ears** at the end of Session 5. Recommended: let it fail informatively — it muffles the sound, but the pull isn't acoustic where it matters. Then Gern's *other* idea (ringing the Bell Junction bell continuously to drown her out) makes Ruth's discovery a **refinement of something they already guessed** rather than a handout.

## Act 4 — the return

**3 TP.** Same route, fast. **Charm drops to DC 13** — they've been bitten, they know her voice, they came prepared. **Draining Kiss and Take Me Home stay live.** Three Talkboy charges against a six-round fight means whoever holds it covers half and has to choose which half. **She is not weaker. They are equipped.**

**Clearing it.** *The green goes out. That's all. No sound. The chalk on the floor is just chalk now.* **This costs Wesley significant delve progress** — he has to redraw elsewhere, and they will never know.

**What the room gives them:** the sigil was *drawn* — chalk, human hand, a compass-steady ten-foot circle. **Tally marks nearby, the same as Session 2, with a fresh stroke added.** A spent torch. Adult boot prints, work sole, ~size 11.

## The closer — Ren goes in

Full treatment in [[Ren Sołtys]]. Summary: **circumstance-driven, not Wesley-driven.** The trigger is Tomek getting through the fence. **The permission is the party** — she watched four kids go into that mill and come back in one day, and her model updated the only way she has ever updated a model. She's wrong because of an ember she can't know about. **Ren is hollowed by her own methodology.**

The party clears the sigil, the walking stops, and on the mill road in the small hours she comes down toward them with the notebook still in her hand. *"Ren?"* She goes right past. *"Ren."* She does not turn her head. **The cold open, inverted, same two lines.** Then: *you take the notebook out of her hand. She lets you.*

**Nine days.** Gone for all of [[Episode 4 — The Crossing]].

## Aftermath

- The walking stops that night. Nobody notices except the party.
- **Tomek is fine.** Completely. At the Sinclair Monday morning. That's the whole point of him.
- **Wendell is still glassy at Marco's**, and now they know what the difference means.
- **Ricky is still doubting**, and his hands still don't work.
- Ren's mother has nine days of a daughter who isn't there, and **the Center brings meals to families in trouble.**
- The Community Center is on their map, with a closed door at the back that nobody mentioned.

## Open dials

- **Ren's saving-grace line** isn't locked.

- **Ruth's tape content** is unwritten. What Walt is saying matters more than it looks.
