## Design commitments

**1. Wesley is causally responsible, and nobody finds out.** [[The Hush]] had nothing to do with him; from Ep3 on the monster exists *because* of him. The players don't need to learn it. They need him **in the mix**, so that three or four episodes from now he becomes the common denominator.

**2. The misdirection points at [[Reverend Hollis]], and every fact is true.** The error is never in the fact — only in the inference, and the inference is wrong only because the party is missing a dataset it can't reach yet. Later flips must land as *"we reasoned correctly from half the picture,"* never as a DM lie.

**3. The Center reads as sanctuary, and the reading is honest.** Both [[Lowell Brandt|Lowell]] and [[Eddie Park|Eddie]] were driven out of Grace Tabernacle and found kindness at [[The Community Center]]. That's true. It's just answering a different question than the players think.

**4. Lore leaks freely; attribution doesn't.** Hollowing, agency-loss, nullprints, and the machine all land in earnest this episode. *"Who is leading people down there"* does not.

**5. [[Eddie Park|Eddie]] is the key witness, not the victim.** Deliberate variety against [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]]'s capture — and doubly reinforced by [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]] playing the same role in Ep3. Two witness-rivals running, which makes the *third* rival's episode the place to vary the shape.

**6. The "late" register belongs to this episode.** Reserved deliberately — Ep3's Siren calls in the opposite key (an invitation you don't have to earn). Lowell's possession is **debt**: expected, failing to arrive, someone waiting. Keep it exclusive.

## The monster

[[Lowell Brandt]] — full profile there; rules in [[Ghosts]].

- **Anchor:** the mill-road **grade crossing**, half a mile short of the mill grounds. Old crossbucks, no gates, a signal scheduled for upgrade before the mill closed and never upgraded. Two freights a week, late, serving somewhere one town over. *(As played: lights only. Proposed: Tuesdays and Saturdays, ~11:40 p.m.)*
- **Why here:** it's on his Tuesday route; it sits between town and the dungeon so **every delve this summer crosses it**; and it's far enough from the grounds that he's the only thing in the dark — the only reason this case is solvable (per the [[The 1991 Mill Incident|1991]] ruling, the grounds are thick with ghosts nobody has separated out).
- **He is commuting, not hunting.** Possession drives a borrowed body toward the mill; his blind spot is the crossing itself.
- **Lethal window: a train on approach.** ~90 seconds, twice a week. Otherwise he's dread. **The freight timetable is public** — a two-TP investigation that converts directly into survival.
- **The visible loop:** a man in a work coat walking the ballast, stopping, **patting his coat pockets for something that isn't there.**
- *(Proposed level-3 stat block, the ember wake for possessed PCs, and folklore tools are in [B2 The Saturday Freight](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/103895ae-b12a-423b-94ec-6cd13aaf484d).)*

## Cold open — Ray Kubiak

Third person. Two or three minutes. **Asymmetry is the goal:** the players see a possession; the characters get a rumour about a bad crossing.

*[[[Ray Kubiak]] — late forties, a [[Marco's Diner|Marco's]] regular so the party has plausibly seen him. Grace Tabernacle for twenty years, quietly drifting to the Center since spring.]* *(Name locked in play. Died Tuesday 9 July 1996.)*

1. Kubiak out the side door of Grace Tabernacle, hot-faced. He went to tell Hollis he was leaving. It got loud; people heard it through the door. It didn't feel how he'd imagined. Behind him through the glass, Hollis is still standing where he left him, not moving.
2. Mill road. No lights past the [[The Sinclair Station|Sinclair]]. Warm, bugs, gravel.
3. Someone at the edge of the road in a work coat. Kubiak slows — his brain files it as a resemblance, because Lowell Brandt has been dead a year and Kubiak carried one corner of the casket.
4. He says the name out loud. *"Lowell?"*
5. **The cold.** Like stepping down into a cellar in July. Every sound in the world moves somewhere behind glass.
6. **His legs keep going.** He notices it the way you notice you've driven home with no memory of the drive — mild, then not mild, because he tries to stop and the stopping doesn't reach anything.
7. He's thinking about a boy's bedroom with a **humidifier** running in it. He's **counting stairs**. He is *so late*, and someone has been waiting on him this whole time, and the shame of that is enormous, specific, and completely unfamiliar. **Ray Kubiak doesn't have a son.**
8. The crossbucks light up. **He can see them. He cannot hear them.** Whatever's driving doesn't look up.
9. He steps around the arm. Not running. Purposeful. A man late for work. *(The table established no gates — if this text is reused, make it the signal post.)*
10. Something in him is frightened, and it isn't frightened of the train — it's frightened it won't get there in time. Then his hands come up and **pat down the pockets of a coat he isn't wearing.**

**Cut. Don't show the train.**

**Then hand them the paper.** Two paragraphs in the Ledger, under the fold, with the word *apparently* in it — and on the same page, the notice for Saturday's one-year memorial vigil at the Community Center for **Lowell A. Brandt.**

> [!note] Deliberate contrast with Ep3
> The sleepwalking **never made the paper** — the Filter swallowed a week of it whole. Kubiak's death does, because a man hit by a train is unignorable. If the party notices that one made the Ledger and the other didn't, that's a real and correct read on how the Filter works. Reward it. *(They now hold Mercer's paper III, which says exactly this — "it is never the same answer twice." A party that connects the two has earned it.)* *(Session 9: they noticed the paper never connected the two deaths.)*

- Half-hearted rumours: kids say Hollis cursed him; adults say that crossing's always been bad. Nobody investigates either.
- **The survivor node.** Someone who nearly stepped out two weeks ago and got grabbed by a friend. Embarrassed, not traumatised — they've told nobody because the story is *"I almost walked in front of a train for no reason."* *(After Session 9 this is a tactical tip — holding on works — not where PC knowledge catches up. - **TP at the Center buys real help** — adults who'll vouch for the kids, a phone, the bulletin, Wesley making a call. No strings, no catch.

### Act 3 — The cure, and the vial fills
- The elixir works. **Full cure — the boy living is the light in a grim episode.**
- **Cost of the lost year:** Denny is left with a **permanent disability** (a bad leg), otherwise healthy.
- **The disease replaces the liquid** — the vial refills with black ichor, warm to the touch, moving a little on its own. That is now the release object: **proof the unfinished business is finished**, per the decided rule.
- **Eddie's punch.** The cure was in the box for a year. He couldn't have known — a vial in a dead man's effects means nothing without the pages, and the pages were under it. Wesley handles it beautifully: *"You couldn't have known."* True, kind, and the beginning of Eddie never having to weigh anything again.

- **Wesley learns** (via Alma, by Monday) what Lowell was searching for, and that four kids brought it up. Ep5+ consequence.

## The Mercer writings (the Gern crack)

> [!important] Revised after Session 9 — they came from Wesley's hands
> The design delivered these "from the hands of the man they suspect, at the Library microfilm." **As played, Bash got them at the Historical Society from the man they don't suspect:** Wesley pulled a Mercer family compendium and Bash photocopied pages. The reread will hurt more. **Three photocopies (decided content, draft text):**
> - **A — a plate from Elias's notes:** two figures on stone; the dark one "drawn down by degrees as the man returns to himself"; the pale one fixed — "The pale do not diminish. I have kept one under observation eleven years." The colour tell, and the fourth strike.
> - **B — a family letter on the funeral custom:** where it held, the thing burned was a thing he had *finished* ("Kowalczyk's last ledger, closed and balanced"); where it failed, a keepsake.
> - **C — Constance Mercer to her brother, 1891:** first seed of **Mercer as a past user.** "Father goes down every Sunday after service… the works below will not keep without a hand on them… He comes up brighter each time, and kinder to no one." Pencil five-bar tallies in the margin.

Mercer's register is **clinical and industrial**: no sides, no enemy, no salvation. He describes the emptying observationally and speculates about an **apparatus** — something built, with intent, and a purpose he can't name. Critically, **he illustrates the nullprint too**, and calls it something like a *discharge figure* or an *index*. Same shape as Figure 9, different caption, and his is fifty years older.

**Why it cracks [[Gern]]:** his authority rests on holding the only text that explains what they're seeing. Now there are two texts that both get the phenomena right and disagree completely about why — and the older one didn't need a cosmology to do it. The true reading is also the bleak one: no one to pray to, no side, no rescue. Gern's temptation will be to reject it *because* it's cold, which is a discernment failure dressed as faith. See [[Certaintism]].

> [!note] Ep3 already loaded this gun — and S8 loaded it again
> [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s notebook is the same shape of argument in a fifteen-year-old's handwriting — right about the *what*, silent on the *why*, and more accurate than scripture.
