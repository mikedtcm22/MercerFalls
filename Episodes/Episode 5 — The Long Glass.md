# Episode 5 — The Long Glass

Up: [[Episodes]]

**A dead mirror in the back room at [[Panels]] started answering this summer. The playground dare attached to it has become a marking ritual, and something behind the glass is working through a list of names two nights at a time. Meanwhile a twelve-year-old has quietly figured out how to walk into the space behind the mirror, and has been looting it for three weeks.**

**Killing the thing ends it permanently — and unseals a mirror three hundred feet under [[The Mill]] that a stranger has been trying to pry off a wall since March.**

> [!warning] Draft — Zander's role is explicitly provisional
> See [[Zander Pruitt]]. The current treatment (schedule-surfing scavenger, pure opportunist) is a **placeholder the DM is not satisfied with** and wants to revisit in a later session. Everything else here is stable enough to build on. **Do not harden Zander's motive into canon without revisiting it first.**

> [!note] Supersedes
> This replaces the [[Episode — The Mirror]] stub. The provenance thread, Russ's sister [[Annie]], and the Mercer-origin seed all survive; the "Order-touched echo with zero Wesley fingerprint" framing does not.

## Cosmology

### The pair

[[Elias Mercer]] built two mirrors and bound a thing into them as a **live seal** — a guard dog fused to a door, not a padlock.

- **The far one — the Tallying Glass** *(name provisional)* — is bolted into dungeon stone on **Floor 1**. It is the dungeon's **record**, and **item 1 of [[The Three Ritual Items]]**, replacing the Tally Stone.
- **The near one** is its twin, currently in the back room at [[Panels]].

### The bridge

A **sixty-foot corridor** between the two mirrors. Not a room in the world — the entity's own extent. Walls lined with a century of pocket contents in **datable strata**: collars and watch chains, mill badges and work gloves, a thin 1991 layer, this month's bright plastic.

**Transit and trophy case. Not a prison and not a boss arena.**

### Two keys

The bridge needs both ends live.

| End | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Far** | Someone disturbing the seal on the Tallying Glass |
| **Near** | The twin sitting somewhere people say their names to it |

[[Panels]] has had the near key since ~1990. **Nothing happened for six years.** Wesley started scratching at the seal in the spring. That timing is his fingerprint — *present, unattributed, and datable.*

### The dare

> *Say your name to the Mercer mirror three times and it'll show you the one thing it kept.*

Playground folklore since the mirror landed at Panels in 1989. Seven years of nothing. Now it's a **filing ritual** — name and face, handed over voluntarily.

**Kids know it. Adults don't. It has never been written down.** Which is exactly why Wesley — hunting through auction ledgers, estate records and provenance chains — has spent five months looking in the wrong *medium* for a thing that lives in the mouths of ten-year-olds.

> [!danger] Wesley's blind spot is thematic, not convenient
> He has spent a year cultivating four kids and has never once asked one of them a real question. He treats them as instruments, not sources. That flaw costs him three months — and he eventually gets the answer from Zander by accident, in a conversation about junk.

**Natural population cap:** doing the dare requires getting into Russ's back room, which is his rapport gate. The marked pool is bounded and specific — the Friday game crowd, maybe a dozen kids, all in Zander's orbit.

### Why the Glass is still on the wall

Every previous generation ended this by **starving** it. Cover the near mirror or move it somewhere nobody looks, and the thing goes dormant with no way out. Cheap, immediate, no delving required, and it has worked four times in a century.

**Wesley is the first person since Mercer who wants the object instead of the quiet.** The party can derive that from the historical record without ever hearing his name.

### Why it's still alive

It has never been killed, **and it does not reset.** It isn't a dungeon creature — it's a bound thing fixed to a specific object. More furniture than fauna. (State this at the table: it makes the party's kill permanent and unrepeatable, which is what gives the episode weight and what makes Zander's door close forever.)

Dormancy doesn't empty the corridor. Cut off, the bridge just sits there unreachable with everything in it.

## The Kept

**An archivist, not a predator.** It takes people because the record is incomplete, and it takes their things because things are part of the entry. A **standing assemblage** of everything it has ever kept — watch chains, mill badges, boots, a lunch pail, house keys, one bright plastic Walkman near the shoulder. It has no face of its own. **It borrows.**

### MO — two nights

| | |
|---|---|
| **Night 1** | Pulls the marked person through. **Hollows them in its chamber**, so the [[Nullprints\|nullprint]] burns *there*, in its collection. Strips their pockets on the way through. Puts them back. They turn up on the mill road at dawn — empty, docile, and missing a watch or a shoe. |
| **Night 2** | Comes back out through a mirror and **kills them where they are.** |

**Why the second visit exists:** a recovered victim's print fades to nothing — *the file deletes itself.* The kill is what freezes the mark **white** and makes the record permanent.

> It doesn't murder people for what it wants. It murders them so the entry can't be undone.

### The truth behind it

> [!danger] DM-only
> What Mercer bound into the glass was **his own reflection** — a copy of an obsessive cataloguer with the deciding part removed. A hollowed thing that has functioned perfectly for a hundred years. It collects because *he* collected.
>
> **The Episode 5 boss is a working preview of Wesley's ending, fought before the party knows Wesley's name.** When they read the Mercer papers in Act 2, they should recognise the voice.

### Mechanical sketch (unbuilt)

- **~AC 15, CR 5–6 hit points.** Danger through attrition and conditions, not spike damage. Party will be **level 3**.
- **Phase gate on faces.** Holds 3–4 **aspects** — people it has filed — lifted off the white prints on the floor. Each grants a different attack. Strip one per HP threshold. When the last is gone it's a pile of objects.
- **Lair actions (on 20):**
  - **Filing** — a white print underfoot opens and a hand comes up. Restrained. *Lesson: don't stand on the dead.*
  - **Cataloguing** — takes one carried non-magical object off a PC and adds it to itself, gaining temp HP. Returned when it drops.
  - **The Naming** — says a PC's name in a voice they know. Wis save or their next attack goes at an ally.
- **The off switch:** mirrors it has dragged into the chamber over the decades. AC 12, few HP. Breaking them thins the lair actions. **The smart play is spending actions on the room** — which is the right lesson for this table and a real job for the non-damage characters.

### Fight location — the dungeon, not the bridge

1. **The lost-day safety net doesn't obviously extend to a pocket space.** Put the fight where the net is and you can build something genuinely nasty.
2. **The ending requires it** — the mirror has to be taken while the party is a room or two away.
3. **The bridge is better as non-combat space.** Dread and discovery. Don't burn the crossing on initiative.

### The filing chamber

- **White prints in century-deep strata.** Everyone whose file completed. Every one of them is a [[Ghosts|ghost]].
- **This month's layer is small — kid-sized.** The dare is a kid thing. The party will read that in about one second.
- **One fresh black print** — someone hollowed last night, alive in town right now, with one night left. The party is standing on the proof and doesn't know the name.

**It's already there when they open the door.** Not stalking, not ambushing — sitting among its prints, keeping vigil over open entries, holding a borrowed face. It looks up.

> [!tip] Long-tail hook
> Dozens of releasable white prints, located. The release rite needs a counter-claim per person. That's years of optional, moral Act 2/3 work born in this room.

## History route

The research payload should be **a usable countermeasure**, not just backstory.

### The mirror-covering custom

**Mercer Falls covers its mirrors.** When someone dies, you drape every mirror in the house. Stricter here than anywhere nearby, practised by families with nothing else in common — Catholic, Baptist, unchurched. Everyone assumes it's an old mourning thing about the soul getting caught. Nobody can say where it came from.

**It came from here.** It's a countermeasure that outlived its reason and got reclassified as superstition.

Discoverable via the funeral home, an old undertaker, [[Grace Tabernacle]], the [[Mercer Falls Historical Society]], or any grandmother in town. The **lore route** ([[Gern]], the Tome of Conviction) converges on the same instruction from the opposite direction.

**How it works at the table:**
- Victim one always dies — nobody knows yet.
- It has to be **every** mirror. A missed one in a hall closet is fatal. House sweep under time pressure — and the scene where [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]'s methodical streak finally beats Zander's instincts at something.
- It buys a night. It doesn't stop the dare, and it doesn't stop the thing.

### Other finds

- **Provenance chain** (canon): Mansion → 1983 Society auction (funds were tight) → estate sales → [[Russ]], 1989.
- **Lot 14** — catalogue entry reads *"Mercer looking-glass, one of a pair; mate not located."* A dry archivist's note. **Planted and unread.** This is what makes them say *oh, crap* in September.
- **Clusters of deaths tracking the mirror's address.** Not disappearances — waves of people going strange and then dying of accidents, falls, drownings, exposure. A statistical smear nobody ever connected, because there's no crime in it anywhere.
- **The pattern of silence.** Households that covered every mirror and then quietly got rid of the big one. Multiple times, across a hundred years, with nothing written down.

> **The thesis, delivered as research:** every generation chose to hide it instead of end it, and that's why it's still killing kids in 1996. The party gets to be the first people who don't. The price is unsealing the far side.

### Strata / timeline

| Window | Layer | Far key |
|---|---|---|
| 1890s–1910s | Collars, watch chains, calling cards, a servant's boot | Mercer himself, working the seal constantly; twin hung in his house |
| Mid-century | Mill badges, work gloves, a lunch pail | [[The Mercer Gentleman's Club]] remnant — gives those attenuated descendants something to have actually *done* |
| 1991 *(optional, strong)* | Thin | Whatever happened under the mill disturbed the seal. Mirror already at Panels, dare already circulating. Two or three deaths absorbed into the larger catastrophe. **Everyone was counting the seven.** |
| Summer 1996 | Bright plastic, a Walkman, a retainer case | [[Wesley Crane]], since March |

**The tell isn't the objects, it's the gaps** — decades of nothing between dense clusters. That's what tells a researcher this is episodic rather than continuous.

## Act structure

**Act 1 — the pattern.** A kid comes home empty at dawn. Two nights later he's dead in his bed and the town calls it a heart thing — **the [[The Reality Filter|Filter]] eating a murdered child in about a day.** Panels, finally: Russ, the mirror, the dare, the curio shelf, Annie's photo. Zander on the couch, unbothered, fencing objects [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] can identify as dungeon material.

**Act 2 — the list and the custom.** *Who did the dare and when* — the roster is the clock. History route yields the covering custom. Legwork to get word around town before dark.

**Act 3 — the race.** Zander stalls, misdirects, lies pleasantly. **His argument is good:** *stop the dare, problem solved, why does it have to die?* Counters: marks already placed still resolve; it has names now; it's been escalating since spring; and **his stuck-open door means the safe fix doesn't fully work.** He is the reason covering it isn't enough. He has to actually choose. The party has to **mark themselves** to get in.

**Act 4 — the crossing and the chamber.** Sixty feet of corridor, then black stone. The filing chamber. The Kept looks up.

## Ending sequence

1. **The Kept dies.** The bridge collapses.
2. **Zander is inside it.** Ejected into the mirror room and hollowed by the ejection. **Full, deep black print** — no ember.
3. **The seal is dead.** The Tallying Glass comes off the wall.
4. **Wesley is standing there.** He was waiting. He takes the mirror.
5. **He performs restoration on the live black print** — and Zander wakes up with Wesley Crane's hand on his shoulder in the dark under the mill.

> [!danger] The recruitment
> Wesley doesn't manipulate him. He **genuinely saves his life**, immediately, competently, with knowledge nobody else in town has. Zander walks out owing him something real.
>
> Per [[Wesley's Recruits]], the qualifier is now satisfied: **hollowed, survived, notched, audible.** Whether Wesley *knew Zander was in there* is the dial that decides whether this is the roster's one **manufactured** hollowing.

**What the party finds when they walk back:** the mirror gone from the wall, a **green sigil** burning in the empty space, and a fresh **black** nullprint on the floor — full and deep, arms slightly open.

By their own forensics that reads: *the sigil guy was here, he took something, and a non-ember-bearer was hollowed on this spot and is still alive out there somewhere.* **Same hand as [[Episode 3 — Second Shift]].** Still no name.

> [!tip] The ticking forensic
> A restored print is **closed** and is consumed fast. If Bash doesn't rub, photograph or measure it that night, it's gone by morning. They will probably miss it. That should sting later.

**What they leave with:** they won. Russ is alive, the dare is dead, the Panels mirror is inert glass and Russ is glad to be rid of it. They know a stranger works sigils in the dungeon and took something off a wall. They don't know what a mirror is worth, that there are two more, or that **they broke the lock themselves.**

Zander's fine next time they see him. A little quiet.

- **Tally Stone → the Tallying Glass.** See [[The Three Ritual Items]].
- **Wesley's pre-Session-1 solo delve becomes a FAILURE.** He found the sealed mirror, learned from the Mercer papers it was half a pair, and has hunted the twin ever since.
- **Floor 1 clue changes** from "fresh recess + abandoned sigil" to **a mirror bolted to the wall, visibly worked at** — chalk, scrape marks, failed attempts. Retrofittable into a room the party has already walked through and ignored.

## Open questions

### Combat
- Statblock proper, aspects, HP thresholds.
- Room layout — and **how the party gets from the chamber back to the mirror room without seeing Wesley.**
- Confirm the mirrors-as-off-switch design.

### Roster
- **Victim list with real names.** Who did the dare, in what order, which are already dead, which is the fresh black print. **This is the episode's clock and the biggest remaining piece.**
- Is [[Russ]] marked? Does his Filter-break fire in the chamber with [[Annie]]'s face, or topside?
- Who is the Act 1 death, and how hard does the Filter eat it?

### Zander
- See [[Zander Pruitt]] — **his whole role is open.**
- Does Wesley know he's in the bridge? (Manufactured-hollowing dial.)
- How much wild magic shows on screen? *Lean: one unexplained step in his method, no more.*
- Does the party ever learn he was in there?

### Structural
- **Where's the race?** The DM wants PC-vs-Wesley competition for each artifact. As written the party never knows the Glass is a prize, so **there is no contest for item 1** — the race starts at Gathered Dust. Accept, or add a late thread where they could plausibly have kept it?
- **Naming.** Episode vs. artifact shouldn't share a name. Both are provisional (*the Long Glass*, *the Tallying Glass*, *the Standing Glass*).
- **Timeline.** How many days does the episode span? How many nights does the every-other-evening schedule give them?
- **What Zander's been fencing, specifically.** Bash's Act 1 hook depends on it.
