# Episode 5 — The Mirror

Up: [[Episodes]]

**Something with no reflection has been using a Mercer looking-glass as a front door into Mercer Falls. The town liar is the only one who has noticed, because his older brother is turning into one of them. Killing it saves the brother — and unlocks the mirror for the man who woke it.**

> [!warning] Supersedes all earlier Episode 5 material (2026-10-17)
> This note replaces **[[Episode 5 — The Long Glass]]** (the "Kept" archivist concept — dropped wholesale) and the **[[Episode — The Mirror]]** stub (the Order-touched echo — dropped). Both paths are now tombstones.

> - **Gone:** the Kept, the filing chamber and archive, the sixty-foot bridge, the dare-as-marking-ritual, the "Danny" brother variants, the Order-touched echo, [[The Black Rotunda]] as boss arena, and any hollowing/nullprint tie for this episode's monster.

## Cold open

*Set early–mid July, roughly ten days before the episode opens — after [[Session 8 — Second Shift|Session 8]] cleared his Room-8 foothold.*

1. **The Long Gallery, by lantern.** A man works at the frame of the mirror at the far end — unhurried, practised; half the bolts are already out. In the glass: the Mercer Mansion drawing room, furnished and gas-lit, and a figure standing with its back turned.
2. **The frame bites.** He cuts his palm. The blood runs down into the glass.
3. **In the reflection, the figure turns around.**
4. **He's good** — calm, lands a real blow with the pry bar, doesn't run. It doesn't matter. Cut to black on the bite.

> [!danger] Information wall
> No face, no voice, no chalk, no green light, **no visible relic** (a steady glow in his hand would sit too close to the players' own warmth). Show his power through competence, not magic. **Optional breadcrumb:** he makes a tally mark on the gallery wall before he starts, matching the tallies at [[The Tollgate]]. Cut it for zero thread.

**Let them think he died.** The ending's fresh green sigil is what tells them the cold-open man and the sigil hand are the same person — and that he got back up. A deliberate escalation: their rival can't be killed down there either. It explains nothing.

## The guard in the glass

### What it is
**A vampire, curated** ([[The Nightmare Engine]]). Primary source ***The Lost Boys* (1987)** — the gang, the half-vampire, the head-vampire rule. Texture from the ***Salem's Lot* miniseries (1979)** — the face at the window, the vampire of the town's haunted house — and the **Hammer Draculas** on late-night TV, where blood wakes the sleeper. [[Russ]] owns all three. Belief beats accuracy: the town's movie rules are its rules.

### The pair, the guard, and the lock
- **The Mercer twins pass anything that casts no reflection.** Ordinary mirrors are not doors; only the pair connects.
- **The dungeon set a guard on its own back door** — and the guard is the one thing that can walk through what it's guarding.
- **It rests in the glass, between the pair.** A resting place, not a place: nobody walks in there and there is no bridge to cross. *(Single town, mega-dungeon.)*
- **The lock: while the guard lives, the mirror cannot leave the dungeon.** This is why the [[The Gremlins|gremlins]] barricaded the approach.
- **Why it woke:** someone tried to take the door. The blood is what it woke hungry for. Awake, hungry, and holding a door that opens onto a town, it goes out through Russ's back room at night and is back in the glass by dawn.

### The gang
They sleep **hanging in the Long Gallery's tall vault** — the *Lost Boys* cave. They came with the guard (the dungeon's permitted extrapolation from the source, like [[The Hush]]'s litter) and woke when it did. **Staking one starts waking the rest.**

> [!warning] One-time — both the guard and the gang
> Neither replenishes. Under the replenish rule a killed creature returns to its original state; a head vampire that comes back would break the *Lost Boys* rule and re-lock the mirror. Run them as the Floor-2 episode boss set.

### The rules, as the town believes them
- **No reflection.**
- **Must be invited into a home.** Panels is a shop, so the back room needs no invitation; houses do.
- **Sunlight destroys; a stake through the heart kills;** holy water and garlic hurt and repel.
- **The head-vampire rule:** kill the head and every half-vampire who hasn't made a first kill comes back.
- **Mercer glass doesn't break.** Russ's hammer rings off it. Nobody kills the guard in the glass.

### What the Long Gallery glass shows

| When | Reflection |
|---|---|
| Before the cold open | The Mercer Mansion drawing room, gas-lit; a figure with its back turned |
| By day, after | The same room; the figure faces you |
| By night | [[Russ]]'s back room — it's out. The moment the party sees this, the episode's two halves connect. |

### Tells topside
Fog seeping from the corner of Russ's mirror after closing. Pale, tired kids at the Friday table. Cole's reflection going thin.

### Stats (open)
Head on a **Vampire Spawn (CR 5)** chassis tuned for a level 3–4 party; the gang on something lighter. Lair behaviour in the Long Gallery: narrow aisle, tall vault, threat from above.

## How it got loose — Wesley's fault

After Session 8 cleared his Room-8 sigil, [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] had no foothold, so he walked down from the entrance. By early–mid July he had reached [[The Long Gallery]] and was working the bolts on the mirror — **item #1 of [[The Three Ritual Items]]**, known to him from the Mercer papers. He cut his palm on the frame. The guard woke and killed him.

> [!danger] Relic ruling (new, 2026-10-17)
> **Killed in the dungeon, Wesley's anti-ember absorbs the death:** no lost day, no lost agency. **He wakes at home, whole** — the same place his exit sigil sends him. Because he walked in from the entrance, the death puts him back at square one: two floors of delve from the thing that killed him. The cost is paid from Wesley himself (see [[Wesley Crane]]) — the next PC who sees him during Ep5 meets a slightly more efficient man. Same lines, delivered faster.

### Why he doesn't go back and kill it
1. **It killed him in one exchange**, and every retry costs a full re-delve with the equinox calendar running.
2. **He doesn't know what it is.** He has never seen *The Lost Boys*. The Nightmare Engine's edge belongs to people who know the catchment, and the answer to this monster is on Russ's VHS wall — the one place in town Wesley has never asked a question. He's as strong as the party and lost because he fought it as a man. **The kids win because they've seen the movie.**
3. **It hunts topside now**, where his relic doesn't protect him — and by day it's in the glass, where nothing reaches it.
4. **So he waits.** Four kids who come back from death keep clearing his problems. He has never asked them for help, and he doesn't need to.

**During the episode:** he re-delves, re-establishes a Floor-2 foothold (location and toll open), and checks the glass by day. When the figure is gone, the guard is dead.

## Zander

**The town liar with one true thing** — see [[Zander Pruitt]]. Fake names by reflex; hustles everybody; fences odds and ends to [[Jerry]]; gives away more than he keeps. Nobody believes him, because about anything else nobody should.

**Rival investigator.** He's been hunting the thing alone on a con artist's budget: a Super Soaker of holy water lifted from the Catholic church's font, garlic in Cole's dinner, NyQuil in his Mountain Dew, sleeping across his brother's door. He fences what he can to Jerry for stakes and a pawn-shop crossbow.

**He lies to the party to protect Cole from them.** They're investigating pale kids, and his brother is one of the things they're hunting.

**The confession beat.** Zander watches Cole try to follow the gang into the Panels glass — and the glass won't take him. *Not yet.* That's how close it is. Zander can't follow (he casts a reflection), so the only way down is the party's. To get their help, the liar has to tell the truth.

**Disposition hinge: believe the liar.** Pitfall: any version of *"prove it."* For [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] this is the foil landing — a person's word against an instrument that can't read it.

## Cole and the clock

**Cole Pruitt** *(placeholder name)*, 17 — the cool one: a car, lake parties, works nights. In July he fell in with a gang at the quarry and drank from the bottle. **Half-vampire, straight *Lost Boys*:** sleeps through the day, sunlight hurts, floats in his sleep, his reflection going thin in the bathroom mirror. No hollowing, no prints.

**The clock is a date:** the gang's feeding night — **a Saturday party at the quarry** — where Cole is expected to make his first kill. Zander overheard it; it's what finally sends him to the party. *Optional give:* each of Zander's scams can buy a night.

- **Win:** the head dies before Cole's first kill, and he comes back.
- **Lose:** Cole is a full vampire. **He isn't dungeon stock** — he sleeps in town and needs no door. The party's choices: **kill Zander's brother**; **let Zander hide him** (the lovable scoundrel becomes a thief of blood); or **let Wesley "take care of him"** — the respectable adult who gives a lost boy a home, which is exactly who the head vampire was in the film.

## Two ways to kill it

| Route | How | What keeps the party off the mirror |
|---|---|---|
| **Dungeon** | By day, stake the gang in the vault. At dusk, fight the guard as it comes out of the glass. | **Put it on quarry night.** The head's death reverts Cole in that instant, but the party is two floors down with no idea whether it worked — and Zander is at the quarry with his brother. |
| **Zander's** | Cover the Panels mirror while it's out. It can't get home, so it runs for a house it's been invited into — the Pruitts', courtesy of Cole. **House siege before dawn.** | The party is never in the gallery. |

> [!tip] Free red herring
> Players who know the film will go looking for a respectable adult who runs a video shop — and the door into town is Russ's back room.

## Ending sequence

1. **The guard dies. The lock opens.**
2. **Wesley lifts the mirror** — behind the party on the dungeon route, or the next morning after a topside kill, once he sees the figure gone from the glass.
3. **He leaves through a fresh exit sigil** drawn where the mirror hung — his new Floor-2 foothold, which also sets up the Gathered Dust harvest.
4. **What the party finds:** a pale, frame-shaped patch in the bas-relief wallpaper and a **live green sigil**. Their own forensics: *the sigil hand took something off a wall.* If they connect it to the cold open: *the man who died here got up.*
5. **The item race begins:** who is doing this, what he's after, and stopping the rest.

> [!tip] If they take the mirror themselves
> If they stay and haul a seven-foot looking-glass up through two floors of gremlin territory, let them. Denying item #1 before the race begins is the biggest win on the board, and the race opens with them holding something the sigil hand needs.

**The pair stays a door.** Wherever the far twin hangs, the Panels twin still opens onto it for anything without a reflection. If Cole turned, he's the only thing left in town that can use it — and the other end now hangs wherever Wesley keeps it.

### The Zander rupture
- **Win branch:** Cole is back, and Zander is still the kid who cried vampire. The party can't back him up without exposing the dungeon. Wesley — who knows exactly what lives in that glass — tells him: *"I believe you."*
- **Lose branch:** whatever the party decides about Cole, Zander's side of it is already chosen.

## The ritual item and the race

- **The Long Gallery twin is item #1 of [[The Three Ritual Items]]**, replacing the Tally Stone (which never appeared in play). Provisional triad retheme: **door / substance / lock**.
- Wesley's pre-Session-1 solo delve was his **first push toward the mirror**, not a Tally Stone retrieval. The mill-area chalk referenced in [[The Hush]] still stands.
- After Ep5, **Gathered Dust and the Weight are both contestable.**

> [!warning] Calendar collision (open)
> Gathered Dust is scheduled for **late July on Floor 2** — the same week and floor as Ep5. **Proposed:** push the harvest to early August so it's the first item the party can actually contest. Flagged, not yet changed, in [[Wesley's Summer Calendar]].
