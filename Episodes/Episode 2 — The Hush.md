# Episode 2 — The Hush

Up: [[Episodes]]

**A standalone Monster-of-the-Week: the dare-night creature comes home. This is where [[Time Points|Town-Time]] is introduced, where the town starts blaming the PCs for [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]]'s disappearance, and where the heroic climax is *available* as a rescue — pulling Ricky out of the [[The Hush|Hush]]'s staging lair before the young are sent to find him. Featured Rival: [[Ricky Vance]] (disposition 2 — Neutral). Wesley fingerprint level: none — the Hush was already loose. **The climax fight is the party's level-2 milestone.**

> [!note] Status: OUTLINE, now running
> Session 3 played the cold open, the reassembly, and the first two Town-Time days — see **[[Episode 2 — The Hush — Play Log]]** for what actually happened and what's still unspent. The Town-Time investigation is mapped in **[[Episode 2 — The Hush — Town-Time Investigation Map]]**.
>
> **Casting locks.** **[[Tank]] & [[Old Man]]** fell (hollowed → lost Day A); **[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] & [[Gern]]** escaped via Wesley's portal. **[[Tank]] is the marked / hunted one; [[Gern]] is the named scapegoat; [[Old Man]] is the party's first monster-tripwire; [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] is [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]]'s cousin.**

## The spine (six pillars)

1. **[[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] as the connector.** The warm through-line who pulls a loose, not-yet-bonded party back together. Unambiguously good news here, and secretly the first turn of the "woman in the van" betrayal. **Fired in Session 3, and then some** — she has the full download from Bash and has provisionally signed on.
2. **The PCs risk getting blamed for Ricky's disappearance.** [[Gern]] is the named scapegoat; all four are tarred as "the mill kids." First sustained social-pressure beat; a personal, no-supernatural-buy-in reason to spend Time Points. **Fired in Session 3** — and it is currently the *only* thread the party is actually pulling on.
3. **[[Tank]] is stalked by the Hush at night.** He carries one dose. **Live as of the Session-3 stinger.** See *The mark*.
4. **Exploration reveals the lore that builds the clock and the world.** The dosing ritual; the [[The Stone of Quietude|stone of quietude]] lead; the [[The Library|Library]]'s disappearance pattern; a first taste of [[The Reality Filter]]. **Largely unspent — see the convergence problem below.**
5. **A delve is on the table** — an optional second run for the stone of quietude. Never required.
6. **The climax brings the kids to the woods**, where — if they earned it — they meet [[Wade Vance]], already hunting the thing. ⚠ **Whether the climax happens in the woods at all is now genuinely open** — see *If they never adopt the rescue*.

## The shape of the session
Cold open (Ricky's capture, third person) → **Day B** (Tank ← Dominique; Old Man ← Petey) → **Day A flashback** (Bash & Gern's first Time Points, the accusation, crossing the hollowed) → **Day-B reconvene** → daytime/nighttime investigation (two tracks) → optional delve → the woods, the lair, Wade, the rescue-fight → aftermath + Ricky's disposition. **Everything through the reconvene is played.**

The investigation is built as **two tracks that converge**: Track A ("Where's Ricky?" — social/blame, daylight) and Track B ("What's hunting me?" — Tank's stalk, after dark). The turn of the episode is the PCs connecting them.

> [!danger] The convergence problem, as of Session 3
> **Both designed routes to the connection are unspent.** [[Panels]]/[[Russ]] was never visited (the Hush is unnamed; *The Quiet Dark* hasn't surfaced), and at the [[The Library|Library]] [[Old Man]] got only *half* the disappearance pattern — the recurring animal-attack deaths, **not** the missing-then-found-dead-a-week-later pairing that makes the clock legible.
>
> Worse, **the party has not adopted "find Ricky" as a goal at all.** They are treating his disappearance as a social problem with a social fix. Their plan is to *fake him being alive*. Nobody has entertained the possibility that something took him.
>
> The good news: **the Patricia branch below is a built-in convergence backstop.** If they never find the pattern or Russ, their own decoy plan drives a frightened mother into the woods, and *that* forces the connection — ugly, earned, and caused by them. You may not need to fix the research gap at all. Let it bite.

## The Patricia branch (the decoy plan's price)

**Player-generated, and now the most likely engine of the back half.** In Session 3 the party invented a **decoy-Ricky plan**: [[Old Man]] casts Disguise Self as Ricky and appears at [[Marco's Diner|Marco's]] to be seen alive, vouch for [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]], and exit with *"my dad's taking me somewhere hush-hush, I won't be around a while."* It is a good plan for the problem they think they have.

**[[Patricia Vance]] will hear that her missing son was seen at the diner, and she will genuinely come apart.** She is the frazzled, present parent; her husband has vanished with a duffel bag of shells; and now the town is telling her Ricky is walking around. She does not read it as good news. She reads it as *everyone is lying to me.*

**The mitigation is a Town-Time visit to Ricky's house — unlocked and unvisited.** A successful visit (calming her, giving her something true to hold, or simply being kind and present) defuses it. This is also the designed source of the **blaze-orange bandana** — the Wade key and the spore mask — so the same trip that prevents a catastrophe also arms the climax. That is the correct shape: *the thing you should have done pays off twice.*

**If they let her fester:** she concludes [[Wade Vance|Wade]] is searching in the wrong place and **goes into the woods to bring him back herself.** She has no training, no Filter resistance beyond a mother's, and no idea what's out there. **She is the one who gets hurt by the Hush** — and *that* becomes the reason [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]] resents the party, **even if they rescue him.**

**Signposting — two nudges, not a neon sign.**
- ✅ **Already delivered:** Tank's defensive coordinator said he'd run into Patricia, she seemed *"real worried,"* and told Tank to go check on him.
- ⚠ **Add one more next session.** [[Jerry]] is the natural second voice — he's already the one who handed them the Wade-and-the-shells lead, he's a Flats fixture, and *"his mother came in here asking if I'd seen Wade"* costs nothing and lands as texture rather than a quest marker.
- Two nudges is enough. If they ignore both, they've chosen, and the consequence is fair.

**Why this branch is better than what it replaces.** The old disposition ladder hung on **[[Wade Vance|Wade]]'s fate in a fight the PCs are already in** — i.e. on combat dice. Patricia moves the causal driver onto **Town-Time attention**: did they notice a frightened woman and go see her? That is exactly what a Persona-layer campaign should be adjudicating, and it makes Ricky's disposition a consequence of *what they cared about* rather than *how the initiative order broke.*

**And note the cruelty of the shape.** The decoy plan was invented to get bullies off Bash's back — a small, real, sympathetic good. Its price is a mother walking into the woods. Nobody at the table will see that coming, and it is entirely their doing. Don't editorialize it; just let it land.

**Timing.** Day B was ~day 2 of Ricky's dosing against a 6–7 day window. The decoy runs Day C morning; Patricia hears the same day; a day or two of festering puts her in the woods around **Day D–E** — comfortably *before* the climax. Consequences: [[Wade Vance|Wade]] is either drawn off the den to deal with her or finds her hurt, which can cost the party their ally *without a single bad roll in the fight.*

## If they never adopt the rescue

**Take seriously that the party may never make "get Ricky out" their quest.** As of Session 3 the only Ricky-shaped problem they recognize is *the football team blames us,* and the decoy solves that. If nothing changes, their encounter with the Hush is driven entirely by **Track B — Tank is being stalked** — and the episode resolves like this:

- **They fight it wherever it finds Tank**, not at the lair. Probably near his house, probably at night, probably on ground they chose. ⚠ **The lair set-piece never fires:** no spore zone, no CON-save DC ladder, no bandana requirement, no day/night lair structure. See the prep note below.
- **[[Wade Vance|Wade]] eventually finds Ricky** — unguarded once the adult is dead, or simply still searching when it dies. The rescue happens offstage, by the person who was actually looking.
- **The PCs get no credit, and correctly so** — they never attempted one.
- **And Ricky's resentment is *true*, not a misreading.** This is the sharp part. The party fleeing down the hatch is *causally why* the adult broke off and took him instead ([[The Hush]]). So Ricky can construct: **"you are the reason it happened to me, and you never came for me."** Both halves are accurate. That is far more corrosive than a misunderstanding, and it is a much stronger shove into [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s arms than any botched fight — because there's nothing to apologize *for* that isn't simply true.
- **It detonates the party's own fracture too.** Tank knows the prank was Ricky's idea; Bash and Gern know Tank was in on it (Tank told Bash in Session 3). "We're the reason he got taken" is a sentence that cuts four ways inside [[The Party]].

**Don't railroad them into the rescue.** This branch is thematically excellent — a campaign about agency should let a party decline a quest and eat the consequence. Just make sure the *information* is reachable (via Patricia if not via Russ or the papers) so the choice is real rather than an oversight.

> [!note] Prep economy — read before building the lair
> The elaborate staging lair (spore zone, DC 8/10/12/13 hazard ladder, the bandana, the day-vs-night structure, the pit) is **contingent prep now, not core prep.** Two of the three live branches — "no rescue" and "Patricia forces the issue in the woods" — may never put the party inside that chamber. Build the **adult Hush fight portable** first: a statblock plus two or three terrain kits (Tank's street at night, a clearing, the den mouth). Build the lair interior only once they show intent to go get him.

## Explanatory notes

### Why the PCs get blamed
Ricky's clique needs a story and builds the cruelest available one: **"You snitched to Ricky's dad about the dare — that's why he got shipped off."** It sticks because **both Ricky *and* his father are missing**, which reads to the town as *his dad took him away.*
- **The named scapegoat is [[Gern]].** No protector, easiest target. **Played:** Blake and Tyler cornered him alone on Day A, he rolled a **3** on Persuasion, and only Bash's mother at the window ended it.
- **The party crucible:** whether they close ranks landed at the Day-B reconvene. **Partially answered** — Tank went to the gym and intimidated the practice squad into standing down, then physically escorted Bash for a day. Gern got protection from the clique and *dismissal* from Tank on the Certaintism front.
- **The mechanical bite:** let the heat reach Gern's parents so they clamp down, costing him Time Points. **Not yet fired** — Gern's strict household is squeezing him at baseline (1 Night TP) but the smear hasn't reached his parents. Still available.
- **The date drops here.** ✅ Delivered — the practice-squad kids said Ricky's been gone *"going on two days now."*

### Ricky's clock — the dosing ritual
Full species detail in [[The Hush]]. The mechanism:
- **The Hush spore-doses a captive roughly once a night.** Each dose poisons them weaker *and* brightens them on the spore-sense. One dose is a bearing; repeated doses are an address. By **day five or six** the prey is weak and loud enough that an **inexperienced young, sent out from the nest, can find and kill it unaided** — a graduation exercise. That window is the clock.
- **The young are never at the staging lair.** They wait at the nest ([[The Pillared Reach]]) until dispatched. This is why the climax is **1 adult vs. 4 PCs, no adds.**
- **The party killed one of the litter** in Session 1, and its dying defensive spore **marked [[Tank]] with one dose.** The ritual is live and completes.
- **The players can watch the mechanism.** *Tank has one dose. Ricky has six.* That symmetry is how the table reasons out the clock — **if they ever get the species fact.** They currently have neither half.
- **The stalk:** the adult homes on Tank's dose because that's the creature that killed one of its young. One dose = prowling the block without pinpointing the window. **Two closes fast; three and it knows where he sleeps.** Track-B ratchet.
- **The horror kicker:** the window is the *outer* bound. It could happen early.

### Reassembly — the staged opening
✅ **Played in Session 3, essentially as designed.** Cold open in third person with the dropped Polaroid; Day B opening on the two who fell (Tank ← Dominique's stoop confrontation; Old Man ← Petey with both payloads); Day A flashback teaching Time Points and running the accusation; Day-B reconvene at the pawn shop and then Bash's house.

**One designed beat didn't fire and one fired better than written.** The two hollowed kids never bumped into each other on a stoop — instead **Bash & Gern tracked hollowed [[Old Man]] into the woods and found him sitting stiff on a boulder,** which is a stronger version of the same lesson. But **hollowed Tank was never witnessed** by anyone, so only Old Man's blankness is on the record. Hollowed [[Wendell Pace|Wendell]] at Marco's carried the rest of that freight.

### The mark ([[Tank]])
**One dose (locked).** Daytime: low-grade deniable wrongness — his own name from an empty room, a dog that won't settle. Night: the stalk goes active.

✅ **Session-3 stinger delivered exactly:** in bed, Tank heard **his own voice, distorted, thrown from no fixed point** (Thrown Voice), then **clicking** rising to right-outside-the-window and receding (spin-and-click at one dose — a bearing on the block, no fix on his window). **He was not re-dosed. Still 1.**

Frame the spotlight as a *party-tactics problem*: Tank is bait and liability. And it's a ratchet — two doses and it closes fast, three and it knows his window.

### The Filter, introduced by texture
Adults are *oddly, wrongly accepting.* The sheriff's line is "wild dog"; the military-school story is swallowed whole; a witness rationalizes it away by morning. **Working already** — Bash's mother bought the sleepover story warmly, and [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] watched Bash conjure a torch in his bare hand and filed it as *a very good magic trick.* [[Russ]] and [[Wade Vance]] are the Filter-*resistant* adults who throw the fog into relief. [[Reverend Hollis]] is a deliberate near-miss: alarmed about the wrong thing.

### The climax — the lair (day or night)
**If they go for Ricky, they fight the adult either way.** The choice isn't *whether* — it's **where**, and **whether the spores are in the fight.** The young are at the nest: **1-vs-4, and the level-2 milestone.** ⚠ **No dungeon safety-net** — topside, death is death. Say it at the table before they go in.

- **The air is the clock, made physical.** Start of each turn in the spore zone: CON save or 1d4 poison — DC **8** (1–2 days) / **10** (3–4) / **12** (5–6) / **13** (7+). Zone = inner chamber plus ~20–30 ft of approach; **clean outside the mouth.** The **blaze-orange bandana** from Patricia is the counter (advantage + resistance); improvised masks +2.
- **NIGHT — the race (easier).** The adult is out hunting Tank; Ricky is unguarded but pit-bound. Extraction means turns in the haze, then the adult finds them **at the mouth, in clean air.** *If Tank isn't in the party it never finds them* — but then Tank is home alone being hunted. **Third path (reward it):** send Tank out as bait elsewhere, ideally with Wade.
- **DAY — fight through (harder).** The adult is home, sleeping over the pit. **The smart hybrid:** draw it into clean air and fight it outside — that converts Day into Night, and it should work.
- **[[Wade Vance]] — if they earned the bandana.** He clocks his son's bandana from cover and breaks cover as an ally. No bandana → no ally Wade. ⚠ **And no bandana if they never visit Patricia** — the two are the same trip.
- **The kill is earned.** **Blind it** — starve its hearing with silence (the Stone) or drown it in noise (firecrackers, a boombox, a car horn) — then use the moment. Blinding is the window, not the kill.
- **Ricky is not hollowed** — a monster's cache, not a dungeon pocket. The abduction is pure terror, and that helplessness is what drives him to [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]].

## Ricky's disposition outcomes

**Set primarily by what the party paid attention to, not by how the fight went.** Three drivers, in descending order of weight:

**1. Did they look after his mother?** ([[Patricia Vance]]) — the new dominant lever.
- **Visited, defused** → she's frightened but held; Wade stays on the den; the bandana is in play. Baseline for any good outcome.
- **Ignored → she goes into the woods → she's hurt** → **Ricky blames the party, and this overrides a clean rescue.** Wary (1), or Hostile (0) if it was bad. *"You made a joke out of me being gone and my mother almost died looking for me."* The most likely bad ending on current trajectory.

**2. Did they try to get him out at all?**
- **Tried and succeeded, cleanly** (Ricky unscathed, Wade unhurt, Patricia safe) → a small **private** uptick. No public warmth ever — admitting the kids he set up saved him would detonate the Iceman image — but later he won't throw a PC under the bus when he easily could. **2 → 3 (Sympathetic).**
- **Never tried; killed the Hush for Tank's sake; Wade found him offstage** → **0 (Hostile), and his grievance is factually correct** — the party is why he was taken and they never came. See *If they never adopt the rescue*. A harder accelerant into Wesley than any botched fight, because there is no misunderstanding to clear up.
- **Never connected the threads at all; left Dad to it** → stays **2 (Neutral)**; no bond, Wesley works him from scratch.

**3. How the fight went** — now the *least* weighted driver.
- **Wade gravely wounded** in the chaos → Wary (1), or Hostile (0) if ugly. Thematically the worst confirmation for him: chaos broke his order-symbol. See [[Ricky Vance]] (the lever).
- **Wade dies** (egregious botch only) → **0 (Hostile)**. *Reserve the father's death for a later, higher-stakes beat.*

Naming the prank / dare night as ammo at any point is a pitfall (shame, doubly loaded — it also exposes the fracture inside [[The Party]]). After this episode [[Wesley Crane]] starts paying attention to Ricky and to the engaged PCs.

## Aftermath & seeds
- **Level 2.** Surviving the adult with no dungeon net is the graduation; award the level here.
- The **adult's** body in dawn light has a **tooth made of something that isn't bone** — dungeon-stone material, a [[Cosmology Seeds|cosmology seed]] for [[Episode 3 — The Click]]. Sheriff's report: "bear."
- **The dropped Polaroid** — fired once as the thing came out the furnace window, now in the weeds by the gate. **Unclaimed.** A blurred corroborator for the Woods/Mill node.
- **The surviving young, still at the nest.** Unprovisioned half-grown Hushes in [[The Pillared Reach]] — a dangling thread, or leave them to starve.
- **Ricky's doses.** He comes home poisoned, spore-marked, changed and withdrawn — which only makes the military-school story feel true.
- A **hooded figure** remains an open question, and [[Reverend Hollis]] has now been *seen circling Wesley* (Session 3 — completely unread by the party; fully reusable).
- **[[Wade Vance]]** has a compact encounter statline and a Tier-3 covert-unit seed. ⚠ Note [[Jerry]] already called him *"one of those hush-hush military badass dudes"* out loud in Session 3, so the *category* is spoiled; the den scene should now deliver **scale** instead — he's already mapped the place before they arrive.
