# Episode 2 — The Hush

Up: [[Episodes]]

**A standalone Monster-of-the-Week: the dare-night creature comes home. This is where [[Time Points|Town-Time]] is introduced, where the town starts blaming the PCs for [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]]'s disappearance, and where the heroic climax is a rescue — pulling Ricky out of the [[The Hush|Hush]]'s staging lair before the young are sent to find him. Featured Rival: [[Ricky Vance]] (disposition 2 — Neutral, shaped by the rescue). Wesley fingerprint level: none. **The climax fight is the party's level-2 milestone.**

> [!note] Status: IN PLAY — the opening is run (Session 3)
> The staged opening, the accusation, and the first Town-Time turns have been played. What remains: the investigation nodes, the **disguise-gambit fallout**, the **[[Patricia Vance]] fork**, and the climax. Node-by-node detail in **[[Episode 2 — The Hush — Town-Time Investigation Map]]**; the beat-by-beat run-sheet and the Part-2 DM screen carry the live version.
>
> **Casting locks.** **[[Tank]]** = marked / hunted · **[[Gern]]** = named scapegoat · **[[Old Man]]** = first monster-tripwire · **[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]** = [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]]'s cousin.

## The spine (six pillars)

1. **[[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] as the connector.** The warm through-line who pulls a loose party together — worried for her cousin Bash, furious at Tank. Unambiguously good news here, and secretly the first turn of the "woman in the van" betrayal; this episode is where the players fall for Free-Dominique. *(The disguise gambit gives her a perfect showcase — see below.)*
2. **The PCs risk getting blamed for Ricky's disappearance.** [[Gern]] is the named scapegoat; all four are tarred as "the mill kids." First sustained social-pressure beat; a personal, no-supernatural-buy-in reason to spend Time Points — **and the pressure the players' own counter-plan grows out of.**
3. **[[Tank]] is stalked by the Hush at night** — he carries one spore dose from the juvenile the party killed. The mark is the supernatural thread that converges on the lair.
4. **Exploration reveals the lore that builds the clock and the world.** The discoverable **dosing ritual**; the [[The Stone of Quietude|stone of quietude]] lead; the [[The Library|Library]]'s disappearance pattern; the first taste of [[The Reality Filter]]. Plus [[The School Gym]] — the one node that sells a *mechanical edge* rather than information.
5. **A delve is on the table** — an optional run for the stone, never required. (Deliberately off Wesley's Session-1 trail.)
6. **The climax brings the kids to the woods**, where — if they earned it — they meet [[Wade Vance]]. His condition, and whether he's there at all, is set by how they handled [[Patricia Vance]].

## The shape of the session
Cold open (Ricky's capture, third person — the Polaroid) → **Day B** confrontations ([[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]]↔Tank; Petey↔Old Man) → **Day A flashback** (Bash & Gern's first Time-Points, the accusation, the hollowed encounter) → **Day-B reconvene** → **the two tracks** (daylight blame / nighttime stalk) + the party's own counter-plays → optional stone delve → the woods, the lair, the rescue-fight → aftermath + Ricky's disposition.

**Two tracks that converge:** Track A ("Where's Ricky?" — social/blame, daylight) and Track B ("What's hunting me?" — Tank's stalk, after dark). The turn is the PCs connecting them.

## Explanatory notes

### Why the PCs get blamed
Ricky's clique needs a story, and builds the cruelest available one: **"You snitched to Ricky's dad about the dare — that's why he got shipped off to a military-style camp."** It sticks because **both Ricky *and* his father are missing** (Wade is off hunting), which reads to the town as *"his dad took him away."*
- **The named scapegoat is [[Gern]]** — no protector, easiest target, and the most on-theme: he took the dare at face value. The smear covers "those mill kids," so Dominique intervenes for Bash anyway.
- **The mechanical bite:** the heat reaches Gern's parents and they clamp down — grounding costs him **Time Points** (debuting the strict/lax modifier).
- **The date drops here** — the smear names *when* Ricky vanished, handing the players the clock's anchor.

### The disguise gambit (player-invented, Session 3)
The party's counter-play: **fake a Ricky sighting** — one of them made up and dressed as Ricky, seen at a distance — to kill the smear.
- **The tell is behavior, not the face.** The clique doesn't know Ricky by his features; they know his walk (easy, unhurried, like the place belongs to him), his laugh, and a dozen in-jokes. The makeup can be flawless and it dies at *"Rick, tell 'em about the thing at Sinclair."* **DCs:** adults or distance **10** · kids who don't know him **12** · the clique or Dominique **18** — and **no roll survives sustained conversation.** It buys minutes, not a relationship.
- **⭐ Two beats fire regardless of the roll.**
  - **The inversion.** Adults buy it completely — a teacher, a shopkeeper, Marco: *"Hey Ricky, tell your mom I said hi."* Nobody looks twice. The players will read it as the [[The Reality Filter|Filter]] and it will chill them. **It isn't** — it's just adults not looking. But they can't tell the difference, and that's the beat: grown-ups in this town don't see kids clearly. The thesis, delivered as a joke that isn't funny.
  - **[[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] sees through it instantly — and covers.** She clocks it across a parking lot, says nothing, steers the clique off, then corners them: *"What are you DOING?"* She's the reason it didn't blow up. **This is the Free-Dominique beat the betrayal arc needs** — the table will love her for it, which is exactly the point.
- **If it fails,** tells in ascending cruelty: the jacket has no tear (the real scrap is on the mill fence) · a name the impostor doesn't have · someone who's known him since kindergarten · *nastiest,* the clique clocks it in three seconds and **plays along,** feeding him rope before springing it in front of an audience. **Consequence: it upgrades the accusation** from gossip to *"why are you pretending to be him? What did you do?"* — the version that can reach an adult, including Patricia.
- **If it works,** heat drops (bullies pivot to *"so where is he, then?"*) — enough to **lift Gern's grounding and restore his TP.** But **the rumor travels, and the party has just done the Filter's job for it:** the town now has a tidy explanation for a boy who is in a hole in the woods being dosed nightly. Nobody is looking. *(Let a player realize this out loud.)* And it reaches **[[Patricia Vance]]** within a day.

### The Patricia fork — the episode's moral hinge
If the gambit worked, **[[Patricia Vance]]** believes her son is alive and walking around town, and she will come find the party. Everything downstream hangs on whether they correct her.
- **Truth** → brutal to do, and it must be the *better* path or the game punishes honesty. She becomes the one adult who knows and believes: the bandana freely given, plus **Wade's truck and his working sector — one free success on the lair search.**
- **Let her believe** → she's a quest-giver with a **false quest:** bring Ricky home, and *tell Wade he's searching the wrong place.* They get the bandana under false pretenses — setting up a hell of a moment when they meet Wade. **Delivering that message costs Wade a condition band** (Sharp→Worn, Worn→Spent): he breaks off a correct search to check town.
- **Never come / never engage** → the **Bystander** branch (see the disposition outcomes).

### Ricky's clock — the dosing ritual
- **What a Hush does with the taken:** it **spore-doses the captive roughly once a night.** Each dose **poisons them weaker** *and* **makes them blaze brighter on the spore-sense** — one dose is a rough bearing, repeated doses an address. By **day five or six** the prey is weak and loud enough that an **inexperienced young, sent out from the nest, can find and kill it unaided.** That ritual — a graduation exercise for the litter — is the clock.
- **The young are never at the staging lair;** they must *travel and find* their prey. Hence the climax is a genuine **1 adult vs. 4 PCs, no adds.**
- **The specific situation:** in [[Session 1 — The Dare|Session 1]] the party killed one of the litter, and its dying spore **marked [[Tank]] with one dose.** The rest survive, so the ritual completes.
- **The players can watch the mechanism.** *Tank has one dose. Ricky has six.*
- **The stalk:** the adult homes on Tank's dose because that's what killed its young. **If it catches him alone at night it doses him again** — two and it closes fast, **three and it knows his window.** The Track-B ratchet; waiting is not free.
- **Horror kicker:** the window is the *outer* bound — the adult could decide Ricky is bright enough and dispatch the young *early.*

### The mark ([[Tank]])
Daytime: low-grade, deniable wrongness — his own name from an empty room, a dog that won't settle. Night: the stalk goes active and bridges to the lair. Frame it as a *party-tactics problem* — Tank is bait and liability. He can't self-diagnose; the others can find it. *(The [[The School Gym]] is where he goes to steady himself — the one room where he knows who he is, and also Ricky's room.)*

### The Filter, introduced by texture
Adults are *oddly, wrongly accepting.* The sheriff's "wild dog"; the military-school story swallowed whole; a witness who rationalized it away by morning. Felt, not explained. Pairs with [[Russ]] and [[Wade Vance]] as the two Filter-*resistant* adults, and with the gym coach as a deliberate **near-miss** (accepts the story, still sets out Ricky's gear). ([[Reverend Hollis]] is the other near-miss: alarmed about the wrong thing.)

### The climax — the lair (day or night)
**They fight the adult either way.** The choice is **where,** and **whether the spores are in the fight.** ⚠ **No dungeon safety-net** — topside, so death is *death.* Say it before they go in.
- **The air is the clock made physical.** Start of each turn in the spore zone: **CON save or 1d4 poison** — DC **8** (1–2 days) / **10** (3–4) / **12** (5–6) / **13** (7+). Zone = inner chamber + ~20–30 ft of tunnel; **clean outside the mouth.** The **blaze-orange bandana** is the counter (advantage + resistance); improvised masks +2.
- **NIGHT — the race (easier).** The adult is out hunting Tank. Ricky is unguarded but **pit-bound,** so the extraction means turns in the haze; then the adult, homing on Tank's dose, finds them **at the mouth, in clean air.** *If Tank isn't in the party it never finds them* — but Tank is then home alone. **Third path (reward it):** Tank as bait *elsewhere,* ideally with Wade.
- **DAY — fight through (harder).** The adult sleeps over the pit; they must beat it **inside the spore zone,** confined, with the pit at their backs. **Smart hybrid:** draw it into clean air and fight it outside.
- **[[Wade Vance]]** — if they earned the bandana. He clocks his son's blaze orange from cover and breaks cover as an ally. Condition set by days elapsed, the approach, and whether they lied to Patricia.
- **The kill is earned.** Blind it (silence or overwhelming noise), then use the moment — focused fire, a chokepoint, or terrain the way *The Quiet Dark* uses a cliff.
- **Most of the glory goes to Dad** publicly. **Ricky is not hollowed** — pure terror and total helplessness, which is what later drives him to [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]].
- Statblock, hazard ladder, and difficulty dials: [[The Hush]] and the DM-screen addendum.

## Ricky's disposition outcomes
- **Clean** (Ricky unscathed, Dad unhurt) → a private uptick. **2 → 3 (Sympathetic).**
- **Messy** (Dad gravely wounded) → he blames the party → **1 (Wary)**, or **0** if ugly.
- **Disaster** (egregious botch) → **Dad dies** → **0 (Hostile)**. *Reserve it.*
- **Bystander** *(Option B — they never went for Ricky and fought the Hush only because it hunted Tank)* → **[[Patricia Vance|Patricia]]** went into the woods after Wade, hit the spore haze near the lair, and collapsed. Wade had to choose — son or wife — got Ricky out, came back for her. Ricky comes home to a **hospitalized mother**: *"my mom got hurt while you were playing monster hunters."* → **2 → 1 (Wary)**, or **0 (Hostile)** if she's badly hurt. A clean accelerant into Wesley. *(The level-2 milestone still lands.)*
- **Disengaged** (never connected the threads at all) → stays **2 (Neutral)**.

## Aftermath & seeds
- **Level 2.** Surviving the adult with no dungeon net is the graduation.
- The adult's body has a **tooth made of something that isn't bone** — a [[Cosmology Seeds|cosmology seed]] for [[Episode 3 — The Click]]. Sheriff's report: "bear."
- **The dropped Polaroid** — a blurred shot of *something* half-out the furnace window; a plantable Woods-node corroborator.
- **The surviving young, still at the nest** ([[The Pillared Reach]]) — never dispatched, unprovisioned; a thread to reintroduce or leave to starve.
- **Ricky's doses.** He comes home poisoned and spore-marked — decide whether the marking fades. Changed and withdrawn, which only makes the "military school" story feel true.
- **If the disguise worked,** the town's tidy false story persists — worth remembering when the next kid goes missing.
- A **hooded figure** remains an open question; [[Reverend Hollis]] has been seen circling Wesley too.
- **[[Wade Vance]]** has a compact encounter statline and a Tier-3 covert-unit seed.
