# Episode 2 — The Hush

Up: [[Episodes]]

**A standalone Monster-of-the-Week: the dare-night creature comes home. This is where [[Time Points|Town-Time]] is introduced, where the town starts blaming the PCs for [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]]'s disappearance, and where the heroic climax is a rescue — pulling Ricky out of the [[The Hush|Hush]]'s staging lair before the young are sent to find him. Featured Rival: [[Ricky Vance]] (disposition 2 — Neutral, shaped by the rescue). Wesley fingerprint level: none. **The climax fight is the party's level-2 milestone.**

> [!note] Status: IN PLAY — the opening is run (Session 3)
> The staged opening, the accusation, and the first Town-Time turns have been played. What remains: the investigation nodes, the **disguise-gambit encounter**, the **[[Patricia Vance]] fork**, and the climax. Node-by-node detail and the **full disguise procedure** live in **[[Episode 2 — The Hush — Town-Time Investigation Map]]**.
>
> **Casting locks.** **[[Tank]]** = marked / hunted · **[[Gern]]** = named scapegoat · **[[Old Man]]** = first monster-tripwire · **[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]** = [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]]'s cousin.

> [!warning] Player-knowledge boundary — the Filter is NOT on the table
> The players know **nothing** about [[The Reality Filter]]; it has not been introduced or teased. **No beat may depend on them recognizing it.** Adult obliviousness reads to the table as ordinary adult obliviousness — write it and run it that way. Filter-shaped material here is a **plant for later,** never a payoff now. (This is the episode's own stated design line: *felt before it's understood,* never named.)

## The spine (six pillars)

1. **[[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] as the connector.** The warm through-line who pulls a loose party together — worried for her cousin Bash, furious at Tank. Unambiguously good news here, and secretly the first turn of the "woman in the van" betrayal; this episode is where the players fall for Free-Dominique. *(The disguise gambit gives her a perfect showcase.)*
2. **The PCs risk getting blamed for Ricky's disappearance.** [[Gern]] is the named scapegoat; all four are tarred as "the mill kids." First sustained social-pressure beat — **and the pressure the players' own counter-plan grows out of.**
3. **[[Tank]] is stalked by the Hush at night** — he carries one spore dose from the juvenile the party killed.
4. **Exploration reveals the lore that builds the clock and the world.** The **dosing ritual**; the [[The Stone of Quietude|stone of quietude]] lead; the [[The Library|Library]]'s disappearance pattern; the first *texture* of [[The Reality Filter]] (unnamed). Plus [[The School Gym]] — the one node that sells a *mechanical edge* rather than information.
5. **A delve is on the table** — an optional run for the stone, never required.
6. **The climax brings the kids to the woods**, where — if they earned it — they meet [[Wade Vance]]. His condition, and whether he's there at all, is set by how they handled [[Patricia Vance]].

## The shape of the session
Cold open (Ricky's capture, third person — the Polaroid) → **Day B** confrontations → **Day A flashback** (first Time-Points, the accusation, the hollowed encounter) → **Day-B reconvene** → **the two tracks** (daylight blame / nighttime stalk) + the party's own counter-plays → optional stone delve → the woods, the lair, the rescue-fight → aftermath + Ricky's disposition.

**Two tracks that converge:** Track A ("Where's Ricky?" — social/blame, daylight) and Track B ("What's hunting me?" — Tank's stalk, after dark).

## Explanatory notes

### Why the PCs get blamed
Ricky's clique needs a story, and builds the cruelest available one: **"You snitched to Ricky's dad about the dare — that's why he got shipped off to a military-style camp."** It sticks because **both Ricky *and* his father are missing**, which reads to the town as *"his dad took him away."*
- **The named scapegoat is [[Gern]]** — no protector, easiest target, most on-theme. The smear covers "those mill kids," so Dominique intervenes for Bash anyway.
- **The mechanical bite:** the heat reaches Gern's parents — grounding costs him **Time Points** (debuting the strict/lax modifier).
- **The date drops here** — the smear names *when* Ricky vanished, handing the players the clock's anchor.

### The disguise gambit (player-invented, Session 3)
[[Old Man]] impersonates Ricky to fake a sighting and lift the smear. **They get a real chance to land it:** a good story **plus** two good rolls, story weighted over dice. *(Full procedure — rubric, DCs, question bank, extraction menu — in [[Episode 2 — The Hush — Town-Time Investigation Map]].)*

- **⭐ The exit ramp is the load-bearing gate.** If the story has no reason "Ricky" must leave *right now,* succeeding at the con is what **traps** them — the jocks believe him, they're thrilled, and they won't let go. **No exit ramp → full success is off the table** regardless of the dice. That's what makes the partial outcome earned rather than arbitrary.
- **Two gates:** the pitch (**Deception/Performance DC 14** — the jocks *want* Ricky to be fine) and one follow-up question (**Deception DC 16** — scrutiny is up). A five-point story rubric grants **advantage on both** at 3+. *(This supersedes the earlier "clique = DC 18, no roll survives conversation" ruling, which was too punitive.)*
- **Live tension:** the mill kids must be *present* for the vouch to land — and their presence is what makes the jocks look closer.
- **⭐ Two beats fire regardless.** (1) **The inversion, played entirely mundane:** adults buy it completely (*"Hey Ricky, tell your mom I said hi"*) — no wrongness, no chill, the lesson is simply *grown-ups do not see us.* Play for **the laugh that curdles.** It's a **plant, not a payoff** — a memory that re-colors whenever the Filter surfaces. (2) **[[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] clocks it instantly and covers for them,** then corners them: *"What are you DOING?"* — the Free-Dominique beat the betrayal arc needs.
- **FULL SUCCESS** → the smear collapses (**lift Gern's grounding, restore TP**); Tank's standing with the team is fine or improved. **The cost stands:** the town now has a reason to **stop looking** for a boy in a hole being dosed nightly — plain logic, no cosmology needed. Reaches **[[Patricia Vance]]** within a day.
- **PARTIAL → an extraction mission.** The jocks won't release "Ricky"; they want to walk him somewhere and get the photo, and it **outruns the one-hour spell.** They take him to [[The School Gym]]; the rest of the party has to pull him out before the illusion drops. *(Optional complication, DM's call: the coach sees "Ricky," buys it, and **calls Patricia** — which fires the fork on the DM's timer instead of the party's.)*
- **FAILURE → jock ire, no brawl.** **[[Old Man]] is squarely in it now** (a named second offender who did something actively weird and cruel — with the odd upside that Gern is no longer isolated *alone*), it can reach adults (the route to Patricia getting the ugly version), and **[[Tank]]'s standing with the team goes in jeopardy** — narrative pressure, deliberately **not** a tracked mechanic.
- **The photo:** the real Polaroid shows a blurred shape half-out the furnace window, *not* the prank. Producing it is a gamble; not having it is a leash (*"then let's go get it"*).

### The Patricia fork — the episode's moral hinge
If the gambit worked, **[[Patricia Vance]]** believes her son is alive and walking around town, and she will come find the party.
- **Truth** → brutal to do, and it must be the *better* path or the game punishes honesty. She becomes the one adult who knows and believes: the bandana freely given, plus **Wade's truck and his working sector — one free success on the lair search.**
- **Let her believe** → she's a quest-giver with a **false quest:** bring Ricky home, and *tell Wade he's searching the wrong place.* They get the bandana under false pretenses. **Delivering that message costs Wade a condition band** (Sharp→Worn, Worn→Spent).
- **Never come / never engage** → the **Bystander** branch.

### Ricky's clock — the dosing ritual
- **What a Hush does with the taken:** it **spore-doses the captive roughly once a night.** Each dose **poisons them weaker** *and* **makes them blaze brighter on the spore-sense** — one dose is a rough bearing, repeated doses an address. By **day five or six** the prey is weak and loud enough that an **inexperienced young, sent out from the nest, can find and kill it unaided.** That ritual is the clock.
- **The young are never at the staging lair;** they must *travel and find* their prey. Hence a genuine **1 adult vs. 4 PCs, no adds.**
- **The specific situation:** in [[Session 1 — The Dare|Session 1]] the party killed one of the litter, and its dying spore **marked [[Tank]] with one dose.**
- **The players can watch the mechanism.** *Tank has one dose. Ricky has six.*
- **The stalk:** the adult homes on Tank's dose. **If it catches him alone at night it doses him again** — two and it closes fast, **three and it knows his window.** Waiting is not free.
- **Horror kicker:** the window is the *outer* bound — the young could be dispatched *early.*

### The mark ([[Tank]])
Daytime: low-grade, deniable wrongness — his own name from an empty room, a dog that won't settle. Night: the stalk goes active. Frame it as a *party-tactics problem* — Tank is bait and liability. He can't self-diagnose; the others can find it. *(The [[The School Gym]] is where he goes to steady himself — the one room where he knows who he is, and also Ricky's room.)*

### The Filter, introduced by texture — and never named
Adults are *oddly, wrongly accepting.* The sheriff's "wild dog"; the military-school story swallowed whole; a witness who rationalized it away by morning; a coach who still sets out a missing boy's gear; a whole clique that buys a kid in a costume. **These are DM-facing labels only.** To the players it is simply a town of unhelpful grown-ups, which is an ordinary thing to be twelve and believe. Pairs with [[Russ]] and [[Wade Vance]] as the two Filter-*resistant* adults — again, unlabelled. Nothing this episode should invite the table to theorize about a fog; the accumulation is for later.

### The climax — the lair (day or night)
**They fight the adult either way.** The choice is **where,** and **whether the spores are in the fight.** ⚠ **No dungeon safety-net** — topside, so death is *death.* Say it before they go in.
- **The air is the clock made physical.** Start of each turn in the spore zone: **CON save or 1d4 poison** — DC **8** (1–2 days) / **10** (3–4) / **12** (5–6) / **13** (7+). Zone = inner chamber + ~20–30 ft of tunnel; **clean outside the mouth.** The **blaze-orange bandana** is the counter (advantage + resistance); improvised masks +2.
- **NIGHT — the race (easier).** The adult is out hunting Tank. Ricky is unguarded but **pit-bound,** so extraction means turns in the haze; then the adult, homing on Tank's dose, finds them **at the mouth, in clean air.** *If Tank isn't in the party it never finds them* — but Tank is then home alone. **Third path (reward it):** Tank as bait *elsewhere,* ideally with Wade.
- **DAY — fight through (harder).** The adult sleeps over the pit; they must beat it **inside the spore zone,** confined, with the pit at their backs. **Smart hybrid:** draw it into clean air and fight it outside.
- **[[Wade Vance]]** — if they earned the bandana. He clocks his son's blaze orange from cover and breaks cover as an ally.
- **The kill is earned.** Blind it (silence or overwhelming noise), then use the moment — focused fire, a chokepoint, or terrain the way *The Quiet Dark* uses a cliff.
- **Most of the glory goes to Dad** publicly. **Ricky is not hollowed** — pure terror and total helplessness, which is what later drives him to [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]].
- Statblock, hazard ladder, and difficulty dials: [[The Hush]] and the DM-screen addendum.

## Ricky's disposition outcomes
- **Clean** (Ricky unscathed, Dad unhurt) → a private uptick. **2 → 3 (Sympathetic).**
- **Messy** (Dad gravely wounded) → he blames the party → **1 (Wary)**, or **0** if ugly.
- **Disaster** (egregious botch) → **Dad dies** → **0 (Hostile)**. *Reserve it.*
- **Bystander** *(Option B — they never went for Ricky and fought the Hush only because it hunted Tank)* → **[[Patricia Vance|Patricia]]** went into the woods after Wade, hit the spore haze near the lair, and collapsed. Wade had to choose — son or wife — got Ricky out, came back for her. Ricky comes home to a **hospitalized mother**: *"my mom got hurt while you were playing monster hunters."* → **2 → 1 (Wary)**, or **0 (Hostile)** if she's badly hurt. *(The level-2 milestone still lands.)*
- **Disengaged** (never connected the threads at all) → stays **2 (Neutral)**.

## Aftermath & seeds
- **Level 2.** Surviving the adult with no dungeon net is the graduation.
- The adult's body has a **tooth made of something that isn't bone** — a [[Cosmology Seeds|cosmology seed]] for [[Episode 3 — The Click]]. Sheriff's report: "bear."
- **The dropped Polaroid** — a blurred shot of *something* half-out the furnace window (and a prop the party may have burned on the disguise gambit).
- **The surviving young, still at the nest** ([[The Pillared Reach]]) — never dispatched; a thread to reintroduce or leave to starve.
- **Ricky's doses.** He comes home poisoned and spore-marked; changed and withdrawn, which only makes the "military school" story feel true.
- **If the disguise worked,** the town's tidy false story persists — worth remembering when the next kid goes missing, and a clean **retroactive plant** for whenever the Filter surfaces.
- **If it failed,** the party is socially poorer going into the climax, and [[Tank]]'s place on the team is in question.
- A **hooded figure** remains an open question; [[Reverend Hollis]] has been seen circling Wesley too.
- **[[Wade Vance]]** has a compact encounter statline and a Tier-3 covert-unit seed.
