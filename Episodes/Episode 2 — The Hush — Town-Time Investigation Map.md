# Episode 2 — The Hush — Town-Time Investigation Map

Up: [[Episode 2 — The Hush]]

**The [[Time Points|Town-Time]] investigation for [[Episode 2 — The Hush]]: two parallel tracks that converge, bracketed by a staged opening and a night climax. Locks the information inventory, the core/unlock node set (with day/night availability), the placements, and the casting.**

> [!note] Post-Session-3 status
> The **opening beats are run.** What remains: the Town-Time nodes below, the **disguise-gambit encounter**, the **[[Patricia Vance]] fork**, and the climax. A Part-2 DM screen (locations + confrontation only) carries the live version.

> [!warning] Player-knowledge boundary — the Filter is NOT on the table
> The players know **nothing** about [[The Reality Filter]]; it hasn't been introduced or even teased. **No beat in this episode may depend on them recognizing it.** Per the episode's own design line, the Filter is *felt before it's understood* and never named — adult obliviousness reads to the table as ordinary adult obliviousness, and should be written and run that way. Anything Filter-shaped here is a **plant for later,** not a payoff now.

## Casting locks
**[[Tank]]** = the marked / hunted one · **[[Gern]]** = the named scapegoat · **[[Old Man]]** = the party's first monster-tripwire · **[[Bash Whitlock|Bash]]** = [[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]]'s cousin (the reassembly's blood tie).

## The two-track spine
- **Track A — "Where's Ricky?"** Blame, "military school," the scared mother, the missing father ([[Wade Vance|Wade]]). Human drama; **runs in daylight (Day TP).**
- **Track B — "What's hunting me?"** [[Tank]] carries one spore dose and is stalked. **Runs after dark (Night TP).**
- **Convergence = C0.** The thing hunting Tank is what took Ricky, and he's on a clock. *(Cleanest route in: Tank has **one** dose, Ricky has **six** — same mechanism, six days apart.)*

## Design constraints
- **Budget:** 4 Day / 2 Night per PC ([[Time Points]]); threshold pricing 1 / 2 / 3–4; a delve is a flat 3. More nodes than one party can comb → forced split-up.
- **Tracks split across the clock:** A = Day, B = Night. Night is only 2 points and dangerous, so each night spend is a bet.
- **Availability:** ☀ day-only · 🌙 night-only · ☀🌙 both · *flips* · *earned* (rapport unlocks after-hours).
- **Nightmare-Engine redundancy:** [[Russ]] is the cheap kill path; the **[[The Library|Library]] folklore shelf** is the backup.
- **Negotiation stays hidden** (per [[The Negotiation System]]); Ricky's disposition moves invisibly.
- **Waiting is not free.** Days spent = another dose in Ricky (weaker, brighter), a thicker spore hazard in the lair, and a chance the adult doses Tank again. See [[The Hush]].
- **One node sells a mechanical edge, not information.** The [[The School Gym|School Gym]] exists so a dry stretch still has something worth buying.
- **Nothing pays off on cosmology the players don't have.** Every consequence in this episode must be reachable by plain kid-logic.

## The disguise gambit (player-invented, Session 3) — LOCKED PROCEDURE
[[Old Man]] impersonates [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]] (Disguise Self) to fake a sighting and lift the smear. **Give them a real chance to land it:** the bar is a good story **plus** two good rolls, and the story matters more than the dice.

### The bar — fiction first, then dice
**⭐ The exit ramp is the real gate.** If their story has no reason "Ricky" must leave *right now,* then succeeding at the con is exactly what traps them — the jocks believe him, they're thrilled, and they won't let go. That isn't the lie failing; it's the lie working too well with **no way out.** So **without an exit ramp, full success is off the table no matter how well they roll** — which is what makes the partial outcome feel earned rather than arbitrary.

Listen for these **five** before any roll:
1. **Accounts for the absence** — fits what the town already believes, or plausibly corrects it.
2. **⭐ Builds the exit ramp** — a reason he must go, immediately, that the jocks will accept.
3. **Vouches for the mill kids without gushing** — *"they're not worth the trouble"* is in character; *"they're good guys actually"* is not.
4. **No checkable specifics** — no names, no places the jocks can call, no *"ask my dad."*
5. **Stays in Ricky's register** — easy, unhurried, a little cruel, like the place belongs to him.

**Hit 3+ → advantage on both rolls** (or −2 to each DC).

### The two gates
| | The moment | Check | DC |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gate 1** | The approach and opening pitch | Deception (or Performance if played as a bit) | **14** — the jocks *want* Ricky to be fine; they aren't suspicious yet |
| **Gate 2** | The follow-up question | Deception (allow Insight first, to read what answer the asker is fishing for) | **16** — scrutiny is up |

One PC may **Help** if plausibly assisting (Tank running interference, Bash coughing a prompt). **Live tension:** the mill kids must be *present* for the vouch to land at all — and their presence is what makes the jocks look closer. Let them feel it; don't resolve it for them.

**Read the result**
- Both gates **+ exit ramp** → **FULL SUCCESS**
- Both gates, **no exit ramp** → **PARTIAL** (they believe him and won't let him go)
- Gate 1 passes, Gate 2 **misses by 1–4** → **PARTIAL** (enthusiasm, not suspicion, is the problem)
- Gate 1 **fails,** or Gate 2 **misses by 5+** → **FAILURE**

### Follow-up bank (pick the one aimed at whatever the story left thin)
- *"Where the hell have you been?"* — their story should already cover it.
- *"Rick, tell 'em about the thing at Sinclair."* — the in-joke trap. **No correct answer exists;** only deflection in character survives.
- *"Your old man let you out?"* — brushes Wade, and dangerous, because Wade is genuinely missing.
- *"You coming to two-a-days? Coach has been asking."* — drags in the team and the coach.
- *"So where's the picture?"* — the money question, and the hook into partial.

### The photo
The real Polaroid **doesn't show the prank** — it shows a blurred shape half-out the furnace window.
- **If they have it,** producing it is a gamble: on a success the blur reads as *"flash didn't catch it"* — a lame, disappointing photo, which **helps,** because disappointment ends conversations. On a failure the jocks squint and something in it is wrong.
- **If they don't,** *"it's at home"* is the natural dodge — and the leash: *"Then let's go get it."*

### Two beats that fire regardless of the roll
1. **The inversion — run it entirely mundane.** Adults buy it completely (*"Hey Ricky, tell your mom I said hi"*). No wrongness, no chill, nothing supernatural: the lesson is simply *grown-ups do not see us.* True to twelve, it stings, and it rhymes with everything the week already taught them. Also **actionable** — they can move through adult spaces more freely than they thought. **Play for the laugh that curdles:** someone jokes, then someone quiet says *"wait — so if something happened to one of us, would anybody even…"* **It's a plant, not a payoff** — when [[The Reality Filter|the Filter]] eventually surfaces ([[Russ]]'s break being likeliest), this memory re-colors, and they'll misremember it as evidence though it was mundane.
2. **[[Dominique Ferraro|Dominique]] clocks it instantly and covers for them** — steers the clique off, then corners them: *"What are you DOING?"* The Free-Dominique beat the long arc needs.

### FULL SUCCESS
The jocks believe Ricky is fine, that he vouched for the mill kids, and that he had to run off.
- **The smear collapses:** lift **[[Gern]]'s grounding / restore TP**; Old Man and Bash are clear too.
- **[[Tank]]'s standing with the team is fine** — arguably improved, since "Ricky" publicly backed his call.
- **The cost stands:** the town now has a reason to **stop looking** for a boy who is in a hole being dosed nightly *(plain logic — a player can reach it unaided; if nobody does, let Dominique or Patricia deliver the innocent version — "thank God, everyone can stop worrying now" — and let it sit)*. Then it reaches **[[Patricia Vance]]** within a day.

### PARTIAL → the extraction mission
The jocks won't let "Ricky" go — they want to walk him somewhere, see him off, get the photo, and the whole thing outruns the spell.
- **The clock: Disguise Self runs ONE HOUR.** Budget it out loud — pitch ~10 min, follow-ups ~10, the jocks' plan 30–45. **The illusion drops mid-event.** Give the impersonator's player a spoken minute-count at every beat.
- **Where they take him: [[The School Gym]].** Already a node, where the team is in summer, and loaded — the trophy case with his own face in it, his locker.
- **◇ Optional complication (DM's call — it moves the Patricia fork onto *your* timer instead of the party's):** if **the coach** sees "Ricky," he buys it completely, then does what a decent adult does — **calls Patricia,** or Wade's house. The party is suddenly racing a phone call, and the fork fires whether they chose it or not. Great tension; decline it if you'd rather the fork stay in the players' hands.
- **The rest of the party's job — get Old Man out without breaking the illusion.** All legitimate: manufactured emergency (Deception / Sleight of Hand **13**) · a mom's voice calling for Ricky from a distance (Deception **15**; great for a voice-thrower) · kill the gym lights / trip the breaker (Investigation or tools **13**) · **ask Dominique — no roll,** she already knows, and it's the second time she saves them · Tank leans on team standing (Persuasion **14**, and it *spends* that standing) · just bolt (Athletics **12** — works, reads bizarre, costs social ground).
- **If the spell drops in front of the jocks:** the useful consequence isn't supernatural exposure — their account comes out so **garbled that nobody believes a word of it.** The social damage sticks; the impossible part doesn't. *(DM-facing: log it. Don't gesture at it.)*

### FAILURE — jock ire, no brawl
It escalates the social problem rather than starting a fight.
- **[[Old Man]] is squarely in it now** — a named second offender who did something actively weird and cruel. Odd upside: **[[Gern]] is no longer isolated *alone,*** a small bonding win inside a loss.
- **It can reach adults** — impersonating a missing boy is what parents hear about. The route to **Patricia getting the ugly version.**
- **[[Tank]]'s standing with the team goes in jeopardy** — **narrative pressure, deliberately NOT a tracked mechanic;** it does not gate the gym boon. Promotable later if play demands it (see Open items).

## The Patricia fork
- **Truth** → she believes them; gives the bandana freely; hands over **Wade's truck and sector = one free success** on the lair search. Honesty is the *better* path by design.
- **Let her believe** → she's a quest-giver with a **false quest** (bring Ricky home; **tell Wade he's searching the wrong place**). Delivering that message **costs Wade a condition band** (Sharp→Worn, Worn→Spent).
- **Never come / never engage** → the **Bystander** branch: Patricia goes into the woods after Wade, hits the spore haze near the lair, collapses; Wade chooses his son, gets him out, comes back for her. → **Ricky 2→1 (Wary)**, or **0** if she's badly hurt. Full detail in [[Patricia Vance]].
