# The Reality Filter

Up: [[Lore & Cosmology]]

**Normies in Mercer Falls perceive the wrongness — they just can't hold onto it or muster the will to follow up. The Filter is not a perceptual veil. It is two things: a deficit of follow-through, and a memory that drains like a dream.**

## The two halves

### 1. The deficit of follow-through

People mostly *see* what happens. The breadcrumb is that **no one ever follows up.** A Fireball in the town square is rationalized as a gas main, nobody calls anyone, nobody investigates. The bigger the event, the weirder the rationalization — accepted instantly, then abandoned.

### 2. The decay clock

> [!danger] Established canon as of [[The Debrief Station]]
> **Memory of the dungeon, and of the fantastical things the dungeon causes in this town, fades the way a dream fades on waking.** Fast, on a clock, and no one notices it happening.
>
> **It is not suppression and it is not a lie.** The person genuinely had it. They reach for it, find a shape where it ought to be, and stop reaching — untroubled, because nothing feels missing. Ask them an hour later and they'll say they had a fever, or slept badly, or drank too much, and **they will mean it.**

| Who | Roughly how long they hold it |
|---|---|
| **Ordinary adult** | **About half an hour.** Mercer's margin, measured on his own men |
| **Children** | **A day or more.** One of Mercer's sweeper boys still had the whole of it a week on |
| **Ember-bearers** | **It does not fade. At all.** Inside or out |

**The countermeasure is a dream journal.** Write it fast enough — your own experience, or dictated from someone who was just down there — and you keep most of it. Wait and it's gone.

> [!danger] Written thoughts do not leave the dungeon
> A magical rule, and it wants no explanation at the table.
>
> **Marks stay** — tallies, route glyphs, chalk, a hammer-hole, *TANK WUZ HEAR*. The dungeon does not care what you scratch on it.
> **Accounts don't** — anything constituting a person's *report* of the place. Paper comes up empty.
>
> So the operation below can keep ledgers, maps and marks that work perfectly **in situ**, and **not one word of it can be exported.** The only channel out is a human head, and heads drain. That is the entire reason [[The Debrief Station]] exists.
>
> Let the boundary be slightly soft and let the party test it. It always fails and they can never quite prove why. See [[The Recall]].

> [!note] Why this half matters for running the game
> The Filter is the hardest thing in the campaign to convey, because **players naturally read it as adults lying or stonewalling.** [[Wendell Pace|Wendell]] grasping and giving up looks evasive. [[Tomek Sołtys|Tomek]]'s *"I probably had a fever"* looks like a cover story. [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s mother looks like denial.
>
> **[[The Debrief Station]]'s log ends that reading permanently** — a hundred years of men *trying* and failing, recorded by the men who watched them fail. It's evidence of effort, not concealment, and it retroactively reframes every adult the party has met.
>
> **The age gradient also explains [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]].** She held Tomek's hollowing after every adult let it go because she was eleven — and she would have lost it too, eventually, if she hadn't started writing it down. Her theory was right and her method was right, for a reason she could never have worked out.

> [!danger] DM-only — what the Filter *is*
> The dungeon's effect on the town, generalized. A century of agency-eating (see [[Hollowing and Agency-Loss]], [[Order and Chaos]]) has left Mercer Falls with a citywide deficit of follow-through and a population that cannot retain the evidence. The stuckness is mechanical — the containment system working as designed.

## Item Rationalization (Visibility Gradient)

People see the item; they just don't act. The bigger and weirder, the more strained the rationalization, but the Filter holds.

| Tier | Item | Rationalization |
|---|---|---|
| Invisible | Pocket knife, walking stick, hunting bow | None — normal |
| Easy | Hunting rifle, machete, hatchet, hockey gear | None, in rural Ohio |
| Moderate | Sword, mace, crossbow, leather armor, biker jacket | "Replica," "costume," "movie prop" |
| Strained | Greataxe, halberd, chain shirt, plate breastplate | "LARPing," "Renaissance Faire" — holds, but witnesses feel off |
| Maximal | Full plate, exotic dungeon weapons, anything visibly inhuman | Filter working hard; vague unease afterward, can't name what they saw |

A tone-and-pacing dial: sword-and-light-armor PCs are nearly invisible in town; a paladin in full plate is making a *choice* every outing. Smart play is the gear stash (see [[Gear and Items]]).

## Who Is / Isn't Subject

| Who | Filter applies? |
|---|---|
| Ordinary adults | Yes — full Filter, ~30-minute retention |
| Young children | Yes, but weakly — **and they hold the memory far longer** |
| Authority figures | Yes — adults under the Filter |
| The PCs' parents | Yes, but more strained — stronger follow-through pressure, weaker results |
| **Other PCs** | **NO** — they see weapons as weapons, and **nothing fades** |
| **[[Wesley Crane]]** | **NO** — ember-equivalent bearer |
| The rival party (post-equinox) | NO — same reason |
| Hollowed adherents | NO — Filter broken in both directions |
| Filter-broken NPCs (mid-game) | NO |

**Parent edge case:** parents are Filtered but have stronger social follow-through with their own kid. Flagrant home gear-flaunting strains it — *"is everything okay at school?"* conversations, Filter-permeable enough to express concern but not to act.

### Filter-porous NPCs — belief is what makes it fail

Some people leak, and the common cause is **having already been told, by someone they believe.**

| Who | Why |
|---|---|
| [[Ren Sołtys]] | Perceives and refuses to let go. **Four years of a notebook** is a deliberate countermeasure to the decay clock, invented at eleven |
| [[Russ]] | Grief. Half-aware his sister's death didn't add up |
| [[Dominique Ferraro]] | **[[Bash Whitlock\|Bash]] told her the whole story, repeatedly, and then showed her.** She sat through an entire hollowing and it didn't slide off |

## Why the PCs Are Immune

[[The Entity]] has touched them — the [[The Embers|embers]] specifically reinforce the bearer's capacity to act, **and stop the memory decay outright, inside the dungeon and out.**

> [!note] The proof the party can run on themselves
> They have been taking notes, drawing maps, and arguing about the reset for weeks, **and none of it has faded.** Nobody has been able to do that since 1889.
>
> **Deliver this as a realization, not a fact.** In [[The Debrief Station]], have somebody check their own pockets.

## Filter-Breaking

Mid-game (Levels 4–10), PCs may learn to **break the Filter for individual NPCs** — a major story-power that recruits allies who can actually act on what's happening.

**Note the two halves come apart.** Breaking the follow-through deficit and stopping the decay are not obviously the same operation, and a partial break — an ally who can *act* but still can't *retain* — is a legitimately horrible outcome worth keeping in reserve.

> [!danger] DM-only — a cosmology breadcrumb
> Filter-breaking is a late-campaign clue about [[The Entity]]'s nature: hollowed mill workers, when finally Filter-broken, may say things that *don't fit* the "benevolent patron" frame. [[Russ]] at [[Panels]] is the strong candidate for the first significant break.

## The 1991 Historical Implication

The [[The 1991 Mill Incident]] was a **mass hollowing, not a mass death**. Workers stumbled into the dungeon, all hollowed, all reconstituted at the entrance and walked back out past coworkers who saw it and don't remember now. The "seven died" are the ones whose hollowed selves walked into machinery or off heights; the rest had safer jobs and are still in town — PCs can find names on the 1991 list by walking up to them at the gas station (see [[Henry Voss]]).
