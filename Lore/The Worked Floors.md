# The Worked Floors

Up: [[Lore & Cosmology]]

**Men from this town clocked in down there. The lower mill isn't alien architecture the dungeon grew — parts of it were worked, on shifts, by [[The Mill|Mercer Steel]] employees, for as long as the mill ran. Lockers, tool cribs, stations, a lunch pail on a hook. Then in 1991 the place shut inside a week and nobody ever went back for any of it.**

## What this resolves

Three facts already in play that had no common explanation:

| Established | Explained by |
|---|---|
| **Everyone who ever documented seeing a nullprint was a mill worker** *(Brother Johan, Session 4)* | They were the only people going down there |
| The 1889 charter uses **stewardship language** and says almost nothing about making steel | The mill was built to work *this*, not iron |
| [[Elias Mercer]] was **obsessive about the exact siting** | He was siting a lid |

It also answers the smaller question it was created for: **why [[Ruth Bielak|a sixty-nine-year-old widow]] was inside the dungeon in 1994.** She wasn't exploring. She was going to her husband's locker.

## What it looks like

Interleaved with the dungeon proper, not separate from it — **which is the unsettling part.** You come out of a room that is plainly not built by people and into a corridor with a light switch and a punch clock.

- **Lockers and hooks.** Coats, boots, a pail, a thermos. Name tags on tape.
- **Tool cribs and stations.** Things a shift would need, laid down mid-task.
- **Signage in English.** Shift rosters, safety notices, a hand-lettered NO SMOKING BEYOND THIS POINT.
- **Wear patterns.** Stair treads worn in the middle. Handrails polished by thirty years of the same grip.
- **Everything is exactly where it was set down in 1991**, because the closure was abrupt and nobody was ever sent to collect it.

> [!note] Not subject to [[The Recall]] or the reset
> None of it was made by the dungeon. **It's people's property, left behind.** It persists like Wesley's chalk and the party's graffiti — permanently, unless somebody carries it out.
>
> Which means the worked floors are a **standing archive of 1991**, and the deeper the party goes, the more of this town's history they are physically walking through.

## Depth

**The deeper you go, the older the work.** Upper worked areas are 1980s: hard hats, plastic signage, a radio. Go further and the fittings get older, the lettering more formal, the equipment heavier and stranger — down toward whatever the 1889 charter was actually describing.

Recommended: **first worked area on Floor 2**, so it arrives shortly after the party opens the Down-Stairs.

## What it's for

1. **Named, ordinary men were down here on shifts.** [[Walt Bielak]] is the first. That reframes the dungeon from *a hole full of monsters* into *a workplace with a hole full of monsters attached*, which is far worse.
2. **Loot with provenance.** Not treasure — somebody's coat, somebody's pail, somebody's photograph taped inside a locker door. Every piece has an owner and most owners are dead or hollowed.
3. **A research channel that doesn't need a book.** Rosters, tags and signage name people the party can go look up at [[The Library]] or ask [[Timothy]] about.
4. **The [[Ruth Bielak|lunch pail]].** First and best example of the whole idea.

## Open dials

- **How far down the work goes**, and whether there's a bottom where it stops being recognisable as work at all.
- Whether any 1991 **shift roster** survives down there. It would be a devastating document — a list of who was on the floor that day, against a town that remembers seven deaths.
- Whether the dogmen or other natives **use** the worked areas, or avoid them.
- Whether [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] has been through them already. *(Almost certainly — and if the party ever finds a locker that has plainly been opened and closed again, that's a fingerprint.)*
- Whether time-clock cards are still in the rack, and what the last punch reads.
