# Certaintism

Up: [[Lore & Cosmology]]

**[[Gern]]'s family's kooky, Scientology-flavored sci-fi religion. It is accidentally *right* that the hollowing is real and observable — and comprehensively *wrong* about what it is: it has the whole [[Order and Chaos]] war backwards, dressed up as a galactic pest-control epic. It is the engine of Gern's arc — a boy raised to (unknowingly) serve Order, powered by Chaos, misreading the entire board — and a gift-wrapped recruiting handle for [[Wesley Crane]].**

> [!danger] DM-only
> The *existence* of Certaintism is player-facing (it's Gern's upbringing). Everything below — that it's Order's gospel with the labels swapped, and what's really happening — is behind the screen. The whole point is that the faith is **true about the phenomena and cartoonishly wrong about the frame.**

## Register — what it sounds like
Scientology by way of a 1970s paperback with a ringed planet on the cover. Past lives, a perfected cosmic authority, a hidden alien infestation, self-improvement-as-cosmic-hygiene, ranks, jargon, maybe a meter, and **doctrine delivered on VHS**. A homeschooled kid quizzed on it at the kitchen table is inherently a little funny and a little sad. **The comedy is the gulf** between the schlocky space-opera frame and the actual cosmic-horror truth underneath; the horror is that the schlock keeps turning out to be *real*.

## Cosmology (in-doctrine)
**All terms below are canon**, including those generated at the table in Session 3.

- **The Concordance** — the perfected intelligence/civilization that eons ago brought the galaxy into **True Alignment**: everything Settled, resolved, in its correct fixed state, at rest. The authority you align *to*. *(Actually: [[Order and Chaos|Order]].)*
- **Lord Veritas the Certain** — the Concordance's personified apex authority, the one you actually **pray to**: the perfected mind at the top of True Alignment, who sees and has always seen. Addressed in worship as **the Certifier**, and habitually stacked with epithets — *the bringer of clarity · the voice of certainty · the perfectly assured · the eternal validator · the one beyond question.* Adherents pile them up devotionally, the way a rosary accumulates.
- **the Snarl** — the enemy. A swarm-like infestation of unaligned particulate that gets into a person and eats their Alignment from the inside, dragging them back down into raw churn. *(Actually: [[Order and Chaos|Chaos]] — i.e. [[The Entity]].)*
- **Dautrons** — the Snarl reckoned as *countable particles*. The doctrinal unit of infestation: they get *into* you, they can be **revealed**, they can be burned out, and a devout adherent talks about his **Dautron levels** the way someone else might talk about cholesterol. This is the everyday, load-bearing word — "the Snarl" is the war, Dautrons are what's in your bloodstream. *(A gift: it makes the metaphysics gossipy and quantifiable, which is exactly how a real cult jargon behaves.)*
- **the stack** — you are not a soul; you're a *stack* of past-life imprints, most of them snarled.
- **the Kernel** — a core of pure Alignment the Concordance issued you, buried down in the stack. The practice is getting it **locked**.
- **auditing / calibration / running the Ladder** — the practice: burn the Dautrons out of your stack, lock your Kernel, read as Aligned so the Snarl can't get a grip. (Optional prop: an **Alignment meter**, the E-meter analog, that "reads" how snarled you are.)
- **Locked / Settled / Aligned** = the goal-state: safe, holy, finished. **loose / snarled / unmade** = the failure-state: erasure.

### The Tome of Conviction
**The family scripture** — the physical book in Gern's house, which his mother drilled him out of. Contains the illustrated hazard plates (including **Figure 9**, the nullprint), the **dampening passage** (below), and enough doctrine that Gern can look things up mid-adventure and sometimes find an answer. **Gern brought it to the party in Session 3** and it has now been corroborated in front of everyone.

### Grand Director Douglas Sturtis
The faith's authority-figure lecturer — doctrine delivered on **videotape**, watched at home. The Hubbard analog: a man in a modest suit in front of a starfield, explaining the infestation with total assurance and a pointer. Gern cites him the way other kids cite a coach. *(Cheap, funny, and a perfect vector for feeding the table doctrine you want them to have: "there's a tape about this.")*

> [!note] One naming call, made by judgment — overrule freely
> Gern's player groped for a name for the *discipline* in Session 3 — "certontology," "certainology," "circuitology." Resolved as: **the faith is Certaintism**, full stop, and **Gern fumbling the technical vocabulary is in character** — a homeschooled kid who has memorized more jargon than he has understanding of it, tripping over the polysyllables he's proudest of knowing. It's funnier and sadder than a clean coinage, and it costs nothing. If you'd rather lock a formal discipline name, this is the place.

## What it gets RIGHT (the real phenomena)
- **The hollowing is real.** People really are emptied and reduced. Doctrine calls it being **Snarled** — an **Unmaking**, a **nullprint event.**
- **The residue is real.** A hollowed person leaves a seared, human-shaped outline burned into the ground — arms slightly open, a person gone to a stain. Doctrine calls it a **nullprint**, and it is literally illustrated in the [[Certaintism|Tome of Conviction]] (**"Figure 9"**), captioned as a hazard placard: *this is what the Snarl leaves.* (In-world this rhymes with the ash-stains ringing the [[The Buzzsaw Room|Buzzsaw]] platform and the mark [[Wendell Pace]] leaves in [[The Pillared Reach]].)
- **The walk-home is real.** A hollowed body that still gets up and moves (the [[Hollowing and Agency-Loss|autopilot walk home]]) has a doctrinal slot: *an emptied stack running on residual imprint.*
- **Resistance is real.** Some people are Unmade permanently and some come back. Doctrine has a passage for this too — see *The dampening doctrine*.

## What it gets WRONG (the inversion — the actual truth)
Two inversions, stacked — which is why nobody sees through it:
1. **Valence backwards.** Certaintism frames being Snarled as *the enemy winning* — erasure, to be feared and warded off. In truth the hollowing **is** agency-theft — but committed by **Order's containment machine** ([[The Dungeon]]), not by chaos. The thing to fear is real; the culprit is the opposite of the one named.
2. **Sides backwards (the big one).** The **Concordance is Order** — the force that actually wants to end agency (fixity, Alignment, everything Settled and unable to change). **the Snarl is Chaos** — change, questioning, the churn of a living will. Certaintism **worships the agency-killer and demonizes the agency-giver.** Gern's parents are, without any idea, preaching for the enemy.

So Certaintism is **Order's gospel with the labels swapped**, wearing a rubber alien mask so the real metaphysics is invisible. Structurally it preaches the [[The Cast and the Permanent Cast|Permanent Cast]] as salvation: *get Locked, get Settled, never have to carry yourself again* — the cast that never comes off, sold as heaven.

## The Affirmation (canon liturgy)
Gern's player recited this on the mill grounds in Session 3, unprompted. **Preserved verbatim as the faith's core devotional formula** — the thing an adherent says when the world confirms the doctrine:

> *I surrender uncertainty. I surrender questions and hesitation.*
> *May every Dautron be revealed! May every hidden thought be known!*
> *May my certainty not belong to me but to the Certifier who sees all.*
> *I am certain. I have always been certain. I will always be certain.*

> [!danger] DM-only — read what that prayer actually asks for
> Line by line it is a **renunciation of agency, out loud, as worship.** It surrenders questioning; it asks for total transparency to authority (*may every hidden thought be known*); it explicitly **transfers ownership of one's own conviction to someone else** (*may my certainty not belong to me but to the Certifier*); and it closes by abolishing the past and future tense into a single fixed state. That is [[Order and Chaos|Order]]'s entire program, said by a twelve-year-old as a comfort. **Have him say it at good moments** — victories, confirmations, relief — so the table hears it as devotion and only much later hears it as a confession. It is also the single best artifact to hand [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]: everything he wants from Gern, Gern is already praying for.

## The low-residue misread (the instrument that points exactly wrong)
- **The fact:** ember-bearers — the **PCs** — leave **almost no nullprint** when they hollow (Chaos is *shielding* them from full erasure). **NPCs leave a full one.**
- **The doctrinal read:** a clean, complete nullprint = a citizen so Aligned the Snarl had to consume them whole. Someone who barely leaves a smudge must be a **partial** — one of *the compromised* — so riddled with Dautrons there's almost nothing Aligned left to burn a clean cast.
- **The result:** Gern, reading strictly by the book, looks at his own party and sees **late-stage Snarl carriers.** The diagnostic is real and points *precisely* backwards.

## The dampening doctrine (the conviction passage)
**Per the Tome of Conviction: susceptibility to nullprinting is not uniform. An Unmaking devastates ordinary people, but *an aspect of one's conviction can dampen the effect* — a person sufficiently steeled can be Snarled and not lost.** Surfaced in play by [[Gern]] (Session 3, religion check) in front of the whole party.

**What it's really explaining.** The observable is [[The Embers|the embers]]: bearers hollow and **recover after the lost day**, while non-bearers like [[Wendell Pace]] stay hollowed for good ([[Death, TPK, and the Lost Day]], [[Hollowing and Agency-Loss]]). Certaintism has no concept of an ember, so the passage exists as **an after-the-fact rationalization written to explain a witnessed anomaly** — somebody, once, saw a person go into the dark, come out emptied, and then *come back*, and the faith needed a reason. Naming that reason "conviction" was the only move a conviction-based religion could make. *(This selects option (b) in the open dials below, at least locally: the passage requires a real prior bearer.)*

**The observable is about to land in front of them.** Next trip to [[Marco's Diner]]: **[[Wendell Pace|Wendell]] is still glassy and zombie-like, while [[Tank]] and [[Old Man]] are back to themselves.** The players have met all three, so the comparison is theirs to make unprompted — and Gern is holding the scripture that appears to explain it. Play the passage *before* the evidence, so the evidence reads as confirmation.

**How it sits against the low-residue misread — not a contradiction, a fork.** Two diagnostics, different observables, opposite verdicts on the same four kids:

| Observable | Doctrinal diagnostic | Verdict on the PCs |
|---|---|---|
| Faint nullprint vs. a full one | *the partials* — barely any Alignment left to burn | **Damning.** Late-stage carriers. |
| Recovered vs. stayed Unmade | *the dampening* — conviction held the Snarl off | **Flattering.** Exceptionally Aligned. |

Both readings are available from the same book about the same kids, and **both are wrong in the same direction** — every one of these effects is Chaos protecting them, credited to or blamed on Order's ledger either way. This is the machinery behind Gern's two false self-readings, and it means he can't sit on the fence forever: eventually he has to decide which passage his friends fall under.

**The ugly edge worth having ready.** Read through the dampening, [[Wendell Pace|Wendell]] stayed hollowed because he *lacked conviction* — he wasn't steeled enough, so he earned it. That gives Gern a doctrinally airtight reason to hold a hollowed janitor in contempt, to feel superior for surviving, and to write off the [[Hollowed Townspeople|hollowed of the Flats]] as the insufficiently Aligned. A faith that explains suffering by the sufferer's weakness is a real thing real people believe, and it is the single ugliest turn available to him. Don't force it; leave it lying there.

## Gern's arc (the trap)
- **The seed — the Wendell moment (Ep1):** in [[The Pillared Reach]] Gern watches [[Wendell Pace]] hollow and leave a nullprint — **Figure 9, made real.** First read: *my parents were right — and it's worse than they said.* The faith lands as **true and frightening**, not aspirational.
- **The corroboration (Ep2, Session 3):** he brings the Tome to the party and the illustration **matches exactly**, confirmed in front of everyone. [[Tank]] refuses to even look and dismisses him outright (*"I'm certain that's in a book too. Doesn't make it real"*); [[Old Man]] engages only to argue Hume and the black swan; only [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] takes him seriously. **He has been proven right and believed by one person out of three** — precisely the fuel for the zeal reading, and a live [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] opening.
- **Two false self-readings** (both wrong, both rich, play them against each other):
  1. *"The Snarl's in me — I'm one of the compromised, I'm turning."* → dread and shame. **Warranted by the low-residue misread.**
  2. *"I've been issued a field-Kernel to hunt the infestation from inside the compromised."* → a commissioned exterminator; **zeal.** The more dangerous read — and **now explicitly warranted by the dampening doctrine:** *I was Unmade and I came back, therefore my conviction is real, therefore I am one of the steeled.* The table handed Gern the scripture that arms his worst reading of himself.
- **His fire betrays the frame.** The embers are warmth, reaching, *change* — pure Snarl signature by the doctrine. A faith-literate, honest kid eventually notices *his own gift smells like the enemy.*
- **The truth he can't see:** his power comes from his gospel's *devil* (Chaos), which is the side actually **protecting** agency; the Concordance he was raised to serve is the agency-killer. His third-position arc: neither the faith nor the fire is telling him the whole story.
- **The Wesley handle:** [[Order and Chaos|Order]] would *love* a Chaos-bearer who believes his Chaos-fire is a holy weapon *for* the Settled order. [[Wesley Crane]] can affirm the "exterminator" reading and recruit Gern to fight for the very thing that made him. See [[The Cast and the Permanent Cast]] and [[The Negotiation System]].

## Ep1 seeds
1. **The Wendell nullprint** ([[The Pillared Reach]]) — the Figure-9 recognition. **Fired as designed.**
2. *(Optional second touch)* **[[The Cataloguing Office]]** — the century-old operation *filing* everything it finds can read to a Certaintist kid as the Concordance's **auditing** at work: reality keeping its books. A quiet second rhyme if you want it.

## Open dials
- **Certaintism's actual connection to Order** — (a) *pure kook coincidence*; (b) a *garbled downstream echo* — someone once witnessed the real phenomenon and built a faith on the wreckage; (c) *genuinely Order-touched* — seeded to cultivate humans who *want* to be Settled. **⚠ The dampening doctrine now requires (b) at least locally.** **Open: who was the witness?** A [[The 1991 Mill Incident|1991 survivor]] is the highest-value answer (it wires Certaintism into 1991 and gives Gern a personal reason to care); [[Elias Mercer]] is the deepest; an anonymous long-ago bearer is the cheapest and keeps the origin fuzzy.
- **Whether the witness is findable.** If the passage traces to a real person, Gern can go looking — a strong Town-Time thread and a route to a *non-Wesley* mentor.
- **Whether Sturtis is alive, and where.** A living Grand Director is a potential mid-campaign encounter and a very funny one; a dead one keeps the faith safely inert.
- **Jargon depth** — currently a moderate apparatus. Dial toward full ranks-and-meter, or down.
- **Who else is Certaintist** — Gern's family at minimum; open whether it has any other footprint in Mercer Falls.

## Running the recognition beat
Hand it to Gern's player privately (note card, whisper, DM channel):
> *You've seen this exact shape before. It's Figure 9. Your mother had you copy it out of the Tome until you could draw it from memory — a person, arms a little open, gone to a stain on the floor. The caption underneath was always the same: "A nullprint. This is what the Snarl leaves."*

Then let him react in front of the others and **explain nothing.** Don't confirm or deny. The horror is the ambiguity — *were they right?* — so leave it hanging.
