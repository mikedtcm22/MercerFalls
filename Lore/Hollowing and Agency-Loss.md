# Hollowing and Agency-Loss

Up: [[Lore & Cosmology]]

**Death in [[The Dungeon]] is not death — it's agency-theft. The dungeon doesn't want your life; it wants the part of you that decides. Time-loss is the symptom; agency-loss is the mechanism. Recovery is real, it compounds, and it leaves a mark that never closes.**

## What Happens at "Death"

When a person is "killed" in the dungeon, they don't actually die there. At the moment of "death" the **deciding part is taken**, the body dissolves into motes that drift up and vanish, and the body **reconstitutes at the dungeon's entrance** some indeterminate time later. (D&D shorthand calls this "death" because it lines up with hit points hitting zero — but the underlying event is hollowing. See [[The Embers]], [[Death, TPK, and the Lost Day]].)

## Natives Don't Hollow (monster death)

Hollowing is what the dungeon does to **visitors** — the people it wants agency from. Its own **native monsters are not hollowed when killed.** They simply die: the corpse **stays where it fell** — it does not dissolve into motes or reconstitute at the entrance — and can be **searched and looted** mid-delve, with anything carried out persisting ([[Gear and Items]]).

**What happens to the corpse afterwards is [[The Recall]]** — inside, it goes back with the floor reset; outside, it's gone by the next sunrise, and nobody ever sees it happen. Full rules there.

Two consequences worth flagging:
- **The dissolve is a diagnostic.** A body that dissolves into upward motes was a *person*; a body that stays and can be looted was a *native*. A party that clocks the difference has a real forensic tell.
- **A monster corpse you didn't make is a read.** A fresh native corpse means someone else was here recently — in Session 1, [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s pre-clear leaves exactly these.

## The Dissolve and the Walk Home

For an ember-bearer, the ember intervenes visibly — narrate in fire vocabulary (see [[The Embers]]):

> The warmth in their chest *catches* — flares — the light pours out of them, upward, and disperses. The body becomes translucent, dissolves into motes drifting toward the ceiling, and a faint shimmer remains on the floor, fading over minutes.

The light is [[The Entity]]'s substance being spent; the motes are the bearer's **agency** being routed to the dungeon; the shimmer is **residue**. **Don't pause the encounter** — the others keep fighting.

**Reconstitution.** The body re-coalesces at the entrance — same clothes, same gear, glassy-eyed, silent, unresponsive to its own name, the chest-warmth now *cold ash, banked too long* — **as if almost no time has passed in the world.** It then **walks home on autopilot** — route-correct, climbing fences, ignoring traffic, weather, and people — lets itself in, and goes to bed.

**The walk is witnessable** (the "dungeon walk of shame") — by townsfolk (whom [[The Reality Filter]] handles), or by PCs and other ember-bearers (who are *not* Filtered and will remember). A major forensic channel needing no dungeon entry. See [[Forensic Delving]].

## The Lost Day

The hollowed person wakes in their own bed, body intact, with no memory of the walk home or of the dungeon, and goes on auto-pilot — *present without will* — showing up to scheduled things, walking past everything else, giving the noncommittal answer where intervention was needed. Anything time-locked advances without them. The Filter covers the rest.

**At the table the lost day is narrated, not skipped**, with the dead player present as pure witness — one or two stinging beats, not a play-by-play. Full procedure in [[Death, TPK, and the Lost Day]].

## Duration, recovery, and the curve

> [!danger] Recovery is real — and it compounds
> **Not every hollowing is terminal.** Most people come back. But **each hollowing takes a bite, each recovery is longer than the last, and eventually one doesn't end.** It's a curve, not a cliff.

| Who | Duration |
|---|---|
| **Ember-bearing PC** | **One day. Every time.** They lose control of the rest of that day and all of the next, then come back — keeping every memory up to the moment they dropped, with only the walk home and the lost day itself blank |
| Ordinary, first event | Days to weeks. [[Tomek Sołtys\|Tomek Sołtys]]: **two weeks**, at seventeen, and then completely fine for four years |
| Ordinary, later events | **Longer each time.** Five days → three weeks. Nine days → seven weeks |
| Terminal | It doesn't end. [[Henry Voss]]'s *first logged* event ran two months; his second has no end date, and his history predates anybody's records |

> [!note] The PCs are not on the curve
> Tank and Old Man have now been hollowed twice and it cost them **the same one day both times.** This is the reveal at the centre of [[Episode 3 — Second Shift]], and the players should derive it by holding their own experience against [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s notebook — where *every* second entry is longer than the first.
>
> Escalation for the party stays available for much later in the campaign, where it will land as *"the thing that protects us is wearing out."* **Nowhere near Act 1.**

## The notch — the permanent part

**The print heals. The shape doesn't.** Once the machine has taken a bite of someone, there is a place in them shaped like its teeth. They regrow the agency; **the notch stays, forever.**

It is the only permanent consequence of a single hollowing, and it is what makes a recovered person **audible** to the dungeon and to things bound to it — the mechanism behind Episode 3. Full susceptibility table in [[Nullprints]].

**The cruel shape: the people most at risk are the ones who look completely fine.**

## Time-Loss Is the Symptom

To the outside world this reads as **time-loss**: a person who can't decide where to go or who to call for nine days reads, to everyone, as a person who *lost* nine days. Townspeople, survivors, and official records all describe it in **time-language** because that's what's perceptible from outside. The truth — *agency-loss* — is invisible to them. This is the dungeon doing what [[Order and Chaos|Order]] built it to do.

## Death Comes Later, Aboveground

For most who are hollowed, **death comes later, aboveground.** A hollowed person has no intact survival instinct — the part that flinches from danger is eaten. Whether and how soon they die depends entirely on daily life:

- A hollowed gas-station clerk with a low-risk routine survives for **years** ([[Henry Voss]]).
- A hollowed mill worker handling molten metal lasts **hours**.
- A hollowed person whose friend suggests a fishing trip dies on the trip — the cause of death is genuinely the accident; the foul play happened upstream. See [[Mike and Doug Tinsmith]].

Many of Mercer Falls' missing-persons cases are agency-loss casualties; some aren't even truly missing — some are **walking around right now**. The [[The 1991 Mill Incident]]'s official "seven died" is the **aboveground death toll** of a mass hollowing, not a dungeon casualty count. See [[Hollowed Townspeople]].

## Cost Summary

See **[[Death, TPK, and the Lost Day]]**: a free-willed delve bills a flat **3-Time-Point dwell**, **hollowing** costs 0 TP but forfeits control of the rest of today and all of tomorrow, and **death outside the dungeon is permanent** (the ember goes inert).
