# Nullprints

Up: [[Lore & Cosmology]]

**The seared, human-shaped mark left where a person was hollowed. Not a stain — a *file.* The dungeon's ledger entry recording the transaction, which the dungeon then slowly eats.**

> [!danger] DM-only
> The *existence* of nullprints is player-facing (the party has seen Wendell's; [[Gern]] can name it from the [[Certaintism|Tome of Conviction]] as **Figure 9**). Everything below — that it's a live file, that it can be operated on, and what the two operations do — is behind the screen and is the spine of [[Episode 3 — The Crossing]].

## What it is

When a person is hollowed ([[Hollowing and Agency-Loss]]), the deciding part is routed into the machine and a **nullprint** is burned into the stone at the exact spot they fell: a human outline, arms slightly open, a whole person reduced to a shape. Doctrine calls it Figure 9 and treats it as a hazard placard. It is actually **bookkeeping** — the machine writing down what it took, and where.

- **Ember-bearers leave almost nothing** — a faint smudge. Non-bearers leave a full, deep print. (The Certaintist misread of this asymmetry is load-bearing for Gern — see [[Certaintism]].)
- **One print per hollowing event**, at the spot of that event. A repeat victim leaves a **trail**, not a single mark.

## Persistence and the fade — the dungeon is eating them

**Nullprints are not cleared by the daily [[Floor Reset and the Intelligence Gradient|floor reset].** They persist for months or years. They fade because **the dungeon consumes them over time** — the harvest continues after the victim walks out. The mark thinning on the stone is the meal in progress.

> This is the correct read on "residue fades." Residue (the semi-physical agency-substance that hangs in a chamber for hours-to-days) is the **smoke**. The nullprint underneath it is the **filing**, and it lasts far longer because it's still being digested.

**Consumption stops when the person dies.** The dungeon only eats the agency of the living; a dead man's account is closed and the remaining mark freezes permanently.

### The colour tell

| Print | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Black** — dark, sunken, visibly thinning over months | The person is **alive** and still being consumed |
| **White** — pale ash, frozen, no further change | The person is **dead**. There is a [[Ghosts\|ghost]]. |

A party that learns this can walk a chamber and read who is still out there and who isn't. It is one of the highest-value [[Forensic Delving|forensic]] reads in the campaign.

## Open vs. closed prints

**A print you were restored from is closed** — spent, inert, and consumed faster than a live one. The only **live** print a person has is the one they were *never* restored from.

So a serial victim like [[Lowell Brandt]] leaves a descending trail of mostly-eaten closed scars and **exactly one live print** — the last. That single print is the anchor for everything below.

## The two operations

The same object supports two rituals, and confusing them is lethal.

### 1. Restoration (subject alive)

Bring a hollowed person to their **live black print** and a comparatively simple rite returns their agency **instantly and fully**, on the spot. [[Wesley Crane]] knows it. Source of his knowledge: presumably the Mercer papers — **open**.

Three things keep this from deflating the horror of hollowing:

- **You have to get them there.** A will-less adult cannot be led anywhere they don't already have a routine for; and once restored, they still have to **survive the walk out.** The dungeon exists to make that nearly impossible for an ordinary human. The practical result of restoration-without-extraction is that a person **ceases to exist as themselves anywhere outside the dungeon** — the [[Lowell Brandt|Severance trap]].
- **[[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s exit glyph is an equivalent-exchange door** — every body that walks out spawns another tough monster on his next delve. Extracting a restored person costs him directly, every time. (Same logic that governed his Buzzsaw Room decision — see [[Session 1 — The Dare]].)
- **The skim.** Agency renews in a person, but *the capacity that renews it* degrades slightly with each hollowing. Restoration returns function, never quite all of it. Enough cycles and there is nothing left to renew — see [[Henry Voss]], whose capacity has simply atrophied after five years.

### 2. Release (subject dead)

Burning the **white print** of a dead person closes the file and lets the [[Ghosts|ghost]] move on. It is a mercy and a laying-to-rest, not a kill.

**It requires a counter-claim.** Ember-fire alone does nothing; the ledger's assertion is *this person had no will*, and a keepsake is weak rebuttal. What works is an object proving that **a choice of theirs outlived them** — ideally a completed errand. Place it on the print and burn it there.

> [!danger] Never perform release on a live black print.
> Doing so to a living hollowed person does not free them — it releases them the way it releases the dead. It would almost certainly **kill them.** This is a loaded gun sitting in the party's hands from Episode 3 onward, and a future antagonist attempting it on someone the PCs love is a strong episode premise (a race to *prevent* it).

## Consequences already in play

- **[[Wendell Pace]]'s print is in [[The Pillared Reach]]**, black, and being eaten right now. The party stood on it in Episode 1. There is no safe operation available to them: restoration is out of reach, and release would kill him.
- **The [[The 1991 Mill Incident|1991]] dead left prints too.** Seven men hollowed and dead within days — seven white prints, and seven active ghosts, which have been in and around [[The Mill]] ever since. Nobody has ever separated one out because everything about the mill is wrong. See [[Ghosts]].
- **A hollowed PC who dies aboveground during the lost day** ([[Death, TPK, and the Lost Day]] — death outside the dungeon is permanent) leaves a *faint* white print and becomes a ghost. Do not raise this at the table; let them find it.

## The de-hollowing horizon (Act 2/3)

The release rite is the **precursor to a genuine de-hollowing method** — and, eventually, to the campaign's answer for deliberately breaking someone out of [[The Reality Filter]]. Locked direction: **it is possible, and it is out of reach at Act-1 levels.** The catch, when it arrives, is that the procedure requires the subject be **hollowed first** — so giving someone their sight means taking their agency, permanently skimming them, and betting you can give it back.

That is [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s exact move, performed by the heroes, for good reasons. Two guardrails when it comes online:
- Keep it clearly distinct from [[Russ]] and [[Wade Vance]], who are Filter-resistant **organically**, through grief and love. Surgical vs. earned; the contrast should sting.
- Decide before the PCs have it whether an **unwilling** target is mechanically possible, or whether consent is required. They will try to weaponize it.

## Open dials

- **Does burning destroy the print or transform it?** Proposed: the white ash **lifts off the stone** and the mark closes, leaving clean rock. Gives the table visible confirmation, and lets them see that Wendell's is still there, still black.
- **Where the restoration rite is written down.** Mercer papers is the obvious answer and wires straight into [[The Mercer Mansion]] arc.
- **Whether the skim has a floor** — is [[Henry Voss]] empty, or merely very slow? Determines whether "give the town back its people" is a real Act-2 objective or a heartbreak.
