# The Gremlins

Up: [[Bestiary]]

**The tenants of [[Floor 2 — The Works]]: goblins and hobgoblins reskinned as the gremlins of the 1984 film and its 1990 sequel. Small, many, malicious, and — the thing that defines the floor — they *build*. Every one of them carries a contraption, and every contraption has a break point.**

> [!danger] DM-only
> Floor-2 lair theme. Per [[The Nightmare Engine]]: D&D mechanics, Mercer Falls flavor. Chosen over a Tommyknocker/mine-folklore skin because the film is far more accessible to the table — the players already know the rules, which makes the research scene fast and the subversions land.

## Source and research

**Primary source:** *Gremlins* (1984) and *Gremlins 2: The New Batch* (1990). Squarely inside the cultural catchment for Mercer Falls in 1996 — video store, late-night TV, and every kid in town has seen at least the first one.

**Research routes** (per [[The Nightmare Engine]]):
- **Recognition** — near-automatic. Most PCs should just *know* on sight; be generous, no check.
- **[[Russ]] at [[Panels]]** — has both on tape, can recite the rules, and will be delighted. One scene, one paragraph, done.
- **Folklore route** — the mill men blamed sixty years of tool loss, line failures, and unexplained breakage on "gremlins." Diner old-timers and the [[Mercer Falls Historical Society]] carry that version. Useful color; adds nothing mechanical.

**Secondary tell:** knocking, scrabbling, and running *inside the walls* — constant on Floor 2 whether or not anything is coming.

## The three rules

**1. Sunlight kills them. True, canonical, and useless down there.**

Keep it, and let it be useless **on purpose**. The party spends a Stretch and a research scene to learn a real, correct, canonical vulnerability that does nothing for them in a hole in the ground. That is the Nightmare Engine's actual lesson — intel is real, *applying* it is the problem. Flashlights and torches do not bother them; there is **no light mechanic on this floor.**

**Banked for later.** These are not a one-floor creature. Once a PC picks up an ability that conjures literal sunlight (Daylight does *not* qualify — it is not sunlight; a Radiant Sun Bolt monk, a Dawn-caster, or an ember-channeled equivalent does), the rule cashes in hard, and it should be a total reversal of a fight the party remembers losing ground in. Also live topside in [[Episode — The Mirror|Ep5]] if anything comes through into a comic shop with a front window.

**2. Water multiplies them. Live, and it reframes the floor.**

Douse a gremlin and it **buds**: one **Wet Spawn** arrives at the start of the next round. **Cap: one bud per gremlin per fight.** Wet Spawns are CR 1/8 — 10 HP, AC 13, no rig, no Nimble Escape, unequipped (contraptions do not copy).

Consequences worth running:
- **[[The Wash]] (room 2) stops being a crossing and becomes a clock.** Any fight there escalates every round the party keeps splashing — which is exactly the room where [[Episode 4 — The Crossing|Ep4]] demands a one-minute stationary hold in standing water while the crew works the sluice. The set piece builds itself.
- It explains why the floor is *full*, and why the reset refills it.
- It shuts off the obvious cheese before a player tries it.
- Casters with water effects have to actually think.
- **It cuts both ways.** A competent Foreman pulls his people *out* of water; Wet Spawns are the crew's problem too. Let the gremlins play it correctly.

**3. Feeding after midnight. Optional, cosmological.**

The dungeon has no midnight. Rule that **the reset is the transformation** — whatever the floor does at reset, it does to them. A quiet [[Cosmology Seeds|cosmology seed]] about what the reset actually *is*. Drop it if it is clutter.

## Roster

All stat blocks run on the standard **goblin chassis** (AC 15, 7 HP, Nimble Escape) unless noted. **The rig is the identity — and the rig is the weakness.** A party that reads a gremlin's contraption can turn it off; that is the competency this floor trains, at combat speed, to prepare them for [[The Shift Floor]].

### Leaders

- **Foreman** — Goblin Boss chassis, **CR 1 (200 XP)**. Redirect Attack. Carries a whistle.
- **The Winder** — **CR 2 (450 XP)**. Spring-harness leaper, 45 HP, 40-ft pounce. Break the harness and it is a Foreman.
- **Batwing** — **CR 2 (450 XP)**. The *Gremlins 2* mutation. Flying. Hold for [[The Spans]].

### The Shift Boss — [[Floor 2 — The Works|Floor 2]] guardian

The **Brain Gremlin** from *Gremlins 2*, wearing a dead foreman's clothes and running the crew on mill discipline. **Hobgoblin Captain chassis**, tuned up to the level the party arrives at. He is the reason this floor builds machines instead of just biting people. He **talks** during the fight, and he can be negotiated with — badly. Calls reinforcements off the wandering table with the steam whistle; the pool is accumulated per the reinforcement ledger in [[Floor 2 — The Works]]. Full encounter design at [[The Shift Floor]].

**In his cage:** the **Spider Gremlin** (*Gremlins 2*), released if the party fails to interrupt the machine.

## The caged mogwai

> [!danger] The reveal in [[The Warren Wall]]
> Past the screw-jack press, in the deepest cell of the warren: a birdcage wired shut with coat hanger. Inside, something small and furry with enormous eyes, **soaked through and shivering**, surrounded by a rigged drain that keeps him that way. **They wet him when they want more of themselves.** He is a factory, and he has been one for a long time.
>
> **Every gremlin on this floor came out of him.** That recontextualizes the entire floor in one beat, and it is what makes the crank's price worth paying.

**What he gives:** fits the tunnels (retrieves things, opens latches from the far side, scouts a room) · knows the floor cold (point a direction and he reacts — a crude but honest information channel) · **will not go near [[The Wash]]**, and will not explain why until they work it out · is the only thing on this floor that is not hostile, which matters after two floors of nothing but teeth.

**What he costs:** a walking hazard. Rain, a canteen, a bad fall into water. And **the floor resets** — rescuing him and leaving him there means he is back in the cage tomorrow, drained and wet, with no memory of them. The only permanent rescue is taking him out of the dungeon, and Mercer Falls has a river, a falls, a lake, and it is summer in Ohio. **Leave that unsolved.** Let the party carry it for weeks.

**Do not stat him for combat.** 6–7 HP, hides, useless in a fight. Something they protect, not something they deploy. He wears a **brass mill tag on a string** with a number stamped on it, matching the hatch keys. Let them name him.

## Wandering table — Floor 2

**d12. Thresholds for 4 PCs at level 3: Medium 600 · Hard 900 · Deadly 1600.**

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 Chewers | 300 | *Easy — a gear raid, not a fight* |
| 2 | 6 Scrappers | 600 | Medium |
| 3 | 2 Greasers + 4 Scrappers | 600 | Medium |
| 4 | 5 Duct-rats + 1 Whistler | 700 | Medium |
| 5 | 2 Sparkjacks + 4 Scrappers | 800 | Medium |
| 6 | 1 Huffer + 1 Boilerplate + 4 Scrappers | 800 | Medium |
| 7 | 3 Stiltwalkers + 3 Scrappers | 900 | Hard |
| 8 | 2 Bottle Rockets + 1 Foreman + 3 Scrappers | 1100 | Hard |
| 9 | 1 Fanboy + 2 Stiltwalkers + 4 Scrappers | 1250 | Hard |
| 10 | 1 Foreman + 6 Scrappers | 1250 | Hard |
| 11 | 1 Winder + 4 Scrappers | 1300 | Hard |
| 12 | 2 Batwings, **or** a stray from below | 1350 | Hard, and it should feel wrong |

Roughly half medium, half hard, with **entry 1 deliberately not a combat** — a table where every roll is a brawl teaches players to dread the die instead of reading the floor.

**Emergence is the default presentation.** Per the ductwork rule in [[Floor 2 — The Works]], a wandering hit means they come *out of the woodwork* mid-room, inside the party's formation — not in through a door. A party that has mapped a room's grates converts the ambush into a normal fight.

**Room-keyed swaps** (the table is a default, not a straitjacket): [[The Wash]] → bias Sparkjacks. [[The Spans]] → Batwings and Bottle Rockets. [[The Bucket Line]] → Fanboys. [[The Door Machine]] → Chewers, because a Chewer eating a component mid-assembly is the best bad thing that can happen to them all night.

## Escalation and drift

**The whistle pool.** Every Whistler that survives a fight and every Foreman given a free round adds a d12 roll to the Shift Boss's reinforcement pool. The party builds it all floor without knowing. Full ledger in [[Floor 2 — The Works]].

**At levels 4 and 5.** Thresholds go to Medium 1000 / Hard 1500 at level 4, and Medium 2000 / Hard 3000 at level 5, so this table drifts from half-hard to mostly-medium to clearing the floor **on its own, without retuning anything** — the intended arc.

Two adjustments when it does:
- **The [[Dungeon Mechanics|level-disparity flee rule]] bites at level 5.** Gremlins are pack-driven and therefore already exempt under that rule's own exception list. Ruling: **they do not flee, they retreat to a rig** — pulling back to a fan, a grease cache, a firework rack, somewhere the environment does the work they no longer can. Same monsters, new tactics. Reads as the floor getting smarter rather than the party outgrowing it.
- **Roll twice and combine on entries 7–12** at level 5 rather than inventing new stat blocks.
