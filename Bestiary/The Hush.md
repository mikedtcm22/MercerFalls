# The Hush

Up: [[Bestiary]]

**An eyeless, sound-hunting predator pulled from a B-movie creature feature — the dare-night chaser and the monster the PCs research and kill. Built like an ear, not an eye.** The dare-night antagonist: met as a **juvenile** in the dungeon ([[Episode 1 — The Dare|Episode 1]]) and as an **adult** topside in [[Episode 2 — The Hush]].

*(Canon name as of the v0.2 redesign; previously filed as "The Watcher," kept as an alias. The in-world film is **The Quiet Dark** (1982), whose teens name the creature "the hush" for the dead quiet before it strikes — that's where the party's name for it comes from.)*

## Concept and source
A [[The Nightmare Engine|Nightmare Engine]] creature: the dungeon curated it from a B-movie creature feature with cultural reach in [[Mercer Falls, Ohio]] — ***The Quiet Dark* (1982)**, an obscure regional creature-feature. Russ at [[Panels]] is a fan and IDs it on sight from a description; the film's climax teaches the **blind-it tactic** (see *How it dies*), not a silence "kill." Native to [[The Dungeon|the dungeon]]; an adult got out years ago and has lived in the woods around [[The Mill]], taking animals and the occasional un-missed drifter. The tie-in comic ***The Quiet Dark: Below*** (see [[The Hush-Hunting Graphic Novel]]) carries the den-tracking lore Russ can't.

## What it looks like (v0.2 — locked)
Not a humanoid. A **wolf-shaped front** — a head carried forward — but with **no eyes and a blank where the face should be**; great **translucent listening-fans** sweep back like ears. Its **hide eats light** (matte, swallowing — the reason moonlight "won't land right" on it). The body is **spider-economical and multi-limbed**, not radially symmetric yet able to move any direction. It tracks sound in every direction and its limbs drive any direction, so **getting behind it gains nothing — it is never flanked**, even though it visibly *has* a behind. Faster in a straight line than the kids, and tireless.

## The hunt grammar (three states)
- **Amble (calm):** front-first, mundane; a glimpse reads as a stray dog. Deniable.
- **Spin-and-click (searching):** spins in place, legs steering independently, head swinging late, fanning slow echolocation clicks — no fix yet. *The window* to freeze or misdirect.
- **Snap-and-charge (found you):** snaps front-to-bearing and runs, clicks speeding out of the sweep. Run on the snap.
When hunting it **decouples facing from travel** — pours sideways or backward without turning.

## Voice-mimicry (the introduction hook)
It plays back sound it just heard — a friend's voice, or a PC's own name — from the wrong place in the dark; timbre right, affect wrong. Nobody should walk toward the fake voice; it sends a powerless party running on their own. Lead with the voice, then the shape.

## The spores — dosing, marking, and the hunting ritual
**This is the species' central mechanism, and the engine of [[Episode 2 — The Hush]]'s clock.**

- **A dose is a homing beacon, installed by breath.** The Hush releases a spore burst — **roughly once a day** is all it can manage — that the victim inhales. Each dose does two things: **poisons them a little weaker,** and **brightens them on the Hush's spore-sense.** One dose gives only a rough bearing (which is why the adult prowls a marked kid's *neighborhood* without pinpointing the window); **repeated doses become an address.** At **three or more,** it knows exactly where they are.
- **The hunting ritual (why it takes people rather than killing them).** The young must learn to *find* prey on their own, so the adult captures a victim, stages them **away from the nest,** and doses them nightly. By **day five or six** the prey is weak enough and blazing bright enough on the spore-sense that an **inexperienced young, sent out from the nest, can locate and kill it unaided** — a graduation exercise. That window is the [[Episode 2 — The Hush|Episode 2]] clock.
- **The young are never at the staging lair.** The whole point is the journey and the search. They wait at the nest until the adult judges the prey ripe.
- **The air goes toxic.** Spores linger and accumulate, so a lair used for days is lightly poisonous to humans: **start of each turn in the zone, CON save or 1d4 poison,** DC climbing with the dose-count — **8** (1–2 days) · **10** (3–4) · **12** (5–6) · **13** (7+). Zone = the inner chamber plus ~20–30 ft of approach; clean outside the mouth. A cloth mask helps (the blaze-orange bandana: advantage + resistance; improvised: +2).
- **Defensive spore** (both ages; rare for an adult — only when cornered/hurt): a faintly visible burst; CON save or poison **and a dose**. In [[Episode 1 — The Dare|Episode 1]] this is how the dying juvenile marked [[Tank]] — a biological *he-hurt-me, get-him.*

> [!danger] DM-only
> **Two forms — as played (Episode 1).** The adult keeps a **litter** of young in the **Pillared Reach** (Floor 1) — **the nest** — where [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] had loosed one on [[Wendell Pace]]. In [[Episode 1 — The Dare|Episode 1]] the party **killed that juvenile**: cornered, it loosed a defensive spore that **marked [[Tank]] with one dose**, and then went down under Brock's finishing blow. The **adult** ranges topside; on dare night it chased the kids on the grounds but **broke off at the hatch — it heard [[Ricky Vance]]** (alone, easier) and took him instead (the [[Episode 2 — The Hush]] cold-open; Ricky survives raw), staging him in a **woods lair** as the litter's hunting exercise. In [[Episode 2 — The Hush]] the adult is doing two things at once: **dosing Ricky nightly** toward the exam, and **hunting the marked voice — [[Tank]]'s —** because that's the thing that killed one of its young.
>
> **The two clocks.** (1) The **dosing clock:** ~6–7 days from taking to dispatch, after which the young are sent out to find and kill Ricky. (2) **The stalk:** the bereaved adult closing on Tank's single dose. Both point at the same place. *If the adult ever catches Tank alone at night it doses him again* — 2 doses and it closes fast, 3 and it knows his window. That's the Track-B ratchet.
>
> **Nest vs. staging lair — keep them distinct.** The **nest** (litter, dungeon, Pillared Reach) and the **staging lair** (Ricky, topside woods) are different places on purpose: the ritual requires the young to travel. This is why the Episode 2 climax is a genuine **1 adult vs. 4 PCs, no adds.**
>
> **Not Wesley's (to the players).** Per [[The Pattern Recognition Arc]], Wesley fingerprint level is *none* — the Hush was already loose. Mill-area chalk is background from his [[Wesley's Sigils|Tally Stone delve]].

## How it dies
On **dare night it cannot be meaningfully fought** — no eyes, no front, it shrugs off kids' weapons; Session 1 is a **chase**, not a battle (flee to the blast furnace, down the hatch). In **[[Episode 2 — The Hush]]**, once the PCs research it (via Russ / the film) and arm up, it becomes **killable by ordinary means** — its sound-tracking is the exploitable weakness. Because it *sees* with its ears, it is **blinded by the absence of sound (silence) OR by an overwhelming excess of it (noise)** — starve the signal or drown it out; either way it goes blind and can't fix a target. **Blinding is a window to act, not a kill in itself** — in *The Quiet Dark* the survivors overload it with fireworks and a blaring radio and lure it off a cliff (the *terrain* does the killing). At the table: misdirect and split its hearing, then blind it (the Stone's silence, or improvised loud noise) and finish it with focused fire or a hazard. The optional **stone of quietude** (a Silence-like artifact) makes the blinding push-button but is never required; it is deliberately **not** in the Pillared Reach — placed elsewhere in the dungeon (location TBD) as a tool they earn toward for the *adult* topside climax. **A lead already exists in play:** the "Marv's Spot / the Quiet Rock" photo+note the party pulled from the mill points at exactly this artifact — lock Marv's identity and the stone's current location for [[Episode 2 — The Hush]]. See [[The Keystone Tablet]] / [[Gear and Items]].

## Statblock — the adult (LOCKED, Episode 2 climax)
*Large monstrosity, unaligned* · **AC** 14 (natural armor) · **HP** 60 (8d10+16) · **Speed** 40 ft., climb 40 ft. · **CR 3**
**STR** 16 (+3) · **DEX** 15 (+2) · **CON** 14 (+2) · **INT** 4 (−3) · **WIS** 14 (+2) · **CHA** 6 (−2)
**Skills** Perception +6, Stealth +4 · **Senses** blindsight 60 ft. (hearing), blind beyond it; passive Perception 16 · **Languages** none (mimics voices)

**Traits**
- **Echolocation.** Perceives entirely by sound. While **deafened** (magical silence, overwhelming noise, thunder damage) it is effectively **blinded:** disadvantage on its attacks, advantage on attacks against it, must guess where targets are.
- **Never Flanked.** No advantage from being unseen or behind it; can't be surprised while it can hear.
- **Sound-Fixed.** Advantage on attacks against any creature that made real noise since the end of its last turn (shouting, verbal components, sprinting, gunfire, breaking glass). Whispers and careful movement don't count.
- **Overwhelmed.** A *prepared*, genuinely overwhelming noise in its space deafens it until the end of its next turn, **no save**; an improvised racket allows a DC 13 CON save.
- **Spore Sense.** Direction + rough distance of any spore-marked creature within a mile; at **3+ doses**, exact location.

**Actions** — **Multiattack:** one Bite + one Claw. **Bite** +5, reach 5 ft., 1d10+3 piercing. **Claw** +5, reach 10 ft., 1d6+3 slashing. **Snap-and-Charge** *(recharge 5–6):* move up to its speed in a line + one Bite; on a hit, DC 13 STR or prone.
**Bonus action** — **Thrown Voice** *(recharge 4–6):* mimic a heard voice from a point within 60 ft.; DC 12 WIS save or disadvantage on attacks against the Hush until the end of its next turn.
**Reaction** — **Fix on Sound:** when a creature it can hear shouts or casts a verbal spell within 30 ft., move up to half speed toward them.
**1/day** — **Defensive Spore** *(when it first drops to half HP):* 15-ft. radius, DC 12 CON, 2d4 poison (half on success) + **spore-marked** (one dose).

**Calibration.** CR 3 sits deliberately above the usual level-1 line, offset by **4-vs-1 action economy**, the **blinding swing** (~a full CR), and **[[Wade Vance|Wade]]** if the party earned him. Target shape: *someone hits 0 around round 3, gets stabilized, it dies in ~4 rounds.* Safety dials in order: Wade's presence → HP (45 comfortable / 75 frightening) → whether *Fix on Sound* bites → the Defensive Spore die. **This fight is the level-2 milestone** — topside, no dungeon reconstitution, death is death.

**The juvenile** (used once, Episode 1; the surviving litter reuses it): AC 12, HP 9 (2d6+2), Speed 30 ft., Bite +3 (1d6+1), blindsight 30 ft., Sound-Fixed. Clumsy and skittish — harries and flees. CR 1/8.

## Aftermath
Body in dawn light: the blank eyeless face, the light-eating hide gone slack. PCs may take a **tooth made of something that isn't bone** (dungeon-stone material) as a trophy/sample — a [[Cosmology Seeds|cosmology seed]] and the recurring-material clue in [[The Pattern Recognition Arc]] (called back in [[Episode 3 — The Click]]). *(In [[Episode 1 — The Dare|Episode 1]] the party did not loot the juvenile's corpse — the not-bone-tooth seed is still unclaimed and can surface off the adult in Episode 2.)* With the adult dead, the **surviving young are still at the nest,** unprovisioned and never dispatched — a dangling thread to reintroduce or leave to starve. The sheriff files the adult's body as "bear." No one follows up — the [[The Reality Filter|Filter]] at work.
