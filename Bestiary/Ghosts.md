# Ghosts

Up: [[Bestiary]]

**A hollowed person who dies while still hollowed. Their death doesn't take — the part that would have died is already filed in the machine — and what's left is a remainder: a person the world has finished with and the dungeon hasn't. Introduced in [[Episode 3 — The Crossing]] and a recurring threat from there on.**

> [!danger] DM-only
> The origin, the no-testimony rule, and the release procedure are all secret. The players discover them across Episode 3.

Base stat block: **MM Ghost (CR 4)** — reskinned, and run as a *hazard with a schedule* rather than a boss. See Running It below.

## Origin

Hollowing routes the deciding part into the dungeon and leaves a [[Nullprints|nullprint]] behind. If the emptied person later dies aboveground — and they usually do; see [[Hollowing and Agency-Loss]] — the death is incomplete. The dungeon stops eating, the print freezes **white**, and the remainder surfaces.

**Every white nullprint is a ghost.** This makes the monster type a direct index of the campaign's body count, and it means [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s past is going to keep walking up out of the ground for the rest of the campaign whether he intends it or not.

## The no-testimony rule (structural — do not break this)

**A ghost cannot answer a question.** No naming, no recounting, no pointing at who did this. The deciding part is exactly what's missing.

It only **broadcasts**: a small loop of unresolved feeling, repeating. That's the whole of its communication, and it's why an investigation can't be short-circuited by interviewing the monster.

This rule is also doing misdirection work in Episode 3 — see that note.

## Behaviour

- **Anchored topside**, to the site or object of the aboveground death. **It cannot enter the dungeon.** The thing that would end it is in the one place it can never go.
- **It is not hunting. It is finishing an errand.** A ghost runs the unresolved loop of its own life, and its violence is incidental to that.
- **The blind spot.** A ghost cannot perceive whatever killed it — that event is the one thing not in its file. This is usually the mechanism by which it kills others.
- **Proximity effects** are the low-grade broadcast: people near the anchor start re-litigating their own failures, plus some that aren't theirs.

## Possession is the kill mechanism

Not a grab-and-throttle. **It borrows a body to run the errand**, because its own doesn't work. The victim is driven, urgently and sincerely, toward whatever the ghost is trying to complete — and straight through the hazard the ghost can't see.

There is no malice anywhere in the mechanism. That is why it's worse.

**Useful 5e detail:** a successful Charisma save grants **24-hour immunity to that specific ghost.** So it can't reuse the people who throw it off, which is exactly why it burns through fresh victims and produces a *pattern* the party can investigate.

## Killing it (you can't) and releasing it (you can)

**Damage does not work.** Reduce it to 0 HP and it discorporates and reforms at its anchor in a few hours. HP is a stalling resource.

**Folklore half-works, and confidently.** Salt, iron, and running water disperse and repel — the widely-circulated 1996 small-town answers, and [[Russ]] will supply them in good faith. They buy time and nothing else.

**The permanent answer is [[Nullprints|release]]:** find the dead person's white nullprint in the dungeon, and burn on it an object proving that **a choice of theirs outlived them.** See [[Nullprints]] for the full procedure and the lethal misuse.

The gating consequence: **you cannot lay a ghost to rest without learning who they actually were.** That is the recurring-monster limiter and the reason every ghost episode is an investigation before it's a fight.

## No source — the Nightmare Engine doesn't cover this

This is the first monster the party meets that **[[The Nightmare Engine]] did not curate.** It has no film, no comic, no paperback. [[Russ]] will search his whole shop and come up with folklore instead of a source, and he will be visibly unsettled by that — *there's no tape for this.*

Ghosts are a **byproduct of the machine**, not an instantiation of a local fear. When the party works out that distinction they've learned something real about where monsters come from.

**And the thing that does have a plate is [[Certaintism|the Tome of Conviction]].** A non-cultural source scoops [[Russ]] in front of everyone. That is enormous fuel for [[Gern]]'s zeal reading and a live [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]] opening — see [[Certaintism]].

## Running it at low level

CR 4 against four level-2 kids is lethal, so **don't run it as a kill encounter** — there's no kill available anyway.

- Frame every ghost scene as **survive / deny / escape**, with a clock.
- Give each ghost a **window** in which it's actually dangerous, tied to its loop (Lowell's is the ninety seconds a train is on approach). Outside the window it's atmosphere and dread.
- Researching the window topside should convert directly into surviving it. Reward the [[Time Points|TP]] spend.
- Withering Touch and Horrifying Visage want reskinning toward **agency-loss flavour** rather than aging — a beat of not being able to choose, a vision of the shape you'd leave. *(Exact stat tuning still open.)*

## The standing population

Per the [[The 1991 Mill Incident|1991]] ruling: **the seven who died are all still active**, in and around [[The Mill]], and always have been. Nobody has ever separated one out because everything about the mill grounds is wrong and always has been. That's an Act-2 reveal sitting in the party's most-travelled location.

## Open dials

- **In-world naming.** Doctrinal Certaintist term for a remainder (unfiled stack / loose imprint) is unwritten. Town/kid slang is unwritten.
- **Stat tuning** for the level-2 encounter band.
- **Whether a ghost can be relocated** by moving its anchor object.
