# The Debrief Station

Up: [[Locations]]

**A one-room cabin in the woods between the mill and [[The Mercer Mansion|the Mansion]], sitting on the head of a shaft. [[Elias Mercer]] built it because the dungeon is a one-way information valve — the operation below could keep ledgers and maps and marks, but not one word of it could be carried out. The only export channel was a man's head, and heads leak on a clock. So he put a chair sixty seconds from the door and somebody in it with a pen.**

> [!note] Status — discovered in [[Session 8 — Second Shift|Session 8]]
> The party climbed out here after killing the Siren, and again (minus Old Man) fleeing [[The Pit Room]]. **They took one paper (III), saw the card, and Gern took [[The Reading Glass]].** Papers I, II, IV and the log are **still on the desk.** See *As played* below.

> [!note] What's in here
> **Four papers, a card, a log — and [[The Reading Glass]].** The papers are the campaign's first hard answer about [[The Reality Filter|the Filter]]; the glass is the only object in the room worth carrying out. *(As played, a curio cabinet of oddities was added — see below.)*
>
> A player-facing handout compiles the card, all four papers, and a log excerpt. Everything quoted below is readable by players.

> [!danger] Optional. Conditional on how they leave.
> The party reaches it **only if, after killing [[The Siren|the Siren]], they climb the shaft instead of walking out the way they came, and think to try the [[The Expedition Post|Room-11 skeleton key]] on the hatch at the top.** *(They did.)*
>
> **The cabin is what curiosity is worth.** It doesn't expire.

## Getting in

**The hatch at the top of [[The Spiral Stair Room]]** — roughly 70 feet up the incomplete iron stair, past the gaps. **The Room-11 key opens it.** No check, no puzzle. *(Confirmed in play. Tank carries the key.)*

> [!note] One-way, permanently
> **The top face is smooth and it auto-locks on close.** No handle, no keyhole, no seam worth finding. **This is an exit, never an entrance** — from the dungeon side it's the party's emergency exit out of the middle of Floor 1, a room away from the Pit Room.

They come up **in the middle of the cabin floor**, which is the first thing that tells them what this building is for.

## Where it is

**The woods between the mill grounds and Mercer land**, on the far arc of the dungeon ring — which is why the hatch comes out half a mile from where they went in. Half-overgrown, no path, invisible from any road.

> [!warning] Re-findable from outside? — open
> The original design said **not findable topside.** As played, the party **walked out the cabin's front door at dawn, picked up trails through the woods, and came out by the mill**, looking back at what reads from outside as an abandoned shack. **Whether they can walk back to it from the woods is held open by the DM.** It matters: papers I, II, IV and the log are still inside.

## What the room is for

Below, the operation functioned perfectly well — ledgers, route maps, chalk marks, tally counts, all usable **in situ**. **None of it could come up.**

> [!danger] The rule — written thoughts don't leave the dungeon
> A magical rule, and it wants no explanation at the table.
>
> **Marks stay** — tallies, route glyphs, chalk, a hammer-hole, *TANK WUZ HEAR*. **Accounts don't** — anything constituting a person's *report* of the place. Paper comes up empty.
>
> Let the boundary be slightly soft and let the party test it. See [[The Recall]]. *(Distinct from the [[The Cataloguing Office|Cataloguing Office]] persistence ruling: writing left **in** the office stays **in** the office. This rule is about carrying accounts **out**.)*

So the only way information got out was **inside somebody**, and Mercer's own experiments had shown him how fast that drains. Hence: a door in the woods, a chair facing it, and a man already holding a pen.

**Everything in this room is about the distance between the hatch and the chair.**

> [!note] Mercer himself does not forget
> He is the operator. The decay clock does not act on him — which is why **every document here is written by a man observing an effect he is exempt from**, and why the papers read as field notes on other people rather than as a sufferer's account.
>
> *(An earlier draft had him losing his own descent memory. Cut — it contradicts his role, and it made the point twice.)*

The cot, basin and tinned goods from the design weren't described and can stay or go.

## ⭐ Searching the room — [[The Reading Glass]]

Found in the **curio cabinet** by [[Gern]] (Investigation 18, Tank helping). Full rules in **[[The Reading Glass]]**. Short version: **ask it a question about the past of a thing, and it names a price in a fact you currently know.** Pay and you watch a full scene that answers. Decline and nothing happens, and the price never changes.

**It is the DM's lifeline for a stalled table**, and the object that eventually tells [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] the truth about his Window — now via Gern.

## The card

> **BEFORE THE HATCH OPENS**
> Pen in hand. Lamp lit. Paper out and dated.
>
> **1. DO NOT GREET HIM. DO NOT ASK IF HE IS WELL.** Begin the report before he sits.
>
> **2. WRITE AS HE SPEAKS. DO NOT WAIT FOR HIM TO FINISH A THOUGHT.** What he cannot finish, he will not recover.
>
> **3. TAKE DOWN EVERY DETAIL, HOWEVER TRIVIAL OR NONSENSICAL.** You are not to judge what matters. You will be wrong.
>
> **4. IF HE SAYS "IT'S GONE" OR "IT WAS SOMETHING ABOUT—", WRITE THOSE WORDS AS HE SAYS THEM.** A gap in the right place is worth more than a guess.
>
> **5. READ IT BACK BEFORE HE LEAVES.** He will correct you while he still can. He will not be able to tomorrow.
>
> **6. NEVER SEND A MAN UP WITHOUT SOMEONE IN THE CHAIR.** An unwitnessed shift is a lost shift.

> [!note] Rule 1 is the one that lands
> *Do not ask if he is well.* A man comes up out of that place and you are instructed not to ask how he is, because the seconds cost too much. **Characterises Mercer harder than any passage in his papers.** **Rule 4 is the one the party can use.**

**Found by Bash on the end table and read aloud; posted to the table chat.** Whether anyone physically kept it is open.

# Mercer's papers — four documents, chronological

Read in order they show a man working the problem out over three years. Hand them out in order.

> [!note] What the party has
> **Only III.** Tank searched the desk as Old Man's proxy, rolled **Investigation 1**, and picked the page with the most words (*"more words are more important, right?"*). **I, II, IV and the log remain on the desk.** The DM is turning III into a PDF handout.

## I — 9 April 1890 · Bragg

> *Bragg went down on my instruction with paper and pencil and orders to set down everything, however small. He was below four hours. He filled — by his own account, and he was emphatic about it — the better part of a dozen sheets.*
>
> *They came up empty. Not smeared. Not faded. **Empty.***
>
> *He could not be made to believe it. An hour later he could not be made to care.*

**Delivers:** nothing written below comes out. **This is why the cabin exists at all.**

## II — 3 March 1891 · Rutkowski, and the margin

> *Rutkowski, up at 11:40, seated 11:41, giving good account of a chamber of pillars. Called away by the whistle at 11:52 and returned at 12:20, at which time he could offer nothing but that there had been pillars — which he knew because I had said the word to him.*
>
> *He was not evasive and he was not distressed. He simply had nothing, and was untroubled at having nothing. Pressed, he said there was **a shape where it ought to be, and the shape was the only thing left.***
>
> *I put it to him as a dream and he agreed at once, and with some relief. A man wakes possessed of the whole of it; it is gone in the time it takes him to find his slippers; and by breakfast he has only the fact of having dreamt. That is the shape of the thing exactly, and I have not found a better account of it in two years of asking.*
>
> ***Half an hour. That is the whole of the margin.** I have written it on the door.*
>
> *And the remedy is the same remedy: **the journal at the bedside. Write it before the slippers.***

**Delivers:** the decay clock, the ~30-minute adult margin, the dream analogy, and the countermeasure.

## III — ON THE INATTENTION OF WITNESSES · compiled 1891–92 *(the party holds this one)*

> [!danger] The other half of the Filter — the one the party lives inside
> I and II explain the **forgetting**, which requires the dungeon. **This explains the rationalization**, which requires nothing at all — it happens to ordinary people who have never been near the mill, every time the party casts a spell in public.
>
> **Landed immediately in play:** an hour after reading it, the party watched the waking walkers at the mill gate each invent a different reason to be going home — *coffee for my husband; the kids will be up soon.*

> **The first.** Carried up the jaw of the thing Bragg killed on the second level, wrapped in sacking, across the yard at shift change. Some sixty men. No remark.
>
> **The second.** Unwrapped. Carried openly, in both hands. Four men looked. Hoskins said it was a hog's.
>
> *It is not a hog's. It has two rows.*
>
> **The third.** Set it on the weighing table in the office and left it a week. Eleven men came into that office in seven days. Not one asked. On the sixth day Prendergast lifted it to dust beneath, and set it back, and squared it to the edge of the table.
>
> **The fourth.** The lamp from the stair chamber, which gives light and takes no oil and has now done so for nine months. Hung it in the office in place of the office lamp. Foreman Whelan remarked that it wanted trimming.
>
> **The fifth.** The crate from the fourth level, which moves, and which is audible at ten feet. Left it in the yard two days. The men walked around it. Asked, Kowalczyk said it was the pump.
>
> *There is no pump in the yard.*
>
> ---
>
> I have taken to asking them afterward. It is more instructive than the trials themselves.
>
> Hoskins, on the jaw: a hog's. Asked where a hog of that size might be got, he supposed from a farm. Asked which farm, he said he had not known I kept hogs. Asked a third time about the jaw, he had lost the thread entirely and wished to know whether the gate was to be shut.
>
> Kowalczyk I asked what he had seen go past on the barrow. He said a tarpaulin. I asked what was beneath the tarpaulin. He said a tarpaulin — and was not in the least troubled at having said it.
>
> **They are not lying to me.** I have watched their faces and there is no lying in them. The answer arrives in them whole, and it satisfies them, and **it is never the same answer twice.** I have had the same object explained to me four ways in one afternoon, each with perfect conviction, by men standing within earshot of one another.
>
> It costs them, when the thing is very bad. After the fifth trial two men were sick and could not say why. Prendergast did not come in the following day, and when he returned he was as he had been, and remembered no illness. **So there is a price, and they pay it, and they do not know they have paid it.**
>
> The practical consequence I set down here because I have twice worked it out and twice mislaid it:
>
> **A witness is of no use to me.** I may do as I please before any number of men and be perfectly unobserved. I may not be corroborated. There is no man in this town who can be brought to say what he saw, because within the minute he no longer saw it.
>
> I am, in the only sense that signifies, alone here.
>
> **So is anyone else who can hold it.**

> [!warning] "Two rows" or "three rows" — open
> **Read aloud at the table as "three rows of teeth."** The text says two. Make the PDF match whichever the DM settles on.

> [!note] What that last line is doing
> Mercer knew there might be others and never met one. **Four of them are standing in his cabin reading it.** It also explains why the papers are here at all — he wasn't archiving, **he was leaving a note for whoever came next.**

## IV — 1893 · The boys

> *The boys are better at it. I have had four of the sweeper lads down to the second level — against my own better judgment, and I will answer for it — and every one of them held the thing a day or more, where a grown man has an hour. One of them still had the whole of it a week on.*
>
> *I cannot account for this, and I do not care for what it suggests about the proper use of them.*

> [!danger] The party are the boys
> They'll notice immediately, and they'll be holding [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s notebook while they do. *(They now are.)*

## The log

`DATE · NAME · DOWN · UP · REMARKS`

Mostly unremarkable. Then the pattern, in row after row across decades: *tried to tell his brother. did not take.* · *began twice. could not.* · *nothing by supper.* · *held four hours. best yet.* · *cannot say it. has tried four times now.*

And the rows with **no UP time**, with a note added later in a different pen:

> *did not come up. found at the gate, 4:20. **14d.***
> *did not come up. found at the gate. **31d.***
> *did not come up. **—***

> [!danger] Two things buried in the log
> **It teaches the exit mechanic.** Men who went down and didn't come up the stairs turned up *outside the gate instead* — exactly what happened to the party, twice.
>
> **That "14d / 31d" column is [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s durations column**, kept by adults a century before she was born. **She reinvented a form the mill had already abandoned**, at eleven, alone. Do not point at it.

**The log stops mid-page, decades before the mill does.** The remaining pages are blank. They gave up trying to understand it and kept working the floors anyway.

## What they walk out with

**Four findings, stated** — **only #3 is in hand after Session 8:**

1. **Nothing written below comes out.** *(paper I — still on the desk)*
2. **Memory of the dungeon fades like a dream** — roughly half an hour for an adult. *(paper II — still on the desk)*
3. **Ordinary people rationalize the impossible on sight, instantly and sincerely — and never the same way twice.** *(paper III — taken)*
4. **Children hold it longer than adults.** *(paper IV — still on the desk)*

**And one they derive themselves, which is the important one:**

> They have been taking notes, drawing maps, and arguing about the reset for weeks — **and none of it has faded.** Nobody has been able to do that since 1889.

> [!note] Set up the proof with their own pockets
> If any PC has written something down while below, **have them check.** **[[Old Man]] keeps a notebook with a page on each party member** and wrote in it below in Session 8 — the cleanest proof object at the table.

### Two tactical rules they now own

**They can cast in public and never be caught.** **And they can never be corroborated** — no adult ally will ever confirm anything they saw. **[[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]'s entire pitch runs on isolation, and the party just proved the isolation is structural.**

### The diagnostic

**It's never the same answer twice.** Two witnesses, two confident incompatible explanations. A test the party can actually run.

## Why this solves a DMing problem

Players naturally read the Filter as **adults lying or stonewalling.** [[Wendell Pace|Wendell]] grasping and giving up looks evasive; [[Tomek Sołtys|Tomek]]'s *"I probably had a fever"* looks like a cover story.

**The log and the trials end that reading permanently** — a hundred years of men *trying* and failing, and a hundred years of men sincerely calling a two-rowed jaw a hog's. Evidence of effort and of sincerity, not concealment.

## Afterwards

**A room Wesley hasn't found**, with a door he can't come up. Undisturbed dust proves it. **First place in the campaign that is genuinely theirs.** Party read on leaving: *"So Mercer was a reluctant caretaker of some sort of cursed thing… I guess we're going to hit the Historical Society sometime."*
