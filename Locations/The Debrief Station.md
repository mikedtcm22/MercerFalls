# The Debrief Station

Up: [[Locations]]

**A one-room cabin in the woods between the mill and [[The Mercer Mansion|the Mansion]], sitting on the head of a shaft. [[Elias Mercer]] built it because the dungeon is a one-way information valve — the operation below could keep ledgers and maps and marks, but not one word of it could be carried out. The only export channel was a man's head, and heads leak on a clock. So he put a chair sixty seconds from the door and somebody in it with a pen.**

> [!danger] Optional. Conditional on how they leave.
> This is not on the critical path. The party reaches it **only if, after killing [[The Siren|the Siren]], they climb the shaft instead of walking out the way they came, and think to try the [[The Expedition Post|Room-11 skeleton key]] on the hatch at the top.**
>
> If they walk out the front, they get [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]] and [[Tomek Sołtys|Tomek]] on the mill road and that is the episode. **The cabin is what curiosity is worth.** It doesn't expire — the hatch doesn't move, and they'd know the key fits something.

## Getting in

**The hatch at the top of [[The Spiral Stair Room]]** — roughly 70 feet up the incomplete iron stair, past the gaps.

**The Room-11 key opens it.** No check, no puzzle. It's the lock that key was cut for.

> [!note] One-way, permanently
> **The top face is smooth and it auto-locks on close.** No handle, no keyhole, no seam worth finding. **This is an exit, never an entrance**, and it never becomes an alternate way in. If they prop it, whatever they prop it with is still there when they come back and the hatch is still shut.

**They come up in the middle of the cabin floor**, which is the first thing that tells them what this building is for.

## Where it is

**The woods between the mill grounds and Mercer land**, on the far arc of the dungeon ring — which is why the hatch comes out half a mile from where they went in. Half-overgrown, no path, invisible from any road.

**Not findable topside.** There is no wandering-around-the-mill version of this discovery.

## What the room is for

Men worked the deep floors on shifts. Below, the operation functioned perfectly well — ledgers, route maps, chalk marks, tally counts, all of it usable **in situ**.

**None of it could come up.**

> [!danger] The rule — written thoughts don't leave the dungeon
> A magical rule, and it wants no further explanation at the table. **Marks stay** — tallies, route glyphs, chalk, a hammer-hole, TANK WUZ HEAR. The dungeon doesn't care what you scratch on it. **Accounts don't** — anything that constitutes a person's *report* of the place.
>
> Let the boundary be slightly soft, and let the party test it. It always fails and they can never quite prove why. See [[The Recall]].

So the only way information got out was **inside somebody**, and Mercer's own experiments had shown him how fast that drains. Hence: a door in the woods, a chair facing it, and a man already holding a pen.

**Everything in this room is about the distance between the hatch and the chair.**

## The room

Undisturbed for decades. Thick dust, undisturbed. **Nobody has been here — including [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]].**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The chair** | Plain, wooden, arms. Facing the hatch at conversational distance. **Wear on both arms, deep.** Hands gripping, over and over |
| **The interviewer's chair** | Lighter, side-on. Almost no wear |
| **The card** | Hand-lettered, tacked by the door, worn soft and greasy at the corners |

| **The log book** | Decades of hands |
| **Mercer's papers** | A bundle, tied. The reason to have come |
| **Cot, blankets, basin, tinned goods** | His. He slept here through long stretches |

## The card

> **BEFORE THE HATCH OPENS**
> Pen in hand. Lamp lit. Paper out and dated.
>
> **1. DO NOT GREET HIM. DO NOT ASK IF HE IS WELL.** Begin the report before he sits.
>
> **2. WRITE AS HE SPEAKS. DO NOT WAIT FOR HIM TO FINISH A THOUGHT.** What he cannot finish, he will not recover.
>
> **3. TAKE DOWN EVERY DETAIL, HOWEVER TRIVIAL OR NONSENSICAL.** You are not to judge what matters. You will be wrong.
>
> **4. IF HE SAYS "IT'S GONE" OR "IT WAS SOMETHING ABOUT—", WRITE THOSE WORDS AS HE SAYS THEM.** A gap in the right place is worth more than a guess.
>
> **5. READ IT BACK BEFORE HE LEAVES.** He will correct you while he still can. He will not be able to tomorrow.
>
> **6. NEVER SEND A MAN UP WITHOUT SOMEONE IN THE CHAIR.** An unwitnessed shift is a lost shift.

> [!note] Rule 1 is the one that lands
> *Do not ask if he is well.* A man comes up out of that place and you are instructed not to ask how he is, because the seconds cost too much. **That tells the party what Mercer valued without a word of editorial.**
>
> **Rule 4 is the one they can use.** It's a technique they'll adopt, and it explains why the surviving reports are full of fragments and blanks.

## Mercer's papers

### The dream passage — first-hand, and the reason he understood the problem

> *I have this morning read my own account of the fourteenth, in my own hand, and I do not recognise a line of it.*
>
> *It is not that I doubt it. I know the hand. I know I was below. But the reading of it is the reading of a stranger's letter, and where the page says I descended a stair of some ninety feet I have nothing — not a stair, not a darkness, not the cold. There is a shape in me where it ought to be and the shape is the only thing left.*
>
> *It is precisely the business of a dream. One wakes possessed of the whole of it, and it is gone in the time it takes to find one's slippers, and by breakfast one has only the fact of having dreamt.*
>
> *So: the journal at the bedside. Write it before the slippers.*

### The margin

> *Rutkowski, up at 11:40, seated 11:41, giving good account of a chamber of pillars. Called away by the whistle at 11:52 and returned at 12:20, at which time he could offer nothing but that there had been pillars, which he knew because I had said the word to him.*
>
> *Half an hour. That is the whole of the margin. I have written it on the door.*

### Bragg — why the cabin exists at all

> *Bragg went down on my instruction with paper and pencil and orders to set down everything, however small. He was below four hours. He filled — by his own account, and he was emphatic about it — the better part of a dozen sheets.*
>
> *They came up empty. Not smeared. Not faded. Empty.*
>
> *He could not be made to believe it. An hour later he could not be made to care.*

### The boys

> *The boys are better at it. I have had four of the sweeper lads down to the second level — against my own better judgment, and I will answer for it — and every one of them held the thing a day or more, where a grown man has an hour. One of them still had the whole of it a week on.*
>
> *I cannot account for this and I do not care for what it suggests about the proper use of them.*

> [!danger] This is the one that detonates
> **The party are the boys.** They will notice immediately, and they'll be holding [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s notebook while they do it.

## The log

`DATE · NAME · DOWN · UP · REMARKS`

Mostly unremarkable — times, names, a line or two. Then the pattern, in row after row across decades:

> *tried to tell his brother. did not take.*
> *began twice. could not.*
> *nothing by supper.*
> *held four hours. best yet.*

And the rows with **no UP time**, with a note added later in a different pen:

> *did not come up. found at the gate, 4:20. 14d.*
> *did not come up. found at the gate. 31d.*
> *did not come up. —*

> [!danger] Two things buried in the log
> **It teaches the exit mechanic.** Men who went down and didn't come up the stairs turned up *outside the gate instead*. Which is exactly what happened to the party, twice.
>
> **And that "14d / 31d" column is [[Ren Sołtys|Ren]]'s durations column**, kept by adults, a hundred years before she was born. **She reinvented a form the mill had already abandoned**, at eleven, alone, in a marble composition book. Do not point at it.

**The log stops decades before the mill does.** They gave up trying to understand it and kept working the floors anyway. Bleakest fact in the room.

## What they walk out with

**Three findings, stated:**

1. **Memory of the dungeon fades like a dream** — fast, on a clock, roughly half an hour for an adult.
2. **Nothing written below comes out.**
3. **Children hold it longer than adults.**

**And one they derive themselves, which is the important one:**

> They have been taking notes, drawing maps, and arguing about the reset for weeks — **and none of it has faded.** Nobody has been able to do that since 1889.

> [!note] Set up the proof with their own pockets
> If any PC has written something down while below — and [[Bash Whitlock|Bash]] almost certainly has — **have them check.** That's the ember confirmed by their own behaviour rather than by a document.
>
> If nobody has, the Bragg note covers it.

## Why this solves a DMing problem

The [[The Reality Filter|Filter]] is the hardest thing in this campaign to convey, because **players naturally read it as adults lying or stonewalling.** [[Wendell Pace|Wendell]] grasping and giving up looks evasive. [[Tomek Sołtys|Tomek]]'s *"I probably had a fever"* looks like a cover story. Ren's mother looks like denial.

**The log ends that reading permanently.** It is a hundred years of men *trying* and failing, written down by the men who watched them fail. The REMARKS column is evidence of effort, not concealment.

Every adult they've met gets reframed retroactively, and nobody was ever hiding anything.

## Afterwards — what the party now has

**A room Wesley hasn't found**, with a door he can't come up. Undisturbed dust proves it.

First place in the campaign that is genuinely **theirs** — somewhere to keep things, to meet, and not be seen. Worth letting them realise that on their own and use it.

## Open dials

- **Whether Mercer's own descent account is here in full.** He references "my account of the fourteenth." It would be the only first-hand written description of the deep floors that exists. Recommended: it's in the bundle but **water-damaged and mostly illegible** — a later payout rather than an immediate one.
- Whether the log names anyone the party would recognise as an ancestor.
- Whether the cabin has a road-side approach at all, or is purely hatch-accessible.
- Whether [[Ruth Bielak|Ruth]]'s [[Walt Bielak|Walt]] appears anywhere in the log. *(Almost certainly too late — the log stops decades before him. But a **later** log, if one ever existed, would be a devastating find.)*
