# Floor 2 — The Works

Up: [[The Mill Sector]]

**Floor 2 of the Mill sector: the gremlin floor. Where Floor 1 was a hunting range, this is a workshop — the first floor whose tenants *build*. A linear spine with one late fork, three Rube-Goldberg machines, and a hobgoblin foreman running a crew on mill discipline. Level band 3–4, with the guardian survivable at 5.**

> [!danger] DM-only
> Opens during [[Episode 4 — The Crossing]] (the white print moved here — see [[The Wash]]) and is the delve floor for [[Episode — The Mirror|Episode 5]].

## Theme and register

Lair theme: **"the works floor."** Territory theme is **[[The Gremlins|gremlins]]** — reskinned goblins/hobgoblins, sourced from *Gremlins* (1984) and *Gremlins 2* (1990), which are the most accessible references in the catchment and already in the players' heads. Full roster, rules, and wandering table live in [[The Gremlins]].

The escalation from Floor 1 is **intelligence, not power**. The pack floor had teeth and no language. This crew has tools, tactics, a hierarchy, tribute obligations, and a boss who talks — a visible step on the [[Floor Reset and the Intelligence Gradient|intelligence gradient]]. Register: **mean and clever, not heavy-supernatural.** The two genuinely uncanny things on the floor ([[The Long Gallery|the mirror]] and [[The Black Rotunda|the rotunda]]) are Mercer-era and predate the gremlins entirely — the crew is scared of both and has walled off the approaches.

## Floor-wide design features

**1. The ductwork rule (the floor's signature).** Every room carries visible pipe and duct runs with gremlin-sized openings — grates, elbow joints, missing covers, bucket chutes, vent mouths. **When a wandering roll hits, the default is emergence, not arrival.** Gremlins come out of the woodwork mid-room rather than wandering in through a door. Consequences to run consistently:

- Encounters open *inside* the party's formation. No approach round, no chance to set up, no chokepoint to fall back to.
- **The grates are the tell and the counter.** A party that spends a Stretch reading a room can map the openings, and a party that spikes, blocks, or watches them converts an ambush into a normal fight. Passive Perception should surface *how many* openings a room has on entry, without a check.
- It explains the reinforcement mechanic diegetically — the Shift Boss's whistle works because the whole floor is plumbed.
- Ambient audio: knocking, scrabbling, and running in the walls, constantly, whether or not anything is coming. Makes the tell unreliable in the right way.

**2. Every contraption has a break point.** A gremlin's rig is both its strength and its failure state. Every device on this floor — enemy gear, room machinery, the boss's machine — can be read and turned off by a party that looks. This is the floor's core competency and the three machines train it at three speeds: puzzle speed, combat speed, and boss speed.

**3. Three Rube-Goldberg machines, in escalating order.**
- **[[The Tinker's Bench]] (4)** — the blueprints. Free, static, readable at leisure.
- **[[The Door Machine]] (7)** — the major puzzle. Assemble it, with a discrepancy between the drawing and the room.
- **[[The Shift Floor]] (13)** — the guardian. The enemy runs the machine and the party interrupts it. Reverse Mousetrap.

**4. Combatscape variance.** Deliberate spread across the roster: cramped (3, 4), expansive (7, 13), vertical (5), water (2), reflective/two-layer (9), long-range exposure (11), enclosed crawl (10). No two combat-likely rooms should reward the same formation. Per [[Combatscape Layers]].

**5. [[Dungeon Mechanics|Stretch-Worth Principle]]** applies as on Floor 1. No pass-through rooms.

## Structure

Linear spine with one fork after the major puzzle. Both branches converge at the guardian.

```
Floor 1 room 10 (Down-Stairs)
  └─ 1. The Tollgate
       └─ 2. The Wash                    ← Ep4 white print
            └─ 3. The Warren Wall        ← the caged mogwai; key candidate
                 └─ 4. The Tinker's Bench (short tangent)
                      └─ 5. The Bucket Line  ⇅ hatch to Floor 1 room 8
                           └─ 6. The Shrine of Small Saints
                                └─ 7. The Door Machine   ← MAJOR PUZZLE
                                     │
                        ┌────────────┴────────────┐
                     BRANCH A                  BRANCH B
                  8. The Long Gallery       11. The Spans
                     ← Ep5 mirror twin          └─ 12. The Underbridge (tangent)
                  9. The Black Rotunda              ← the Nothic
                     ← Ep5 boss arena
                 10. The Tribute Chute ↑
                        └────────────┬────────────┘
                                     ↓
                            13. The Shift Floor      ← GUARDIAN
                                     └─ Down-Stairs to Floor 3
```

**Branch asymmetry is the point.** A is short and announces you (the chute is unavoidably loud). B is long and may let you arrive quietly, or — if the Nothic likes you — through the back of the foreman's cage.

## Entry points

- **Primary:** the Floor-1 **Down-Stairs (room 10)**, arriving at [[The Tollgate]].
- **Shortcut:** the floor hatch in the Floor-1 **[[The Spiral Stair Room]] (room 8)**, dropping into [[The Bucket Line]] at its **top landing, ~45 ft up the shaft**. Locked; opens to the **Floor-2 brass mill tag** (see Keys below). The Episode-1 footlocker key opens the *upper* hatch in room 8 (the sealed mill-grounds exit) — wrong-but-similar, as already written.

> [!note] Keys
> The Floor-2 hatch key is a **brass mill tag on a numbered fob**, matching the [[The Cataloguing Office]] style. **Three candidate locations: [[The Warren Wall]]'s hoard, the Nothic's salvage at [[The Underbridge]], and the ring on the Shift Boss's belt.** Whichever the party checks first is canonical; the others hold something else. Keeps the shortcut reachable on any route.

## The rooms

**1. [[The Tollgate]]** — *cramped, low, the stair mouth.* A scrap turnstile wired across the bottom of the Floor-1 stairs. Two alarm cords, one obvious and one under the tread. A tribute pile heaped beside it with recognizably topside goods, and Wesley's tally marks continuing. The first contraption they see, and it's junk — a deliberate baseline so the later machines read as escalation.

**2. [[The Wash]]** — *wide, shallow, flooded.* ⭐ **Ep4 objective.** Knee-deep standing water, a collapsed conveyor arch making a broken bridge, a rope ferry. **Lowell Brandt's white nullprint** on the far bank — on his side, one arm out toward the crossing. A sluice gate the crew can pull. The vial burn is a one-minute hold, and per [[The Gremlins]] water buds Wet Spawns, so the hold escalates on its own. Water is difficult terrain and noisy, inverting the Hush lesson. See [[Nullprints]], [[Lowell Brandt]].

**3. [[The Warren Wall]]** — *mid-size, honeycombed.* Fifty holes too small for a person. A **screw-jack press** widens one cell, at three costs: it must be *held* (it winds back in ~6 seconds, so the party splits and the cranker is alone), it is *loud* (automatic wandering roll from the harder end of the table), and it *crushes* — every crank past the third collapses another cell, so greed visibly costs loot in real time. **Behind it: the caged mogwai** (see [[The Gremlins]]). Key candidate.

**4. [[The Tinker's Bench]]** — *cramped workshop, short tangent.* Vise, springs, fishing line, a coffee can of teeth, half-built snares. **Two blueprints** pinned above the bench, butcher paper in grease pencil, drawn with a child's proportions: **"THE DOOR"** (room 7, shown *as designed*, which is not how the room now sits) and **"THE CAGE"** (room 13, with a carefully-rendered drawing of the thing in the cage). Lifting the good loot springs a deadfall. Portable gremlin traps recoverable here and pre-placeable in room 13. **Blueprints leave the dungeon with the party** and only respawn if moved but not removed — so they can be studied topside between delves.

**5. [[The Bucket Line]]** — *tall vertical shaft.* Continuous chain-and-bucket loop at walking pace. Three levels: **top landing (~45 ft)** with the hatch to Floor 1 room 8; **mid landing (~25 ft)**, a plank platform where cargo comes off and therefore where gremlins are; **floor level** with the exit and the brake lever. Riding is a check on and a check off; miss the dismount and you ride past or over the top. The **brake** gives a safe motionless climb and is heard by every gremlin on the floor. Best elevation room on the floor.

**6. [[The Shrine of Small Saints]]** — *narrow, crowded, alcoved.* A wall of stolen faces: dolls, mannequin hands, school photos, a polished hubcap, and one or two Mercer-era objects that shouldn't be down here. **The take-and-give rule is readable in three layers**: every object is paired with something given (marble on a photograph, bottle cap in a doll's hand), a two-column tally board on the alcove frame, and — ten feet back down the corridor — a years-old delver's body still holding a china figurine, with a dozen small puncture wounds. Trading deliberately, taking deliberately, and blowing past without looking are all legitimate outcomes. **Softens or hardens the wandering table accordingly.**

**7. [[The Door Machine]]** — *wide and tall.* ⭐ **The floor's major puzzle.** A machine that doesn't run and an exit slab with no handle, hinge, or lock. Five stations; two work, three were cannibalized for parts to build the boss's machine. Station 1 needs a component hidden in the room; station 2 needs something carried or improvised; **station 3 needs a part currently installed in station 4**, which is where it stops being a scavenger hunt. **The twist:** the room has changed since the blueprint was drawn (fallen ceiling section or shifted block), so building it exactly as drawn *fails* — they must notice the discrepancy and adapt one step. Failure costs a Stretch to reset, makes a racket (wandering roll), and fires one payload into the room. Target 45–60 minutes at the table. A wandering roll landing mid-assembly is the best version of this room.

### Branch A

**8. [[The Long Gallery]]** — *narrow, tall, wrong.* ⭐ **Ep5: the Mercer Mirror's twin.** A fragment of [[The Mercer Mansion]] interior rendered in dungeon stone — wallpaper in bas-relief, furniture-shaped rock. The twin mirror at the far end. Gremlins have barricaded the approach with scrap and won't pass it.

> [!danger] What the mirror shows — before and after
> **Before the Ep5 bridge exists:** the same room as the **Mercer Mansion drawing room** — furnished, papered, gas-lit, warm. The party appears in the reflection standing *in that room*, in their own clothes, movements matching. At the far end of the reflected room, past where the real room ends, **a figure stands with its back turned.** Each time someone looks away and back, it has closed distance. It never moves while watched.
>
> This leaks nothing about [[Panels]] (so Wesley learns nothing about where the twin is), and it is the first hard material link to [[Elias Mercer]] — it should send the party at the [[Mercer Falls Historical Society]] and the mansion annex.
>
> **After the Ep5 bridge opens:** the reflection **changes** — the drawing room is gone and they are looking into Russ's back room. The switch is the reveal; a window that was always there would not be.

**9. [[The Black Rotunda]]** — *circular, two-layer.* ⭐ **Ep5 boss arena.** Floor of polished translucent black stone. Beneath it, a second chamber under **reverse gravity** — its occupants stand on the underside of the glass, and the stone's optics present them right-side-up, so the victims read as *reflections* until someone notices the reflections are doing things they aren't. **Flip panels:** wedge sections pivot on a central axis; step on one and you are deposited on the underside, where down becomes up. Attacks and spells do not cross the glass. Sight does. Two boards, one footprint; the boss can walk beneath the party in plain view and surface behind them.

> [!warning] Required rulings so the fight can't stall
> As written, anyone under the glass is untouchable and the boss can kite forever. **Use both:** (1) **flipping costs a full action and resolves at the start of your next turn**, so there is a telegraphed, committed, visible round; (2) **the boss cannot attack from beneath** — going under is repositioning only, and it must surface to deal damage. Optional escalation if the fight runs long: **flipped panels lock for a round or two**, shrinking the available exits until someone can be cornered on either side.
>
> Freeing a victim means going under and flipping them up — someone chooses to be on the wrong side of the glass mid-fight.

**10. [[The Tribute Chute]]** — *steep, enclosed, upward.* The gremlins send tribute **down** this chute to the thing in the Rotunda, so the Rotunda sits low and [[The Shift Floor]] sits high, and the party climbs it against the grain. Slick with decades of what tribute leaves behind. Athletics to climb; failure slides you to the bottom to start over, taking a little on the way. Enormously loud in a metal tube — **the Shift Boss learns how many are coming and roughly when.**

### Branch B

**11. [[The Spans]]** — *long trestle over a black drop.* The [[The Buzzsaw Room|Buzzsaw]] room's logic blown out wide. **2–3 rounds to cross**, with gremlin archers on wing platforms reachable only by a catwalk that doubles back on itself. Three options, all legible from the entrance: push across and eat the fire; advance behind something (bucket, plate, shoved crate); or go after the wings — which, because the catwalk is convoluted, **takes about as long as just crossing**. Gremlin trick: **pull-pins in two trestle sections** — they would rather drop the span than lose it. **Tuning flag:** three rounds of sustained ranged fire at level 3 gets ugly fast; start at **three archers** and hold two in reserve on the wandering table rather than committing five.

**12. [[The Underbridge]]** — *low span beneath the trestle; tangent.* ⭐ **The Nothic.** One of [[Elias Mercer]]'s 1880s expedition men who went too deep and did not stop looking. The kids will read it as the troll under the bridge, which hands them the toll framing for free. **Built to be roleplayed, not fought.** Its currency is **secrets** — Weird Insight is the stat block telling you how to run it; it wants something true you have never said out loud.
- **Payouts, scaling with how the scene goes:** what the Shift Boss's machine does and what's in the cage · that the Rotunda floor opens, and how · fragments about the mirror, from a man alive when it was made · scavenged goods off delvers who did not get past it, including a spare hatch key · **the back way into [[The Shift Floor]]** — a crawl that surfaces above and behind the foreman's cage, letting the party open the guardian fight with the machine already in reach.
- **Statting up from CR 2 to 3–4:** HP to ~75–85, Rotting Gaze DC to 14, and a reaction that triggers Weird Insight when someone lies to it. Nothics are cowardly by default — the power-up should read as *cornered and knows too much*, not brawler. If fought, it flees into a crack and returns later with a grudge.

### The guardian

**13. [[The Shift Floor]]** — *galleried hall, elevation-heavy.* Machine hulks for cover, a raised foreman's cage on two stairs, the whistle pipe, and along one wall **the second Rube-Goldberg machine** with something caged at the end of it.

**The Shift Boss** — the Brain Gremlin from *Gremlins 2*, wearing a dead foreman's clothes. Hobgoblin Captain chassis, tuned to the level they arrive at. He talks during the fight and can be negotiated with, badly. **He pulls the steam whistle to call reinforcements, rolled off the floor's own wandering table** — and the pool was built by the party's own floor choices (see below).

**The machine advances one station per round**, each tended by a gremlin whose job is to reset it. [[The Tinker's Bench|Blueprint B]] is what lets the party read the whole chain at a glance instead of discovering it a station at a time.

| # | Station | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Burner under a small boiler; pressure climbs | Douse it, or bleed the valve |
| 2 | Pressure pops a counterweight off a shelf into a hanging bucket | Tip the bucket, take the weight |
| 3 | Bucket descends, pulling a lever that drops a bowling ball onto a track | Cut the rope, block the track, take the ball |
| 4 | Ball rolls a spiral, drops, knocks a wedge from under a spring-loaded bar with a boot on it | Re-wedge it, jam the spiral, stand in the ball's way and eat the hit |
| 5 | Boot swings, kicks a crank arm, crank lifts the cage pin | Hold the arm, spike the crank, pull the retaining bolt |

Breaking a station costs the crew **1–2 rounds to reset**, so the party is buying time rather than winning outright. **Two threats, one action economy** — the Boss works the whistle while the machine counts down, and the party has about one person to spare for either. A group that read the blueprints and pre-placed [[The Tinker's Bench|Bench]] traps at stations 3 and 4 gets to feel very clever.

**In the cage: the Spider Gremlin** (*Gremlins 2*). They should hear about it earlier on the floor — from the Nothic, from the tribute chute, from the blueprint drawing — so the countdown has weight from round one.

Beyond: the **Down-Stairs to Floor 3**.

## The reinforcement ledger (floor-wide prep layer)

The Shift Boss's whistle pool is not authored, it is **accumulated**, and the party builds it without knowing:

| Party action | Effect on the pool |
|---|---|
| Every **Whistler** that survives a fight | +1 roll |
| Every **Foreman** given a free round | +1 roll |
| Burning, flooding, or bricking **[[The Warren Wall|the warren]]** | −2 rolls |
| Looting **[[The Shrine of Small Saints|the shrine]]** without giving | +1 roll, and harder entries |
| Trading properly at the shrine | −1 roll, and softer entries |
| Loud crossings (the crank, the brake, **[[The Tribute Chute|the chute]]**) | +1 roll each, cumulative |

This turns the whole floor into boss prep without a single extra rule, and it is the payoff for the floor-memory principle already running on Floor 1.

## Plot hooks

- **[[Episode 4 — The Crossing]]** — [[The Wash]] (room 2) holds Lowell's white print. Ep4's delve therefore **opens Floor 2 during the episode**, with [[The Pit Room]] demoted from destination to gate. The party's first taste of the gremlin floor is the Ep4 climax.
- **[[Episode — The Mirror|Episode 5 — The Mirror]]** — ⭐ **now locked as Ep5.** [[The Long Gallery]] (8) holds the twin; [[The Black Rotunda]] (9) is where the echo takes its victims and where the fight happens. The Panels-side bridge changes what the twin shows.
- **[[Elias Mercer]]** — rooms 8, 9, and 12 are all Mercer-era and all predate the gremlins. Floor 2 is where Mercer stops being a name in papers and becomes a previous *user* of this place.
- **[[Wesley Crane]]** — his tallies continue through [[The Tollgate]]. How far down he has been is a dial.
