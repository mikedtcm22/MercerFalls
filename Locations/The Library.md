# The Library

Up: [[Mercer Falls — Setting]]

**The Mercer Falls public library — the town's memory, kept by [[Timothy]]. A core, daytime investigation node: where the disappearance pattern surfaces, where the Hush can be reverse-engineered without [[Russ]], where the Mercers' strangest custom is on file, and where a stern preacher is suspiciously haunting the same records the kids are.**

## Description
A small Carnegie-era public library in town — stacks, a **local-history shelf**, a folklore/legends section, and a back-room **microfilm reader** with the run of the *Mercer Falls Ledger* going back decades. Open daytime hours only (☀ — closed at night). Walk-in; no unlock required. Debuts as a core node in [[Episode 2 — The Hush]].

## Timothy, the librarian
Runs the desk. Competent, a little lonely, and **visibly pleased** when anyone asks him for something — nobody requests local history in July. The load-bearing distinction:

> **Timothy can pull *subjects* on request. He cannot notice a thing no one in this town has ever noticed.**

So delegated research yields **folders, not findings.** He'll assemble the Mercer material for free; he will never hand over the disappearance pattern, because seeing it requires looking at two unrelated clippings and connecting them, which is exactly what the town cannot do. That split rewards a party that thinks to ask without letting it replace investigating — and it makes the Library **[[Old Man]]'s node**, the way [[Jerry's Pawn Shop]] is his through the guitars.

## In play (Episode 2)
- **The disappearance pattern — key connective tissue.** In the old papers, a small *"[name] missing"* note buried in one issue, then ~a week later a *"[name] found dead — suspected animal attack"* headline — and **none of the articles ever connect the two.** Paired with the Hush's behavior, *this* is what makes the PCs think **the same thing has [[Ricky Vance|Ricky]]** — missing now, on that same ~week clock. **1 pt DC 12 · 2 pt DC 10 · 3 pt DC 8** *(relaxed from 15/13/10 after a failed first attempt in Session 3).* Historically corroborates the dosing window (C2).
- **⭐ The Mercer folder — free, or 1 pt, no roll.** Eighty years of Mercer funeral coverage from the *Ledger*'s society pages. The recurring detail: at every Mercer interment, after the committal, the family **burns an article belonging to the deceased on the grave** and works the ashes into the sod. One clipping quotes a widow saying the article must be one *"he would have answered to."* A later clipping notes it was done *"as at the funerals of his father and before him his grandfather."*
  - **Garnish — the graves face the mill.** Every Mercer headstone in the family plot is turned away from the church and toward [[The Mill|the mill]] on the bluff. A local-history pamphlet calls it *"a peculiarity of the family plot, variously explained."*
  - **Why the Library has this and the [[Mercer Falls Historical Society|Historical Society]] doesn't:** the Society holds the family's *curated self-image* — the charter, the portraits, the keeper-duty. The Library holds the *public record:* society pages, which covered funerals the way small-town papers do, as social events with color detail. The family would never have archived this. A newspaper absolutely would.
- **The kill, the hard way.** The folklore / local-legends shelf (or a run of old "wild animal" sighting reports) is the backup path to the [[The Hush|Hush]]'s weakness (a sound-hunter — **blind it with silence *or* an overwhelming wall of noise, then use the moment;** the source film ends by overloading it and luring it off a cliff) and the dosing ritual — so a party that skips [[Panels]] can still assemble the solution.
- **The silence relic (F1).** With the Marv's-Spot photo, the local-geography holdings surface the legend of a dungeon relic that *drowns all sound around it.* Points toward the [[The Stone of Quietude|Stone of Quietude]] delve.
- **Gateway to the Mansion.** Good work here surfaces the Mercer papers and **unlocks [[The Mercer Mansion|the Mansion]]**.
- **Maybe the graphic novel.** If [[Russ]] has sent them for ***The Quiet Dark: Below*** and the Library is the under-used node, it's here — checked out to Russ once, back on the shelf.

> [!info] ◆ Episode 3 seed — hand the Mercer folder over freely; it is inert now
> The Mercers keep a **degraded folk memory of the ghost-resolution rite:** the right object, the right burning, and the **wrong location.** Four generations performing a correct ritual in the wrong place, because they kept the custom and lost the *where.* *"One he would have answered to"* is the counter-claim concept sitting in plain sight, meaningless until it isn't.
>
> With no cosmology in hand this reads as gothic rich-family eccentricity — a little morbid, a little charming, exactly the sort of thing a Bard files away. It says nothing about Ricky, the Hush, or the dungeon. It rhymes with the Mansion charter (*"keep the work lit against the dark under the hill"*): the Mercers understood their obligation in outline and lost the specifics, generation by generation. Same shape as [[Certaintism]] — a doctrine that is half-right and confidently misapplied.
>
> **The productive wrong lead:** if the players remember this before they understand nullprints, they'll try burning something on a **grave** — and it fails. Failing is what teaches them the location matters, which beats being told the rule.
>
> **Held in reserve** (a second garnish, if this node gets another pass): a Mercer death notice that ran, was retracted the following week with no explanation, and ran again eleven days later. Reads as a printing-office blunder now; reads as *the filing was wrong once* later.

> [!danger] DM-only — Hollis in the microfilm
> **[[Reverend Hollis]]** is here, hunched at the microfilm, actually digging **dirt on [[Wesley Crane|Wesley]]** for their rivalry — something he'd never explain to a pack of kids. His cover is honest ("a man doing some reading"), he **clams up when they get close,** and he keeps **turning up wherever they turn up.** His interests overlap theirs *precisely* because Wesley is the hidden hinge between the mill, the Mansion, and the disappearances — so the players draw the damning line themselves. First time a **second party is shown independently circling Wesley,** and the quiet tragedy: the one adult building a real case against the villain gets filed under "suspect." **Play him shifty and petty, not sinister-competent.** His church unlocks as a follow-up.
