# The Stutter Stairs

Up: [[The Mill Sector]]

**Floor 1, Room 15 — the fake-out "stairs down." A stairway descends to a landing and rises again — but an invisible mirror at the bottom loops the party back where they started, without their ever turning around. Crack the loop and it opens the way to the Compass-Keystone enclave (16).**

> [!danger] DM-only
> Spoiler room. NOT the way to Floor 2 (that's the Down-Stairs, room 10) — a looping decoy that hides the Compass-Keystone route. Reached from the [[The Switchbacks]] (4) secret passage; wraps around the central bite on the Buzzsaw/Wesley-tangent side.

## The trick — a V that's really a loop
The stair appears to travel in a **V**: down to a bottom landing, then up again. In fact, an **invisible magic mirror** stands across the bottom landing; crossing it silently reverses the party, so the "ascending" flight is the descending flight *retraced* — they climb back to exactly where they started, never having turned around. It reads, at first, like the way down to Floor 2. It isn't.

## Making the loop land at the table (don't narrate it)
The moment you *say* "the stairs go back up," it's dead. Instead:
- **Seed a recurring landmark** and let the players catch it — a distinctive broken step, a guttered torch, a dead rat, or (best) **their own chalk mark / dropped torch** reappearing ahead of them. The reveal you want is a player saying *"wait — haven't we passed this?"* — and that realization is what prompts someone to finally look for magic.
- **Let it cost.** Each loop quietly burns **Stretches and torches and draws wandering rolls** while they make zero vertical progress — the attrition is the dread, not the geometry.
- **Optional visceral beat** (hold for the moment they commit to "one more flight down"): don't say the stairs turned — say a foot comes down expecting a *drop* and hits a *rise*, a lurch of vertigo, though no one ever turned around.
- **Never truly trapped:** retreating back up the way they came always works. Only the tempting "keep descending" loops.

## Cracking it — perceive, then break
- **Perceive the mirror.** An **Arcana check** or **Detect Magic** reveals the invisible mirror — a shimmering vertical plane across the bottom landing. (The recurring-landmark realization is usually what prompts someone to look.)
- **Break it — the hollow steps.** The **last few steps down to the landing feel hollow** underfoot (a Perception / careful-search tell). They **lift like a hatch**, revealing a **switch that deactivates the mirror**. There's a hollow-step hatch-and-switch **on each side of the mirror** (redundant — kill it from whichever side you're on). In practice the party reaches the **near-side** switch first; flip it, and the mirror dies.
- **The reward.** With the mirror off, the party can finally cross the landing for real — and on the **far side** sits the **door to the Compass-Keystone enclave (16)** (the [[The Keystone Tablet|Compass-Keystone]] route). That door, and the redundant far switch, are only reachable once the loop is broken (or by someone coming *from* 16).

## Stretch-worth
The whole room is a Stretch-sink by design — but a *fair* one: diagnosing the loop (forensic landmark-reading), perceiving the mirror (Arcana/Detect Magic), finding and lifting the hollow steps, and reaching the hidden **room-16 door** are all Stretch Activities, and the payoff is the Compass-Keystone enclave. See [[Dungeon Mechanics]], [[Forensic Delving]].
